# Chu Three-Households Anti-Qin Memory / 楚虽三户式反秦记忆

Chu three-households anti-Qin memory / 楚虽三户式反秦记忆 is the source's reading of the line "楚虽三户，亡秦必楚" in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-1-wangjian-lv-liushiwan-dajun-duizhan-xiangyan-haoxi-kaichang-le-lsf2puinwg0m3bgyi25znof7aznu]]. The host treats the line as a condensed memory of [[ChuState|楚国]]'s continuing anti-Qin force after [[XiangYan|项燕]]'s defeat, not as a literal demographic statement.

In the episode's explanation, "三户" points to the major Chu lineages Qu, Jing, and Zhao. That makes the sentence a claim about aristocratic and cultural persistence: even when the state is militarily broken, a Chu identity network can outlive the regime and later reappear in the anti-Qin struggle associated with [[XiangYu|项羽]] and the Chu-Han transition.

The concept is a memory frame, not a proof by itself. It should be linked to [[Shiji|《史记》]], [[SimaQian|司马迁]], and later Chu-Han outcomes with explicit source labels, because the episode is moving between battlefield narrative, transmitted sayings, and historical afterlife.

## Key Claims
- A defeated state's political memory can remain usable after its formal army and ruler are broken.
- "Three households" is interpreted by the source as an elite-lineage formula around Qu, Jing, and Zhao, not as a literal count of common households.
- The line turns Xiang Yan's death from a military endpoint into a prophecy-like memory of later Qin collapse.
- The memory is powerful because later anti-Qin figures can be read as fulfilling it, but that fulfillment reading remains historiographical.
