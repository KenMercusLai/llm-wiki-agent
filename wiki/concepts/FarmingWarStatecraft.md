# Farming-War Statecraft / 耕战逻辑

## Definition
Farming-war statecraft is a reading of Qin's reform order in which agricultural and textile output sustain the state while military merit converts battlefield service into rank, land, and honor. It is an institutional incentive frame, not a claim that every Qin campaign had the same cause.

## Current Synthesis
Shang Yang's reforms privileged production and codified military rewards; the later campaign against Wei makes the possible personal payoff concrete. A rival adviser's assessment of Qin's cultivation, transport, and rewards shows that this capacity also mattered to an opponent deciding whether to risk war. Subsequent warnings and counterfactuals extend the frame to the difficulty of containing Qin and to campaigns after unification, but those extensions are arguments by speakers in one historical podcast series, not demonstrations that reward rules made expansion inevitable.

## Key Claims
- Shang Yang's reform program couples rewards for agricultural and textile production with rank and material rewards for battlefield achievement, while imposing severe social controls.
- The prospect of military reward helps explain the narrator's reading of Shang Yang's personal campaign against Wei; the victory and Shangyu fief show how a battle outcome could become personal status.
- Zhao Bao's warning about accepting Shangdang treats Qin's cattle-assisted farming, water transport, and military rewards as a combined strategic capacity, not just a large army.
- Kong Bin's warning after Changping casts Qin's incentive system as a reason Wei should not assume Qin would stop after attacking Zhao; it remains a contemporary warning as retold by the episode.
- The claim that killing Ying Zheng would not undo Qin's institutional advantage, and the claim that frontier wars absorbed post-unification reward expectations, are interpretive extensions rather than necessary consequences of the reform rules.

## Evidence
- **Institutional design:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] describes production incentives, collective liability, and the military-merit rank system's access to land, office, and honor. The same reform also created resistance; this episode stops before the later fallout.
- **Reward realized—and its cost:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] reads Shang Yang's own command and his deceptive peace proposal to Gongzi Ang against the expectation of a profitable campaign. Its direct continuation, [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]], recounts Wei's defeat and Shang Yang's Shangyu fief, but also the lost credibility that later made refuge in Wei harder. These installments are one continuous case, not independent confirmation.
- **Opposing calculation:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-2-lrlulw0b6v0fb2xpr2i5ifem1h4n]] presents Zhao Bao's fuller argument from the *Shiji* tradition about Qin's farming, transport, and rewards as reasons accepting Shangdang could transfer Han's danger to Zhao.
- **Expected continuation:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]] reports Kong Bin's warning to Wei that Qin's appetite would not end with Zhao. This political forecast is paired in the episode with coalition difficulties and other motives for war, not a measured law of permanent expansion.
- **Limits and later extension:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] argues counterfactually that Jing Ke's success would not automatically reverse Qin's systemic advantage; the installment stops before the actual strike. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] interprets southern and northern campaigns after unification as a possible outlet for military-reward expectations, alongside conscription of lower-status groups and administrative incorporation of territory.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All seven notes come from *Ruiqi Jiangtou Zizhi Tongjian*; the Shang Yang pair is a serial narrative, and literary or historical voices quoted within episodes are not seven separately checked primary sources. Deception produced short-run gains but imposed a trust cost. War also reflected geography, ruler decisions, diplomacy, revenge, and opponent weakness; institutions do not prove perpetual campaigning. Jing Ke's hypothetical success cannot be observed, and the alleged post-unification inertia and explanation of conscript status are the narrator's interpretations, not settled historical laws. Severe sanctions and backlash belong in the assessment of the reform's costs.

## What Changed
- Distinguished reform design, realized individual rewards, rival risk assessment, and speculative long-run persistence.
- Treated the Shang Yang installments as one case and the entire declared source set as a single podcast-series cluster.
- Retained strategic and ethical costs rather than equating Qin capacity with inevitable victory or unending war.
