# 揣摩 / Chuaimo Technique

## Definition
揣摩 / Chuaimo is a source-described persuasion technique combining assessment of political circumstances with close reading of an audience's incentives, fears, self-image and reception conditions.

## Current Synthesis
From [[SuQin|苏秦]] and [[Guiguzi|Guiguzi]] textual tradition to [[Chunshenjun|黄歇]], [[FanJu|范雎]] and [[SuiHe|随何]], the episodes distinguish alliance-building, court manipulation, defensive interest reframing, protected remonstrance and forced defection. The same attention to the audience can serve prudent negotiation or destructive institutional capture; successful speech is not a general law of psychology.

## Key Claims
- Study of a political situation must be paired with audience and access calibration; knowledge alone does not secure a hearing.
- Coalition arguments change with rulers' material constraints and dignity, while a persuader may also shape another envoy's career incentives.
- Reading a ruler's self-image can convert moral ideals into dangerous transfers of institutional power.
- Defensive persuasion can redescribe restraint as the stronger side's own advantage and sequence risky criticism around protection and eavesdroppers.
- Defection persuasion may pass from private assent to an irreversible public act, with costs to the persuaded actor.

## Evidence
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-30-suqin-de-kaigua-rensheng-lkmn-fruukdxgkddx7zb4lc3o9bw]] links Su Qin's deep study of [[YinfuJing|《阴符》]] to dual reading of context and motive; after [[FailureDrivenSelfStudy|self-study following failure]] and further failed approaches to [[ZhouXianwang|Zhou]], [[YingSi|Qin Huiwen Wang]] and [[ZhaoState|Zhao]], [[YanWengong|燕文公]] funds him when he presents Zhao as [[YanState|Yan]]'s shield against [[QinState|Qin]]. The note distinguishes 《史记》/《战国策》 title variants and does not equate self-study with immediate success.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-31-hezong-lianheng-weiou-qin-lncpgmxrojc0y1meg9a3ul-o9nih]] describes [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] as anti-Qin coordination with hostages, mutual rescue and defector penalties. Su Qin reads [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s pride, humiliates and secretly funds him into Qin to buy alliance time; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-32-shishang-shouwei-lianheguo-mishuzhang-suqin-lgfkk45yq1c0yblequ-d1t7mol3]] shows his material-threat and [[DignityBasedPersuasion|dignity]] framing tailored to [[HanXuanhuiwang|Han’s ruler]] and his finite land, Wei’s army, [[QiState|Qi]]’s [[Linzi|临淄]] [[WarringStatesUrbanProsperity|urban prosperity]] and [[ChuWeiwang|Chu’s ruler]]’s alliance role. The 333 BCE triumph remains source-scoped because of [[ChronicleChronologyDrift|chronology doubt]].
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-47-yanwangkuai-de-lixiangzhuyi-lhgl6rqkv9nonzxc9xqquzzlz5pr]] has [[SuDai|苏代]] and [[LuMaoshou|鹿毛寿]] exploit [[YanWangKuai|燕王哙]]'s wish to demonstrate trust and ancient-style virtue: [[ZiZhi|子之]] gains appointment power and control of people and finance through [[RuleBreakingAbdication|rule-breaking abdication]]. This is manipulation, not a commendation of the outcome.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-84-fangzhi-qinguo-miechu-jing-yong-zhe-zhao-llgh12eistejijsl6yildzkixocm]] records Huang Xie's letter to [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]]: by praising Qin's strength, warning that Han/Wei routes and an exposed rear make attacking Chu risky, then proposing a Qin–Chu alliance, he reframes Chu's survival as Qin's strategic interest. The podcast attributes the halted [[BaiQi|白起]] campaign to the letter but does not independently expose Qin's internal decision process.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-88-ni-zenme-kandai-fenghuangnan-2-lpzyziww177yvvjksmuioa-p-1qi]] says Fan Ju first shocks the ruler about [[XuanTaihou|宣太后]] and [[WeiRan|魏冉]], then delays until an eavesdropper leaves and political protection is promised before arguing against distant attack and for [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|远交近攻]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-158-koucai-hao-de-tianhuaban-yiren-ding-baiwan-dajun-lnup-vmw-ff-nqmuh6-x8si0k8zu]] reads [[YingBu|Ying Bu]]’s three-day refusal in [[JiujiangChuHan|Jiujiang]] to see Sui He as fear of [[XiangYu|项羽]] and desire for a Han option; Sui He observes Ying Bu’s reaction before securing private assent, then publicly declares it and urges the killing of a Chu envoy, binding defection to [[LiuBang|刘邦]]. Ying Bu is later defeated and flees, so persuasion was not an effortless battlefield victory.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- All seven notes come from the same narrated history series; speech and motivation are transmitted interpretations. The Su Qin chronology and the Yan ruler's private intentions are uncertain, and the killing of an envoy breaches a norm rather than validating the tactic ethically.

## What Changed
- The initial Su Qin method now includes a harmful Yan-court case, defensive Qin–Chu letter, protected court criticism and a Chu–Han defection with mixed aftermath.
