## Definition
鸿都门学 was an Emperor Ling-sponsored late-Han literary and artistic training and appointment channel. In this podcast series its importance lies not simply in arts teaching but in competition with established recruitment through [[Taixue]] and elite recommendation.

## Current Synthesis
The six sequential episodes trace precursors, establishment, competing qualifications, and remonstrance around 177–178 CE. They are one host's reading of a historical narrative, not six independent attestations. The ruler's alleged anti-士人 purpose is the host's interpretation; artistic competence and the risks of favoritism are not mutually exclusive.

## Key Claims
- Literary favor around the Hongdu gate preceded the school's later formal treatment; patronage could become access to office.
- Cai Yong's 177 CE memorial warned against promoting minor literary and visual arts above ritual and administrative competence before the Hongdu establishment discussed in subsequent episodes.
- The new court-backed curriculum and accelerated office route redistributed scarce appointments away from Taixue and established 士大夫 channels while admitting genuine artistic talent.
- The host interprets Ling's investment in Hongdu as a political counterweight to elite recommendation and anti-eunuch 士人 reputation, not simply an educational innovation.
- Yang Ci, Cai Yong and Yang Qiu criticized flattering or underqualified beneficiaries through different memorials; the ignored objections show a feedback and appointment problem without proving all Hongdu students lacked skill.
- Xidi office sale is a subsequent, separate appointment channel that deepened the broader governance concern; it was not Hongdu's curriculum or an identical admissions process.

## Evidence
- Early access: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-907-suoyou-de-bing-doushi-ni-de-xinbing-ltqzyohcauduwuanlvqph9l81d79]] reports [[EmperorLingOfHan]] favoring fu, edict-writing, bird-seal and calligraphy talent near the Hongdu gate; Yue Song and Jia Hu are described as using access to recommend flatterers. This predates the later school's institutional account.
- Earlier warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-908-nijing-chu-qiangzhe-xialu-chu-yongzhe-gudu-chu-zhizhe-lqih1dradhzi0mipxsaiedd52dlj]] recounts [[CaiYong]]'s 177 CE memorial distinguishing useful “small ways” of writing/painting from criteria for serious office and classical-ritual responsibility. Ling partially restored rites and changed the unrelated false filial-son appointments; this did not resolve later Hongdu recruitment.
- Channel design: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-910-shehui-shengcun-wo-quan-ni-duodian-chengfu-lp2fvuyb8ampzoeyr7znztnr0fgk]] reads the 178 CE school as Ling's challenge to Taixue and great-clan selection; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-911-gudai-gongwuyuan-ye-neng-bao-fenpei-ls3pxu9xlpuulsdailx8tcoylv9e]] describes fu, fiction-like writing, letters, calligraphy and painting, recommendation by high officials and local jurisdictions, and quicker office access through eunuch-favored channels. [[OfficialSelectionChannelCompetition]] is thus a quota/patronage dispute as well as a curriculum dispute. [[ShiYiguanLateHan]] and [[LiangHuLateHan]] are the episode's counterexamples to a blanket incompetence verdict.
- Omen remonstrance and leakage: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-912-mimi-daodi-gai-bugai-shuo-chukou-lhsgipxwc17pahc-r6jl7xdyrs2a]] reports [[YangCiLateHan]] using palace omens to criticize favored flatterers over capable officials; Cai Yong also connects ornamental writing and private requests to rapid promotion. [[CaoJieLateHan]]'s leak of Cai's confidential memorial illustrates [[CourtFeedbackCollapse]] through exposure to retaliation; it is not evidence that the omens caused political disorder.
- Prestige and dissent: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso]] says Ling had Hongdu figures painted and praised. The host links this to resentment after [[ChenFanLateHan]] and [[DouWuLateHan]]'s anti-eunuch crisis. [[YangQiuLateHan]] accused some favorites of shallow learning, powerful connections, delegated writing and promotion by favor; Ling ignored his memorial. The same episode subsequently describes Xidi rank/revenue-based office prices and the imperial private store, a distinct [[OfficeSaleLegitimacyDamage]] mechanism.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The anti-elite motive belongs to the host's interpretation, not a documented statement of Ling's interior purpose. Yang Qiu's charges are a remonstrant's accusations, not an assessment of every artist; Shi Yiguan and Liang Hu complicate that reading. Cai Yong's 177 criticism is a pre-establishment warning; Hanji 914's Xidi sale of offices is adjacent in chronology but not identical to study at Hongdu. Six notes from one series should not be counted as independent historical corroboration.

## What Changed
- Combined repeated episode-arrival paragraphs into precursor, appointment competition and remonstrance claims.
- Distinguished genuine artistic ability from objections to promotion and separated Hongdu from Xidi office sale.
- Kept political-intent and omen readings attributed to speakers rather than elevating them to historical certainty.
