# Interest-Based Coalition Sabotage / 利益瓦解合纵

Interest-based coalition sabotage / 利益瓦解合纵 enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]], where [[FanJu|范雎]] responds to a renewed [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] gathering in [[ZhaoState|赵国]]. The episode says he reads the coalition activists less as ideologues than as people looking for wealth and rank.

His countermeasure is therefore financial rather than military. Qin agents bring heavy money to Zhao's Wuan city, hold banquets, distribute funds, and let the would-be coalition organizers turn on one another before the anti-Qin project matures.

The concept differs from [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|联盟自保失灵]]. Self-preservation failure describes allied states refusing to spend their own strength once a coalition reaches implementation. Interest-based sabotage acts earlier: it attacks the coalition labor market and trust network before the coalition has become an army.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-2-qinshihuang-de-cp-weihe-bei-quzhu-chuguo-lj0222vw8ijbvj25iwfzh-qhvzr9]] adds a later and more coercive Qin version after [[LiSi|李斯]] is restored. The source says [[QinState|秦国]] sends persuasive agents with gold and jewels into the other states, buys off elites where possible, kills those who cannot be bought, and alienates rulers from ministers before sending generals. This is broader than breaking one hezong meeting, but it shares the same logic: use money and court incentives to make rival states damage their own coordination before Qin's armies arrive.

## Key Claims
- A coalition can be disrupted by changing the incentives of organizers before armies move.
- Bribery works best when the persuaders or brokers already have mixed motives around wealth, office, and recognition.
- Sabotage can succeed without resolving the target states' strategic fear of Qin; it only needs to prevent coordinated action at the right moment.
- The tactic fits [[ZonghengjiaDiplomacy|纵横家外交]] because persuasion, money, reputation, and court access are treated as instruments of interstate power.
- Qinji 110-2 adds the coercive escalation: bribery can be paired with assassination and ruler-minister alienation as preparation for military conquest.
