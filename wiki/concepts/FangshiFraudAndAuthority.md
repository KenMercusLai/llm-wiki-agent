# Fangshi Fraud And Authority / 方士骗术与权威

Fangshi fraud and authority / 方士骗术与权威 is the source's pattern for how ritual specialists, fortune-tellers, spirit mediums, and immortality technicians can convert uncertainty into status and extraction. [[87-guonian-zui-shihe-liao-xuanxue-tantao-shenxian-fangshu-fazhanshi-wei-wan-daixu-704051997]] opens with a warning drawn partly through [[JiangHuCongTan|《江湖丛谈》]] and then scales the same problem up to Qin-Han courts.

The concept does not claim every believer or practitioner is a fraud. Its boundary is ethical and procedural: when a specialist uses unverifiable claims, props, secrecy, fear, grief, erotic access, money, or official favor to trap others, fangshu becomes predatory authority.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-2-ribenren-daodi-shi-bushi-xufu-de-houdai-lhgbbhg3zw8rlx1jjb7lpb5ihihh]] adds the Xu Fu case in concrete court terms. [[XuFu|徐福]] fails to bring back immortal medicine, then explains the failure through a sea deity, visible-but-unavailable [[Penglai|蓬莱]], insufficient Qin ritual courtesy, and the need for more costly offerings. The episode treats this as a way to convert unverifiable access into renewed resources: children, craftsmen, seeds, grain, and authorization for another voyage.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] adds a nearby but distinct [[LuSheng|卢生]] case. Lu Sheng also fails to bring back immortals, but the source emphasizes the returned 图书 and "亡秦者胡" rather than a new supply demand. The authority pattern therefore shifts from extraction to interpretation: the specialist's access to the unseen gives the court a warning that can be read into policy.

## Key Claims
- Scam authority often begins by meeting a real emotional need.
- Props, distance, darkness, special vocabulary, staged verification, and failure reinterpretation can make ordinary technique feel supernatural.
- Court patronage raises the stakes by turning private deception into office, wealth, marriage, punishment, or policy.
- Anti-fraud skepticism should protect believers and victims rather than merely mock them.
- Qinji 122-2 adds failure reinterpretation at imperial scale: the failed specialist can blame inaccessible sacred conditions and turn disappointment into a larger budget.
- Qinji 123-1 adds that a failed specialist can also convert disappointment into a warning text, making authority consequential even without a direct resource request.
