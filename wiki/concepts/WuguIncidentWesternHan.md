## Definition

The Wugu Incident / 巫蛊之祸 is the late [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] succession trauma in which [[LiuJuCrownPrinceWesternHan|卫太子刘据]] is destroyed and later presumed dead. In the current wiki it matters in two bounded ways: Hanji 391-1 uses it to explain the source-scoped hidden charge against [[LadyGouyiWesternHan|钩弋夫人]], while Hanji 396-2 uses its aftermath to explain why a later false Liu Ju return claim becomes politically explosive.

## Current Synthesis

The current wiki evidence remains narrow but now has a pre- and post- branch. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] says Han Wudi later investigates Liu Ju's death and, in the host's interpretation, discovers that Lady Gouyi helped [[JiangChongWesternHan|江充]] and [[SuWenWesternHan|苏文]] frame the crown prince. The source treats this as the hidden reason for Lady Gouyi's death but also says Han Wudi does not publicize it because her son [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|刘弗陵]] is the intended successor.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] does not retell the full Wugu Incident. It uses the incident as inherited political memory: Liu Ju is presumed dead afterward, but that presumption remains important enough that a man claiming to be him can unsettle officials, attract a crowd to [[WeiyangPalace|未央宫]], and force [[JunBuyiWesternHan|隽不疑]] to prioritize dynastic order over identity uncertainty.

## Key Claims

- The Wugu Incident functions in the current source as a succession-memory background rather than a fully narrated event.
- Hanji 391-1 source-scopedly treats Lady Gouyi as a hidden participant behind Jiang Chong and Su Wen in Liu Ju's destruction.
- Han Wudi's alleged concealment of Lady Gouyi's charge shows the incident continuing to shape the legitimacy of Liu Fuling's succession.
- Liu Ju's presumed death after the incident is necessary for understanding why the later "return" claim creates shock.
- The incident leaves enough unresolved emotional and political charge that resemblance can briefly paralyze officials.
- Jun Buyi's response depends on treating Liu Ju's post-incident status as politically disabled even if physical survival were possible.

## Evidence

- Hidden-guilt branch: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] says the host regards Lady Gouyi, not only Jiang Chong or Su Wen, as the true behind-the-scenes actor in Liu Ju's frame-up.
- Succession-legitimacy concealment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-391-1-wei-le-li-taizi-hanwudi-weihe-xian-sha-chongfei-gouyi-furen-lhsva8zj7n7mfy6u4vyfhrwmbwzg|Hanji 391-1]] says Han Wudi does not publicize this alleged guilt because Liu Fuling is already the chosen successor.
- Succession-memory background: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] says Liu Ju was already believed dead after the Wugu affair when a man appears claiming to be him.
- Crisis effect: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] says officials hesitate because the claimant resembles the former crown prince and because the claim draws palace-gate attention.
- Legal-political interpretation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-396-2-hanwudi-zhizi-liuju-wugu-zhi-an-hou-fuhuo-le-lhj6dzjsikkmbbcjlfgnz1bbok-1|Hanji 396-2]] says Jun Buyi argues that even a true surviving Liu Ju would remain a state criminal after offending the former emperor and fleeing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This page should not be treated as a complete account of the Wugu Incident. Hanji 391-1's Lady Gouyi claim is source-scoped and not upgraded here into settled historiography. Hanji 396-2 does not reconstruct causes, accusations, participants, deaths, legal procedure, or historiographical debate; it only uses the aftermath to explain the false-Liu-Ju crisis under Han Zhao.
