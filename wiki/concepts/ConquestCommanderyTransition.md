## Definition
Conquest commandery transition is the conversion of defeated or surrendered territory into appointed commandery-county administration, rather than leaving its former kingdom or creating a new hereditary holder.

## Current Synthesis
The episodes distinguish three stages. [[QinState|Qin]] first named the conquered [[ChuState|Chu]] zone provisionally and later subdivided it; post-Chu Jiangnan/Kuaiji and post-unification Lingnan were different southern extensions. After the six-state conquest, the Qin court rejected [[QinWangWan|Wang Wan]]'s proposed princely fiefs, paired appointed commanderies with standardized measures, disarmament and elite relocation, and incurred coercive costs. During Chu-Han war, [[LiuBang|Liu Bang]] also made commanderies from some surrenders and captures, but still installed a king in Han. The label therefore describes a recurring administrative option, not an inevitable or uniform result of conquest.

## Key Claims
- The temporary Chu commandery and later Jiujiang, Zhang and Kuaiji divisions show that a victory can precede a staged reclassification of territory.
- Qin's Jiangnan-to-Kuaiji extension after Chu differs in time and geography from the 214 BCE Lingnan campaign's Guilin, Nanhai and Xiang commanderies and convict settlement.
- The empire-wide Qin choice was contested: Wang Wan proposed imperial sons in distant former states; Li Si and Qin Shi Huang chose appointed commanderies over renewed territorial fiefs.
- Direct rule required social and material implementation, including displacement of local elites, disarmament and common measures, with resentment and coercion rather than automatic pacification.
- Liu Bang's Henan, Henei, former Yong and West Wei conversions show Han use of the tool in wartime alongside continued royal appointments elsewhere.

## Evidence
- Chu and southeastern staging: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]] records the provisional Chu commandery after [[WangJianQin|Wang Jian]] and [[MengWu|Meng Wu]] defeated the last Chu resistance, including the source's account of Changping Jun's final enthronement and defeat, and its later Jiujiang/Zhang/[[KuaijiCommandery|Kuaiji]] subdivision; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]] places Jiangnan/Kuaiji after Chu while explicitly reserving deeper Lingnan for later. Neither campaign narrative nor a new map proves that conquered-state elites stopped gathering in unconquered Qi, the [[RemnantResistanceBase|remnant-base risk]] raised by Qinji 118-2.
- Post-unification frontier: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] dates the [[Baiyue|Baiyue]] campaign to 214 BCE, naming [[GuilinCommandery|Guilin]], [[NanhaiCommandery|Nanhai]] and [[XiangCommandery|Xiang]] and the claimed relocation of fifty myriads of convicts to guard and settle. Its account of drafting fugitives, live-in sons-in-law and merchants shows [[MarginalStatusConscription|low-status conscription]] was part of this conversion, not evidence that settlement was consensual.
- Institutional choice: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-3-jiemi-qinshihuang-weihe-faming-junxianzhi-li4bzz54trstteapodnonxzdzkuv]] recounts Wang Wan's distant-fief proposal, [[LiSi|Li Si]]'s objection, [[QinShiHuang|Qin Shi Huang]]'s acceptance and the reported thirty-six commanderies with governor, commandant and inspector; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] records disarmament, annually checked common measures and relocation of 120,000 households to Xianyang alongside resentment. This attempted [[ImperialHomogenization|uniform transmission of central commands]], not a demonstrated uniform tax or transport outcome in every new commandery.
- Chu-Han variation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-2-lpohbb3prsgvokpjipfd7i1ntiwn]] records [[ShenYang|Shen Yang]]'s surrendered Henan becoming [[HenanCommanderyChuHan|Henan commandery]] and [[SimaAng|Sima Ang]]'s captured territory becoming Henei, yet also Han Wang Xin's kingship; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-1-lishi-jizai-shenme-qingkuang-hui-fasheng-ren-chi-ren-canju-lrwz-prnxd8hzchagyrbywkux7lh]] dates the former Yong pocket's incorporation after [[Feiqiu|Feiqiu]] and [[ZhangHanQin|Zhang Han]]'s death into [[ZhongdiCommandery|Zhongdi]], [[BeidiCommandery|Beidi]] and [[LongxiCommandery|Longxi]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-2-zhuan-gei-ni-piqi-da-de-pengyou-weiwang-panhan-jing-shi-liubang-ai-maren-ls5nvunmhakvefd2oxn4zdyoob4l]] follows [[HanXin|Han Xin]]'s [[RiverCrossingDeception|deceptive river crossing]], [[WeiBao|Wei Bao]]'s capture and [[HedongCommandery|Hedong]], [[Shangdang|Shangdang]] and [[TaiyuanWarringStates|Taiyuan]] divisions; the battle tactic enabled the conquest but is not itself a commandery policy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These are episodes of one interpretive history series, not eight independent archival confirmations. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-118-2-chi-gua-le-wangshi-de-gua-bao-tian-lns7jt-kbv3qunm66hzltdgugbco]]'s Chu genealogy and final sequence have separate unresolved disputes and do not decide the administrative point. Subdivision is not proof that resistance disappeared; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] stresses coercive implementation. The first Jiangnan Kuaiji reference and the later Lingnan three-commandery campaign must not be collapsed. Han Wang Xin's appointment prevents a claim that Liu Bang converted every conquered region to direct rule. The thirty-six-commandery and migration figures remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Separated battlefield takeover, territorial classification and coercive social implementation.
- Added Qin–Han contrast and distinct pre-/post-unification southern stages.
