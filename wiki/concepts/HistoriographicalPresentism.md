# Historiographical Presentism / 史评当代性

Historiographical presentism / 史评当代性 is the source-reading pattern named from [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-109-2-chunshenjun-bei-huangdi-dajiuge-quanzu-miemen-locma2plu7v-vsssm5dr5frc3szw]]: later evaluations of old figures often answer the evaluator's own political moment. The episode uses the familiar claim that all history is contemporary history to explain why the same [[WarringStatesFourLords|战国四公子]] can receive praise, regret, or condemnation.

The source's main comparison runs across five layers. [[SimaQian|司马迁]] can regret [[Chunshenjun|黄歇 / 春申君]]'s late fall while still remembering his earlier merit. [[JiaYi|贾谊]] can praise the Four Lords in [[GuoQinLun|《过秦论》]] because early Western Han anti-Qin argument benefits from admiring eastern patrons who respected worthy people. [[YangXiong|扬雄]] can reject the same power pattern because late Western Han court politics makes overmighty ministers, eunuchs, in-laws, and usurpation feel dangerous. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] can sharpen that rejection from a Song centralization and ruler-instruction perspective. [[ZhuXi|朱熹]]'s [[ZizhiTongjianGangmu|《资治通鉴纲目》]] tradition can then make value judgment visible in terse wording such as "盗杀黄歇."

This concept is not a license to ignore evidence. It is a caution to keep evaluative frame, historical problem, and source language visible before treating any inherited judgment as neutral.

## Key Claims
- Historical evaluation often changes because the evaluator is solving a contemporary political problem.
- Praise and blame may both preserve real evidence while emphasizing different dangers.
- A text's wording, naming, omission, and quotation choice can carry political judgment.
- Source criticism should separate the event being described from the later purpose served by describing it that way.
