# 分布式地方韧性 / Distributed Local Resilience

## Definition

分布式地方韧性 / distributed local resilience is the capacity of a region to preserve order when its administrative center is threatened by spreading defense, food security, trusted local coordination, and mobilization capacity across multiple counties or communities.

## Current Synthesis

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] builds the concept from [[DuJiLateHan|杜畿]]'s defense and later governance of [[HedongCommandery|河东郡]]. Du Ji can abandon the commandery seat without abandoning the region because county-level defenses and an already cultivated support network continue to function. Rebels cannot take his camp and cannot obtain grain from surrounding counties, so capturing or threatening the center does not produce usable territorial control.

The episode's sixteen-year governance account broadens resilience beyond emergency defense. Agriculture, sericulture, dispute handling, schooling, fortification, arms production, and training create mutually reinforcing livelihood and security capacity. The resulting system is resilient because ordinary prosperity supplies allegiance and material depth, while distributed preparedness prevents one local seizure from collapsing the whole region.

## Key Claims

- A regional center can be relinquished tactically when authority and defensive capacity survive in multiple local nodes.
- Food denial matters alongside fortification: rebels who cannot obtain supplies cannot convert movement into stable rule.
- Trust accumulated before crisis can become rapid mobilization and willingness to defend local order.
- Agricultural prosperity and military preparedness can reinforce each other when treated as one governance system.
- Distributed resilience reduces dependence on a single city, commander, or relief column, but does not eliminate the need for outside reinforcement.

## Evidence

- Distributed defense: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] says Du Ji gives up the commandery seat while county defenses and an already organized support network remain active.
- Logistics denial: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] says the rebel coalition fails to take Du Ji's camp and then fails to obtain grain from surrounding counties.
- Livelihood-defense integration: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] joins farming, sericulture, schooling, fortification, weapons, and troop training in the long-term Hedong settlement.
- External limit: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1072-duji-danqi-shou-hedong-jing-shi-baixing-yuan-wei-ta-fusi-ltiuwt7v-rjcjpjpovqf9ffl88hu|Hanji 1072]] also requires Cao-side coordination and Ma Teng-associated reinforcement to finish the rebellion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

This is currently a single-source concept derived partly from the host's “distributed warfare” analogy. The episode does not establish a formal county-autonomy doctrine, identify every defensive node, or prove that popular support alone defeated the rebels. Outside military assistance remains decisive, so the concept describes local staying power and logistics denial rather than self-sufficient victory.

## What Changed

- Created the concept from Du Ji's center-relinquishment, county-defense, supply-denial, and long-term governance sequence.
