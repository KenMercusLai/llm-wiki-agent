## Definition
Experiential retail makes an in-person visit, participation, or spatial encounter part of the customer's value, beyond simply obtaining inventory. Restaurants, fitness rooms, parks, and destination districts are adjacent applications with different operating models, not interchangeable kinds of store.

## Current Synthesis
Across the bounded cases, value can come from co-creation, embodied brand presentation, live service, an immersive story world, or a temporary event. A compact ice-cream tasting and a theme park demand different dwell times, yet both make the visit more than a transaction. Experience is not a substitute for supply chains, safety, service, site quality, local fit, and financially sustainable operations. Destinations and photogenic streets extend the idea beyond retail while also exposing its limit: traffic and sharing alone do not establish repeat use.

## Key Claims
- Participation can create attachment to the product, whether it is an extended making ritual or a brief tasting and freshness cue.
- A physical environment can communicate a brand and build direct customer relationships that a catalog or wholesale rack cannot fully carry.
- Hospitality and group fitness sell live, perishable interactions; atmosphere matters only while food, instructors, comfort, and service hold up.
- Immersive IP places and time-limited activations can turn stories or events into physical brand contact, but their economics differ from ordinary stores.
- Repeatable experience requires operating systems, appropriate locations, and market-specific proof, not just novelty or attractive design.
- Destination attention and social photos may generate discovery while undermining the lived, return-worthy experience if the place serves only check-ins.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The 15 sources span retail, foodservice, fitness, parks, campaigns, and tourism: sharing the word “experience” does not make their margins, capital needs, or repeat-use behavior equivalent. The FengTouQuan episodes form a recurring editorial perspective, not independent verification of each trend or brand-performance claim. Advice Line suggestions about opening or financing a store are proposals, not proven results. A scene-led food market is a successor format in the cited episode, not evidence that redesign alone revives a declining food court. Site failures and service lapses can outweigh an otherwise appealing atmosphere.

## What Changed
- Compressed a long arrival-ordered catalog into six operating mechanisms with grouped evidence.
- Distinguished core retail examples from hospitality, parks, event, and whole-destination analogies.
- Brought repeat-use, operational discipline, and proposal-versus-outcome limits into the synthesis.
