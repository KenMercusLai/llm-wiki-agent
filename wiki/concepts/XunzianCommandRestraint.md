# Xunzian Command Restraint / 五权三制

Xunzian command restraint / 五权三制 is the closing military-ethics frame in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-3-xunzi-bingfa-li-cangzhe-renxing-de-mimi-lgfcp4hkruyhhrjguqhjbhllvwp43]]. After presenting [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s argument for [[XunzianMilitaryLegitimacy|renyi-based military legitimacy]], the episode turns to what a commander must weigh and when a commander may refuse the ruler's order.

The "五权" are treated as judgment disciplines. A general should not preserve office at the cost of sound advice, chase victory while forgetting defeat, build internal prestige while neglecting external danger, pursue benefit while ignoring harm, or deliberate carelessly while being stingy with resources. Command is therefore not only battlefield aggression; it includes risk accounting, independent judgment, and generous support where the situation requires it.

The "三制" define refusal boundaries. A general may choose death rather than station the army in a dangerous and untenable place, fight a battle that cannot be won, or use the army to bully the people. The source makes Xunzi's military thought a limit on ruler command as well as a theory of cohesion.

## Key Claims
- Command legitimacy depends on careful weighing, not only courage or obedience.
- Generals must resist incentives that make them protect rank, chase glory, or mistake visible advantage for real advantage.
- Refusing a ruler can be required when terrain, odds, or people-protection make an order destructive.
- The doctrine links military professionalism to [[PeopleBasedPoliticalSecurity|people-based political security]]: abusing the people undermines the same legitimacy that makes armies effective.
