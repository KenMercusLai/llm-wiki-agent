# Post-Success Arrogance / 得势后的骄傲

Post-success arrogance / 得势后的骄傲 is the leadership failure pattern [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-964-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-bie-jiaoao-lo2-edzffu0v9ixoabc06rx-oqoq|Hanji 964]] draws from [[WangYunLateHan|王允]], [[LiuDaiLateHan|刘岱]], and [[LyuBu|吕布]]. Success, office, clan status, or merit can make a person less able to hear advice, preserve proportion, or protect relationships.

In Wang Yun's case, the host reads the killing of [[CaiYong|蔡邕]] as post-victory insecurity: having become the public anti-[[DongZhuo|董卓]] hero, Wang Yun fears the scholar's control over historical record and overprotects his reputation. In Liu Dai's case, Han-clan status and face make him reject [[BaoXin|鲍信]]'s materially sound defensive plan. In Lü Bu's case, real merit in killing Dong Zhuo is weakened by repeated public boasting.

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-965-bangguo-bieren-de-mang-weishenme-yao-shao-ti-ltjd-cv59hnm-n8ar9jxdogp9q0q|Hanji 965]] extends the same pattern by showing how Wang Yun's post-success posture damages crisis design: he fails to create a credible safety path for [[LiangzhouWarlordClique|凉州系军阀]] remnants, helping make [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s attack-Chang'an advice persuasive.

## Key Claims

- Success is a character test because it changes how people receive correction and treat others.
- Merit can become politically corrosive when it is converted into entitlement, public shaming, or uncontrolled reputation management.
- Status confidence can hide material weakness, as Liu Dai's case shows against the Qingzhou Yellow Turbans.
- The concept overlaps with [[RationalHumility]] and [[SituationalCharacterTests|五观识人法]], but focuses specifically on behavior after victory, office, or visible credit.
