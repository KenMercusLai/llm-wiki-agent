# Rebel Mandate Manufacture / 起义天命制造

Rebel mandate manufacture / 起义天命制造 is the bottom-up legitimacy technique [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-2-qilai-bei-yapo-de-renmen-lpx9-iicwog4nj-nudaj-i4ajb25|Qinji 128-2]] attaches to [[ChenSheng|陈胜]] and [[WuGuang|吴广]]. After the [[DazexiangUprising|大泽乡起义]] becomes necessary for survival, the leaders still need to make rebellion feel larger than a desperate mutiny.

The source gives two layers. First, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang claim that [[FuSu|扶苏]] and [[XiangYan|项燕]] are not dead, borrowing the moral appeal of an unjustly killed Qin prince and the anti-Qin memory of a Chu general. Second, they create sign-like evidence through fish-belly writing and bonfire fox cries, turning "Chen Sheng shall be king" into a message that appears to come from beyond ordinary politics.

This concept sits near [[ChenweiPolitics|谶纬政治]] but is not identical to court prophecy. Qinji 128-2 is about rebels fabricating and distributing signs to mobilize followers, not a ruler interpreting omens to decide state policy.

## Key Claims
- Survival pressure can start a revolt, but legitimacy stories help it scale beyond the initial endangered group.
- Borrowed names matter when they connect the revolt to existing moral grievance and regional memory.
- Fabricated sacred signs can work politically even if they are manufactured, because they coordinate belief, rumor, and expectation.
- The source presents the fish-belly and fox-cry devices as practical mobilization techniques, not as supernatural proof.
