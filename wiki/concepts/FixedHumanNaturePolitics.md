# Fixed Human Nature Politics

## Definition
Fixed-human-nature politics is the risk of treating vice, virtue, species or identity as an unchangeable essence that settles what political institutions and education can accomplish.

## Current Synthesis
The late-Han episodes use suspicious interest analysis; a Qinji episode directly contests the notion that people cannot change. Literary and philosophical readings complicate both permanent human badness and a fantasy of perfectly rational innocence. These are interpretations across one history series and book discussions, not independent tests of an anthropological law.

## Key Claims
- The claim that power reveals and people can be screened but not educated is a court-risk maxim, not demonstrated human nature.
- Actors under similar late-Han pressure make different choices because legitimacy, ambition, trust and family exposure matter alongside self-interest.
- The Mencius–Xunzi contrast does not exclude moral formation through learning, habituation, ritual, law and changing circumstances.
- Swiftian satire questions permanent human depravity and the opposite ideal of cold rational purity; narrator and author cannot be equated.
- Political realism and human–animal comparison can expose deception without entailing that cruelty is always justified or civilization always false.

## Evidence
- Power as a limited lens: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-943-quanshi-kaoyan-renxing-beihou-cangzhe-juda-xiongxian-lg7ia5xok-swdmhml35yv9qqkvt]] opens “人，只能筛选，无法教育”, then recounts the 189 succession crisis after [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]] left no formal heir. [[JianShuoLateHan|蹇硕]] tries to preempt [[HeJin|何进]], who destroys Jian Shuo and then the [[EmpressDowagerDongLateHan|董太后]] faction under [[EmpressDowagerHe|何太后]]'s new court. [[SuccessionNonDesignationRisk]] explains why the actors' danger was real, but their choices do not prove nobody can learn.
- Incentives are differentiated: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-954-renxingbenshan-haishi-renxingbene-lkurz9dbb25jrjyl5c-rrahda39h]] opens from [[XingETheory|性恶论]] but distinguishes [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s concern for the captured [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]]'s recognized legitimacy, [[YuanShu|袁术]]'s ambition in resisting an alternative ruler, and [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]]'s refusal of kingship amid his son [[LiuHeLateHan|刘和]]'s exposure at Chang'an. These are the host's motives, not direct access to their thoughts. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-988-shoujin-lingru-ta-shi-hanmo-beican-de-huangdi-lsdkpakvk5u-omegcri-wstup-zl]] uses [[HanFei|韩非]]'s interest analysis of the 195 court collapse but also notes benevolent love and [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]] as trust-building counterpoints.
- Formation against fixity: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-2-ruhe-kandai-xingbenshan-yu-xingbene-lmawsqkttjmitwkczhyfs7f7-mqt]] contrasts [[Mengzi|孟子]]'s [[XingShanTheory|性善论]] and [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s raw desires, then insists Xunzi's teaching, law and rites can alter behavior; [[HumanNaturePlasticity]] and [[ActionDefinesIdentity]] resist judging a person from a permanent stain. Its smoke-mask disaster anecdote illustrates [[DisasterCivilizationRetreat]] under urgent scarcity, not permanent evil.
- Satirical test: [[51-yanshi-fanrenlei-tonghua-gushi-geliefu-youji-ke-shen-le-qu-le-632315320]] reads [[JonathanSwift]]'s [[GulliversTravels|《格列佛游记》]] through [[ScaleReversalSatire]], Laputa's abstract technocracy and the [[Houyhnhnms|慧骃]]' cold order. [[LemuelGulliver]]'s disgust at his family and a kind captain is not necessarily Swift's final judgment; [[PureRationalityTrap]] and [[AuthorCharacterSeparation]] make the distinction essential. Its references to [[ThomasHobbes]] and [[NiccoloMachiavelli]] are the hosts' intellectual comparison, not direct testimony from those thinkers.
- Realism without closure: [[72-junzhulun-du-ta-shi-yichang-weixian-de-maoxian-673440837]] treats [[ThePrince|《君主论》]] as situated in Florentine struggle, distinguishing [[MachiavellianRealism]] and [[NonMoralPoliticalAnalysis]] from unlimited wickedness; [[GoodIntentionsPoliticalLimits]] keeps responsibility alongside effectiveness. [[39-zhexuejia-yu-lang-zai-langgeduoke-yongheng-de-xiatian-611233065]] reads [[MarkRowlands]]' life with wolf [[Brenin|布列宁]] as a challenge to automatic “civilized human” superiority: [[CivilizationAsDeception]] and [[AnimalIntelligenceModes]] are philosophical comparisons, not settled zoological proof.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Qinji 101-2's educability directly challenges Hanji 943's “cannot educate” aphorism; neither opening slogan should be laundered into an empirical universal. The Hanji episodes share one history-commentary series, and Dong Taihou's death itself has divergent chronicle and novel accounts.
- Identity labels can describe politically relevant histories without exhausting a person's possibilities. Swift's fictional Houyhnhnms and Rowlands' wolf memoir do not establish policy or animal-science laws; Machiavelli is interpreted through a book episode, not quoted as endorsing every host conclusion.

## What Changed
- Recast severe historical aphorisms as one contested position beside plasticity, satire and bounded political realism.
