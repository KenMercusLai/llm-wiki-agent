# AI Content Devaluation

## Definition
AI content devaluation describes a fall in perceived scarcity or attention value when cheap generation expands the supply of text, music, images and apps, especially when audiences cannot see the author’s selection or care.

## Current Synthesis
More output need not mean all work is worthless. Taste, original reporting, credible provenance and embodied experience may become relatively more valuable; productivity gains and audience resistance coexist.

## Key Claims
- Low-cost creation expands supply faster than discovery and monetization capacity in some markets.
- Repeated model conventions can make competent output feel generic, while distinctive human selection remains scarce.
- Disclosure and the location of human authorship affect trust differently across product categories and newsrooms.
- AI assistance can increase access and coverage without establishing that all AI-made work lacks value.

## Evidence
- [[TatianaCirasano]] describes [[GenerativeAIMusic]] tools such as [[Suno]] opening music creation to more people while worsening [[ArtistDiscoveryFragmentation]] and the work of [[AIArtistDevelopment]]: attention and revenue do not expand in proportion to songs. [[SophieHagney]] argues that easily imitated [[CorporateMemphis]]-type styles become stale; [[AITasteSimulation]] can reproduce conventions without embodied [[EmbodiedTaste]] or early discovery. Sources: [[tech-20260817-tech-pod-128-tech-20260817-tech-pod-128]], [[tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260813-0813-mp-tech-pod-128]].
- The [[FengyanFengyu]] Vol. 164 hosts’ “AI did not read” reaction concerns articles, apps and social posts whose author seems not to have thought through the communication; this makes [[AICommunicationAbility]] part of the value proposition. [[JustinYan]] similarly says his [[NewSpot]] product keeps its daily line human-authored even while models help filter news. The [[XFFXFramework]] reading interview distinguishes [[AIAuthorshipPresence]] and a reader’s [[PersonalKnowledgeEcology]] from instantly generated answers: a reader can transform generic material into a contextual note, but that does not make every output worth paying for under [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]]. These are audience judgments, not a price index. [[VibeCoding]] and [[OnDemandApps]] offer a software analogy: rapid generation is not product readiness. Sources: [[vol-164-cong-pingguo-liaodao-ruanjian-weilai-agentic-software-zhende-yaolaile-1-6639-1]], [[vol-160-yi-nian-duo-yihou-zai-liao-ai-xie-daima-vibe-coding-1-6623-1]], [[dushu-jiushi-zai-du-yige-ren-de-f-li4qt9zs2bss4tklnj3yg9y-quo1]].
- [[ColleenKirk]] reports lower trust, authenticity, purchase intent and word of mouth for AI-origin labels, strongest for identity or emotionally important products; human-led writing with AI assistance did not draw the same penalty in her account. Her [[GenuineFred]] label illustrates [[HumanAuthorshipPremium]] and context-specific [[AlgorithmAversion]], without implying that a utilitarian pen or carpet buyer has identical preferences. Sources: [[tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260413-0413-mp-tech-pod-128]].
- [[WillaRemus]] describes how, at [[ThePlainDealer]], AI meeting transcripts, lead-finding and letter summaries help stretched [[LocalJournalism]], while its [[AIRewriteDesk]] and [[AdvancedLocalExpressDesk]] byline prompt objections about [[AIWrittenJournalism]] and [[AIJournalismTrust]]. [[LocalNewsAutomationTradeoff]] asks whether otherwise absent basic coverage offsets a thinner reporting voice. [[BuzzFeed]]’s [[QuizParty]] and [[MediaAIRescueStrategy]] are survival attempts, not evidence of user willingness to pay for automated media. Sources: [[tech-20260311-0311-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260311-0311-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[tech-20260320-0320-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260320-0320-mp-tech-pod-128]].
- [[AIShortDrama]] changes [[ShortDramaEconomics]] by lowering per-minute costs but [[AIVideoProductionWorkflow]] still needs scripts, editing and platform distribution; copied topics can create sameness. [[Seedance]] and other [[VideoModels]] similarly widen output supply, without proving audience demand. [[ErinGriffith]] argues [[AnswerEngineOptimization]] and [[GenerativeEngineOptimization]] for [[AIDiscoverySEO]] may reward dense factual brand content over generated fluff. [[FengTouQuan]] points to [[AIResistantExperientialConsumption]]—[[Shunde]] food tourism and [[HumanConnectionUnderAI]] in communities—as a contrasting scarce experience. Sources: [[266-cong-hongguo-dao-ai-duanju-shui-zai-ge-shui-de-ming-lgzf6bu7bfalr5qvnhlfzkufahob]], [[zhili-bianzhi-de-chunjie-jianwenlu-yu-nachang-zhengzai-yunniang-de-youdai-weiji-1]], [[tech-20260304-0304-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260304-0304-mp-tech-pod-128]], [[137-cong-shunde-zhuroupo-dao-hanguo-shengshuidong-naxie-ai-wufa-qudai-de-tiyan-xiaofei-feng-tou-quan-1-137-1]].
- [[KateCrawford]] treats [[AISlop]] and [[ModelCollapse]] as possible feedback effects of saturated synthetic material, alongside the real material costs of [[AIMetabolicInfrastructure]]. [[StanislawLem]]’s fictional electronic poet in [[TheCyberiad]] overwhelms editors with competent poems under multiple names, while human poets lose status. It anticipates [[MachineCreativityThreat]] through supply and prestige, not an empirical observation about [[ChatGPT]] or music markets. Sources: [[kate-crawford-mapping-empires]], [[45-jiqirendashi-duo-xiwang-laimu-neng-pingjia-yixia-chatgpt-a-621855112]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The music, consumer-product and local-news examples have different markets and measures; none proves universal price decline.
- Routine AI assistance, human-curated output and wholly generated work should not be treated as one authorship category; the science-fiction example is illustrative.

## What Changed
- The argument now distinguishes supply, distribution, aesthetic sameness and authorship trust rather than labeling all generated content worthless.
