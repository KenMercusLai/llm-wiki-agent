## Definition
Cross-cultural product design adapts interfaces, content and distribution to local meanings while deciding which underlying product mechanics can remain shared.

## Current Synthesis
A designer's interface examples, a former TikTok PM's global-platform account, and two short-drama production accounts point to different localization layers. Translation and visual polish are insufficient if the trust cue, creator ecology, status fantasy, consent signal or [[DataDrivenEntertainmentProduct|paid-acquisition economics]] fails in the target setting. The cases do not establish a single global recipe.

## Key Claims
- Language length, date format, visual density, color and proof of trust can change usability and conversion without changing the core need.
- A unified platform container can coexist with local content supply, creator conventions and operating judgment.
- Fiction export can retain an emotional narrative engine while changing genre shell, setting, appearance and status cues.
- Local performers and market-feedback loops can expose script and value mismatches that translation alone misses.

## Evidence
- Interface layer: [[ep87-duihua-duli-shejishi-daqi-tongguo-sheji-bangzhu-chanpin-zuohao-zengzhang-luymytt48g-ejwsl6bfuko2xsdoc]] has [[Daqi]] describe China, Southeast Asia, European and U.S. differences in text expansion, date format, information density, color and trust proof, drawing on [[Lazada]] and [[TikTok]] work. [[ProductRoast]] emphasizes problem framing, scenario copy and CTA before polishing a page; visits and account-manager knowledge sharpen those assumptions. These are practitioner examples, not universal color rules.
- Shared container, local ecology: [[musical-ly-ruhe-chengwei-tiktok-pm-yanzhong-de-zijie-chanpin-wenhua-he-quanqiuhua-zhilu-zijie-tiaodong-di-5-ji-ludflvaw7lid-tci9gagbbxobvtz]] has [[Vanessa]] reject the notion that [[TikTok]] was merely an overseas [[Douyin]] algorithm transplant. [[MusicalLy]] already supplied a music-centered community and creation tools; ByteDance added recommendation, safety, growth and gradual 2018 brand integration. Her [[GlobalProductLocalization]] shorthand is “容器统一，内容本地化,” with market timing and regional teams rather than identical content everywhere.
- Story cues: [[cong-yangshi-jilupian-dao-baokuan-ai-duanju-di-yi-pi-zhuanshen-de-daoyan-s10e11-3c05e3d5-d8f6-44c1-97ca-698261d7b2bc]] describes [[Chouxiangzai]]'s [[WuGeGeGeDouChongWo]] changing settings, appearances, status symbols and fantasy scale for overseas audiences via [[RodeoAI]]; he attributes reported overseas success of [[YiWanFuWengHuiGui]] despite visible AI flaws to story fit, not visual perfection. This AI-production anecdote is distinct from live-action performance.
- Performance and acquisition: [[ep240-ba-zong-tian-chong-zai-haiwai-duanju-chuhai-de-chanye-mima-ltvxvfj5ko6djhsjatiphdy-dzek]] distinguishes translated/dubbed domestic hits from overseas self-production. [[DuanNongyu|段农裕]] reports local actors correcting awkward lines and intimacy or consent cues; werewolf/vampire shells may carry familiar romance beats. The reporting team, including [[HuangZiyiSanlian|黄子逸]], treats [[ShortDramaPaidTrafficDistribution]] as a separate test of hooks, click response and recovery of spend, while [[WomenOrientedMelodramaDemand]] already had foreign genre precedents.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Daqi's regional observations do not establish that green is a universal Muslim-majority brand norm; the old page's specific assertion lacks support in the note. AI and live-action dramas differ, and neither a ranking claim nor paid-traffic ROI is independent proof of repeatable cross-cultural success. A performer can suggest a change without settling every cultural or consent issue; the shared container has product-fit limits, including Vanessa's criticism of forcing image/text jobs into a full-screen swipe feed.

## What Changed
- Distinguished interface localization, platform ecology and story production rather than treating all as translation.
- Marked reported market outcomes and regional tastes as interview observations.
