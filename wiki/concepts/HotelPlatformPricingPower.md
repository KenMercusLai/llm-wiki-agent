## Definition
Hotel platform pricing power is the ability of a booking intermediary to influence a hotel's effective rates, discounts, visibility, inventory distribution and customer acquisition through traffic, contracts and booking tools. Merchant-side control is distinct from user-side fee disclosure.

## Current Synthesis
The Ctrip episodes describe a concentrated [[OnlineTravelAgency]] as both valuable booking infrastructure and a potential source of merchant dependence. An early-2026 investigation-focused account documents hotel complaints; a later episode reports a penalty centered on exclusivity and price-parity practices. Large hotel groups can build direct demand with membership benefits, while newer AI-led order channels raise a separate attribution/fee question. Allegations, reported regulator action and platform replies have different evidentiary status.

## Key Claims
- Inventory, booking interfaces, customer habits and demand aggregation give an OTA negotiating leverage beyond its headline commission.
- Merchant complaints about promotions, display and automatic repricing point to reduced pricing autonomy, but specific conduct remains allegation unless established by the regulator.
- Status-linked exclusivity and cross-platform price-parity monitoring can reinforce demand dependence; the later episode attributes a 51.79亿元 penalty to merchant-side conduct, not to every consumer complaint.
- Hotel loyalty and direct booking create a partial counterweight for scaled groups; smaller independent hotels and homestays lack comparable member networks.
- AI assistant-to-order channels introduce distinct questions about recommendation neutrality, completed-order charges and attribution rather than proving a new paid-ranking system.

## Evidence
- Concentrated infrastructure: [[kunzai-xitong-li-de-jiudian-ni-buzhidao-de-xiecheng-longduan-lianchengshi-keji-luandun]] traces [[Ctrip]]'s early phone/hotel booking operations, inventory and [[HotelPMSInventoryControl]], then investment and integration around Qunar, Elong and Tongcheng. The hosts cite complaints by [[YunnanHomestayAssociation]] and other operators about forced promotions, differential display and automatic price changes; they propose [[PlatformDataRegulation]] to inspect orders, splits and prices, not nationalization.
- Claimed parity and sanction: [[ep91-dingfang-dingpiao-dingjiangshan-xiecheng-51-yi-wei-aoman-maidan-lovhfkz4rklv1ik-uqyeswrdf3uw]] reports that [[StateAdministrationForMarketRegulation]] imposed 16.58亿元 in confiscation plus 35.21亿元 in fines, totaling 51.79亿元. It describes “特牌” merchant exclusivity against rivals such as Fliggy and “金牌” prices allegedly 20 yuan or 5% below competing channels, with “调价助手” and “挂牌通” monitoring/adjustment tools. These are the episode's representations of [[TravelPlatformMerchantExclusivity]] and [[TravelPriceParityEnforcement]], not independently examined primary legal documents. It reports 2024 core share around 56%, above 70% including connected platforms, on its own definitions.
- Alternative demand: [[140-jiudian-jituan-weishenme-dou-rezhongyu-dazao-huiyuan-he-jifen-tixi-chuantai-yuanxingzhe-yu-suibingjiang-lj-xa3wxzjog6-8zm9nfakh6ulew]] explains [[HotelLoyaltyPrograms]] and [[HotelDirectBookingChannels]]: member rates, breakfast, points, status and app booking can improve repeat business if rewards cost less than avoided OTA commissions. It describes [[HuazhuGroup]]'s more integrated Chinese membership than [[JinjiangHotels]] and the appeal of [[MarriottInternational]]'s 2016 Starwood acquisition. This is a large-group strategy, not a general escape for independents.
- New entry fee: [[xingbake-huiying-mixue-bingcheng-daigong-deng-chuanwen-li-ning-fouren-yu-mubapei-qianyue-1006054195]] reports some merchants' 12% comprehensive fee for hotel orders from [[Doubao]] via [[Douyin]], while Doubao said life-services had no paid promotion and charged only after completed orders. The disagreement concerns [[AITravelCommerceTrust]] and [[PlatformIntermediationTax]], not proven paid placement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
An OTA can lower search friction and provide fulfillment, service and reliable inventory; concentration alone does not establish abuse. The January investigation and July reported penalty are different stages. Merchant-specific automatic repricing and displays need separate substantiation; the later podcast's penalty account should not be treated as a primary regulator decision. [[TravelBookingHiddenFees]] and cancellation-price disputes concern consumer trust but, according to that episode, were not the penalty's core merchant-side basis. Loyalty benefits must be financed and cannot necessarily displace all platform traffic. The 12% figure is a merchant report, not a general platform rate; Doubao contests the paid-promotion inference.

## What Changed
- Separated platform leverage, specific merchant allegations and a later source-reported enforcement account.
- Distinguished direct-booking counterstrategy from a universal remedy and AI-channel fees from paid ranking.
- Preserved source-reported sanction components and parity thresholds with their attribution.
