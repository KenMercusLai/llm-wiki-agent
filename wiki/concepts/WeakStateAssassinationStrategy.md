# Weak-State Assassination Strategy / 弱国刺杀式破局

Weak-state assassination strategy / 弱国刺杀式破局 is the late-Warring-States pattern introduced by [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]]. [[YanState|燕国]] cannot defeat [[QinState|秦国]] directly, and [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] rejects [[JuWu|居武]]'s slower [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] plan as too uncertain. The prince therefore imagines a shortcut: concentrate national effort on killing [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] personally.

The concept differs from ordinary battlefield strategy and from private revenge. It treats assassination as an attempted state-level substitute for failed coalition capacity: if the dominant ruler dies, the stronger state may fall into disorder and the weaker state may gain time. The source presents this as desperate and structurally weak, not as a reliable way to repair the balance of power.

The episode also shows why the chosen assassin matters. [[JingKe|荆轲]] is useful precisely because he does not look like a professional military killer; his wandering shi and侠士 identity can be made to carry both access and moral legitimacy. [[TianGuang|田光]]'s suicide then turns the strategy into an honor-bound mission as well as a political gamble.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-3-jingke-ciqin-weihe-jiujiu-bu-xingdong-zai-deng-shui-liihe-kgwigu3bggxmyofgvotged]] adds the execution layer. The weak-state shortcut still requires [[AssassinationAccessCredential|access credentials]] strong enough to bring Jing Ke close to Ying Zheng: [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s head and the [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]]. It also requires weapon preparation through [[XuFuren|徐夫人]]'s dagger, assistant selection through [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]], and enough trust between patron and operative to wait for the right partner.

That trust is where the strategy weakens. [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]]'s suspicion turns Jing Ke's delay into [[MistrustTriggeredMissionLaunch|mistrust-triggered mission launch]], suggesting that assassination as a weak-state strategy can fail before reaching the target if urgency destroys operational discipline.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] adds the strategy's macro and micro limits. On the macro side, the episode argues that even killing Ying Zheng might only have caused short-term Qin court disorder because [[QinState|秦国]]'s [[FarmingWarStatecraft|farming-war statecraft]], [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|military-merit rank]], and [[QinEastwardPressure|eastward pressure]] already outlived any one ruler. On the micro side, the plan deteriorates inside [[Xianyang|咸阳]] when [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]] panics and [[JingKe|荆轲]] has to approach alone.

## Key Claims
- Assassination can appear attractive when direct war and slow alliance repair both seem unavailable.
- The strategy depends on converting a state problem into a person problem: reach the ruler, induce succession disorder, and buy time.
- Its plausibility is fragile because it assumes killing one ruler can disrupt an institutionalized conquest machine.
- The operative's social identity matters because access, suspicion, and moral reputation become part of the plan.
- Weak-state assassination is adjacent to [[PoliticalAssassinationEthics]], but here the justification is future survival rather than retrospective punishment.
- Qinji 114-3 adds that access materials, assistant fit, weapon choice, and sponsor trust are not secondary details; they are the practical conditions that decide whether the shortcut can even be attempted.
- Qinji 115-1 adds that a successful strike against one ruler may still fail strategically when the target state's institutions, incentives, and military momentum survive the ruler.
- Qinji 115-1 also shows the execution risk becoming visible before the strike: a mission built as two-person coordination can degrade into solo action under court pressure.
