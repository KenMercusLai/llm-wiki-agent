# Weak-State Assassination Strategy / 弱国刺杀式破局

Weak-state assassination strategy / 弱国刺杀式破局 is the late-Warring-States pattern introduced by [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]]. [[YanState|燕国]] cannot defeat [[QinState|秦国]] directly, and [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] rejects [[JuWu|居武]]'s slower [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] and [[Xiongnu|匈奴]] plan as too uncertain. The prince therefore imagines a shortcut: concentrate national effort on killing [[QinShiHuang|嬴政 / 秦始皇]] personally.

The concept differs from ordinary battlefield strategy and from private revenge. It treats assassination as an attempted state-level substitute for failed coalition capacity: if the dominant ruler dies, the stronger state may fall into disorder and the weaker state may gain time. The source presents this as desperate and structurally weak, not as a reliable way to repair the balance of power.

The episode also shows why the chosen assassin matters. [[JingKe|荆轲]] is useful precisely because he does not look like a professional military killer; his wandering shi and侠士 identity can be made to carry both access and moral legitimacy. [[TianGuang|田光]]'s suicide then turns the strategy into an honor-bound mission as well as a political gamble.

## Key Claims
- Assassination can appear attractive when direct war and slow alliance repair both seem unavailable.
- The strategy depends on converting a state problem into a person problem: reach the ruler, induce succession disorder, and buy time.
- Its plausibility is fragile because it assumes killing one ruler can disrupt an institutionalized conquest machine.
- The operative's social identity matters because access, suspicion, and moral reputation become part of the plan.
- Weak-state assassination is adjacent to [[PoliticalAssassinationEthics]], but here the justification is future survival rather than retrospective punishment.
