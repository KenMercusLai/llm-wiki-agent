## Definition
Alcohol as power catalyst / 酒作为权力催化剂 is a source-scoped interpretive pattern: drink can release or expose existing desire, grief, humiliation, solidarity or violence, while rank and institutional leverage determine the consequences. It is not a universal or sufficient cause.

## Current Synthesis
At Wancheng, [[HuCheEr|胡车儿]] drinks with [[DianWei|典韦]] and steals his heavy halberds before [[ZhangXiu|张绣]]'s attack. The guard's disarming matters because [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s treatment of Zhang Xiu's surrendered household, his gift to Hu Che'er, the leaked killing plan and [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s camp-access tactic already created [[DesireDrivenCommandExposure|command exposure]]. Dian Wei dies defending the entrance; [[CaoAng|曹昂]] and [[CaoAnmin|曹安民]] also die, and [[LadyDingCaoCao|丁夫人]] later breaks with Cao over Cao Ang's death. The woman named 周氏 in one transcript is 邹氏 in another; the episode's “for one woman” is causal compression, not the whole chain.

At [[ChangpingguanBattleLateHan|长平观]], [[HanSui|韩遂]] arrives ostensibly to mediate between [[MaTeng|马腾]] and [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] but joins Ma Teng; “酒误我” dramatizes a plausible alliance shift, not proof that drinking created it. After defeat, Han Sui's hometown appeal leads to drinking with [[FanChouLateHan|樊稠]], turning hostility into a politically dangerous personal contact. In restored Zhao, [[LiLiangZhaoGeneral|李良]] already has [[WangLiQin|王离]]'s offer when [[ZhaoWuchenSister|武臣姐姐]] drunkenly fails to return his salute: status injury and a viable defection path together precede the killing of [[WuChen|武臣]]. The [[LuWineHandanWarCascade|鲁酒薄而邯郸围]] anecdote likewise requires tribute etiquette, [[ChuXuanwang|楚宣王]]'s affront and interstate opportunism, not weak wine alone.

The catalyst can run in other directions. [[LiYuanTang|李渊]]'s palace drinking setup with [[PeiJi|裴寂]] and [[LiShimin|李世民]] is presented as leverage toward rebellion under [[YangGuangSui|杨广]] only given Li Yuan's prior resources; [[ZhangFei|张飞]]'s drunken loss of Xuzhou deprives [[LiuBei|刘备]] of a base. [[GaoYangNorthernQi|高洋]] exemplifies the far greater harm of unchecked imperial impunity. [[JinPingMei|《金瓶梅》]] uses “酒为色媒人” for desire and corruption, whereas [[GuifeiZuijiu|《贵妃醉酒》]] stages [[YangGuifei|杨贵妃]]'s abandonment (with [[MeiLanfang|梅兰芳]]'s later adaptation). [[LiQingzhao|李清照]]'s wine lyrics span youthful leisure, marital separation and later grief; [[LiBai|李白]] and [[DuFu|杜甫]] place wine in poetic society. [[TemperateDrinkingSelfControl|Restraint]], expressed through 《礼记》, [[Confucius|孔子]] and the episode's [[GenghisKhan|成吉思汗]] reading, is the boundary between enjoyment and disorder.

## Key Claims
- Drink can loosen restraint or facilitate social contact, but prior incentives and access explain why an outcome becomes consequential.
- In military command and political hierarchy, intoxication can expose guards, amplify humiliation or give cruelty a much larger radius.
- Status insults and diplomatic gift disputes become dangerous only inside already unstable relationships and available strategic options.
- Literature uses drinking to depict desire, loneliness, social warmth and grief, not only damage; self-command changes the result.

## Evidence
- Command vulnerability and alliance: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1013-dianwei-zhisi-jingshi-caocao-haose-haileta-lhe6jrpjp3cfbdhvjtdiezijfnoh|Hanji 1013]] gives the guard-disarming sequence, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-973-changpingguan-zhizhan-shengsi-zhidi-zhanhou-jing-chonggui-yuhao-lgazc9dewfayb8qnkncfkfa12yet|Hanji 973]] dramatizes Han Sui's mediator-to-ally shift and later contact with Fan Chou.
- Political scale and coercion: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-2-pandian-huangdimen-jiuhou-xiaopihao-livpg3zh7lsqffgca2pomiqnlvyd|Qinji 131-2]] contrasts Zhang Fei's lost Xuzhou, Li Yuan's palace predicament and Gao Yang's violence; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-129-2-lishishang-hejiu-neng-wu-duo-da-shi-zhuan-gei-jiugui-laogong-kan-zhe-ji-lvzxpoxclwdrr66bwejd-bga8dp|Qinji 129-2]] records Li Liang's preexisting Qin offer and drunken ritual slight.
- Tribute and relational context: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-15-jiu-bu-chun-yinfa-de-shijie-dazhan-li-iojxthq8komhlhyumho3yvtem|Zhouji 15]] traces the Lu-wine anecdote through Chu's response and Wei's opportunistic Handan siege.
- Constructive literary and ethical limits: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-3-liqingzhao-zhongnian-fuqi-libie-hejiu-jie-xiangsi-lnzxhrj2iulahccy2-pkm-wphdei|Qinji 131-3]] develops Li Qingzhao's life phases, Du Fu's drinking portraits and “得酒趣，不失酒德”; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-131-2-pandian-huangdimen-jiuhou-xiaopihao-livpg3zh7lsqffgca2pomiqnlvyd|Qinji 131-2]] supplies the theatrical and novel examples.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
None of these episodes establishes alcohol as a single determinant of war, rebellion or abuse. The Wancheng source's name variant and compression remain attributed; Qinji 131-2's Li Qingzhao passage cuts off and Qinji 131-3 provides the fuller account. Li Yuan's story is not a transferable strategy, and Genghis Khan's restraint is not a complete explanation of his success. The Zhouji tale is a literary-historical causal chain, not measured proof that wine alone caused the siege.

## What Changed
- Recast discrete drinking stories as a conditional power-and-access mechanism.
- Preserved constructive poetic use and restraint beside military and political harms.
