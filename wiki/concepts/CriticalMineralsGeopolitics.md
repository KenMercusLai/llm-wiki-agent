## Definition
Critical minerals geopolitics concerns how extraction, processing, capital, trade relationships and environmental burdens turn physical inputs into strategic bargaining power.

## Current Synthesis
Ore availability alone is a weak proxy for security: processing know-how, skilled labor, reliable customers, permits and finance determine whether a mineral reaches an industrial user. The Greenland and Latin American accounts show that partnership and consent differ from territorial ownership. AI and battery demand increase the stakes while ecological and substitution effects complicate a simple “mine more” answer.

## Key Claims
- Rare-earth vulnerability often lies in separation and refining, not merely the location of deposits.
- Price volatility, downstream demand and patient financing can defeat mine reopening even where resources are known.
- Strategic access can be negotiated through trade, infrastructure and existing defense agreements without overriding local sovereignty.
- Electrification, AI and defense may compete for copper, grid equipment and specialist labor as well as rare earths.
- Resource security must account for water, land, waste and the option to substitute or recycle rather than only acquisition volume.

## Evidence
- Processing and financing: [[battlefield-rare-earths-how-the-us-lost-to-china]] follows the 1949 [[MountainPassRareEarthMine]] discovery, [[Molycorp]]'s television-era europium sales, later low-price [[China|Chinese]] competition, its [[ProjectPhoenix]] reopening and price-crash failure, then [[MPMaterials]] and [[NioCorp]] rebuilding efforts. [[RodEggert]] describes China's long finance, training and domestic-processing strategy; the episode estimates around 90% of processing there. The 2010 [[Japan]] export shock and 2025 tariff-linked restrictions illustrate [[RareEarthExportLeverage]]. U.S. grants, loans, floor prices and a reported 15% 2025 Defense Department MP Materials stake are responses, not guaranteed success.
- Access with consent: [[how-to-get-what-greenland-has-with-permission]] says [[Greenland]] has heavy rare earths but needs roads, ports, electricity, long timelines and separation capacity. [[GracelandBaskaran]] describes infrastructure constraints; self-government, [[Inuit]] land norms and Greenlanders' refusal to be sold constrain any plan; U.S. defense access dating to 1951 and possible partners including Brazil, Australia, Saudi Arabia and India show [[StrategicAccessWithoutAnnexation]]. [[latin-lessons-the-donroe-doctrine-boost-6a4233e26b6690f46aae4e3d]] reports 2025 [[LatinAmerica|Latin American]] FDI of $205bn, [[Chile|Chilean]] copper, prospective [[Paraguay|Paraguayan]] minerals and a U.S.-backed [[Brazil|Brazilian]] rare-earth mine amid [[UnitedStates|U.S.]]–China rivalry; it warns small rare-earth markets may not fund every project.
- Shared bottlenecks: [[DanDreyfus]] in [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-dan-dreyfus-americas-critical-minerals-crisis-is-here-41594225]] links copper, silver, rare earths, transmission, permits, offtake, grid upgrades and [[CraftLaborBottleneck|craft labor]] to [[DataCenterPowerBottleneck|data centers]], aerospace, defense and electrification. His 50,000-ton-per-1GW copper estimate and claimed Ford shutdown exposure are interview assertions. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-all-ins-2026-predictions-39681260]] records [[ChamathPalihapitiya]]'s 2026 copper and critical-metal investment prediction, not a demand measurement or return proof.
- Material costs and adaptation: [[kate-crawford-mapping-empires]] has [[KateCrawford]] connect Chilean lithium, water tables, land and short-lived AI hardware to [[AIMetabolicInfrastructure]]. [[e229-cong-shougong-zuofang-dao-quanqiu-diyi-zhongguo-dongli-dianchi-nixi-shi]] traces [[CATL]] and [[BYD]] battery capacity to protected demand, process yields and local clusters; [[SodiumIonBatteryStorage]] and [[BatteryRecyclingLoop]] are potential partial reductions in lithium, cobalt and nickel exposure, not resource-free batteries.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Planet Money source expressly finds no “smoking gun” for [[MarkSmith]]'s accusation that China intentionally crashed rare-earth prices. Dreyfus and Chamath give investor views rather than independently verified mineral forecasts. Crawford's ecological critique does not by itself quantify one mine's harm; the battery note describes domestic manufacturing, not independently demonstrated overseas mineral independence. Greenlandic agency is a substantive condition, not a logistical inconvenience.

## What Changed
- Distinguished mineral deposits from processing, financing and skilled execution.
- Added sovereignty, ecology and substitution as constraints on strategic supply plans.
