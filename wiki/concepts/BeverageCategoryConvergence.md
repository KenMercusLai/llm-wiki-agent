## Definition
Beverage category convergence is competition between established coffee and tea-drink chains as one uses its store and supply-chain network to sell the other's products; market, operational and brand conditions limit how far it travels.

## Current Synthesis
The strongest evidence concerns Chinese tea chains testing coffee, not a proven symmetric or worldwide merger of categories. [[MixueBingcheng|蜜雪]]'s [[LuckyCoffee|幸运咖]] tests higher-tier offices, where [[LuckinCoffee|瑞幸]] has established density and memberships. Korea and the United States illustrate geographically different opportunity for [[GongCha|贡茶]], not that coffee chains there are necessarily converting to tea.

## Key Claims
- Existing stores, franchisees and supply chains can let Chinese tea brands add coffee without building an entirely new network.
- Coffee standardization may ease tea-to-coffee entry, whereas fresh-fruit tea preparation and customer brand perception complicate the reverse move; this is the hosts' judgment, not a universal rule.
- Lucky Coffee's move into higher-tier offices is a competitive test, not evidence it has already displaced Luckin's customers.
- Coffee saturation and tea-drink competition differ across geographies: Korean and U.S. milk-tea cases do not mechanically follow China's product cycle.

## Evidence
- Network leverage: [[141-kafei-zhanzheng-2026-jigouhua-yu-bentuhua-feng-tou-quan-1-141-1]]'s [[FengTouQuan|疯投圈]] hosts argue [[MixueBingcheng]] and [[Guming|古茗]] can reuse existing procurement and stores to sell coffee as an incremental daily drink; the same hosts describe scaled [[CoffeeChainInstitutionalization|coffee chains]] as hard to dislodge.
- Asymmetric workflow: [[141-kafei-zhanzheng-2026-jigouhua-yu-bentuhua-feng-tou-quan-1-141-1]] contrasts comparatively standardized coffee making with fruit-tea ingredient handling, preparation and permission from customers who recognize a coffee brand principally as coffee; this is a forecast about entry difficulty, not proof that no coffee chain can sell tea.
- Office-channel contest: [[lanjian-hangtian-wancheng-zhongguo-shouci-ludi-huojian-huishou-yushu-keji-shizhi-chaoguo-3000-yi-1007302506]] says Lucky Coffee surpassed 10,000 outlets and was entering first- and second-tier office and industrial-park districts using a low-price/franchise model. It also identifies Luckin's office density and member system as near-term defenses; no actual market-share transfer is established.
- Local opportunity: [[137-cong-shunde-zhuroupo-dao-hanguo-shengshuidong-naxie-ai-wufa-qudai-de-tiyan-xiaofei-feng-tou-quan-1-137-1]] describes high [[SouthKorea|Korean]] coffee consumption, [[MannerCoffee|Manner]]'s difficulties there, [[Sandunban|三顿半]]'s distinct instant positioning, and Gong Cha's relative tea-drink opening. [[e232-canyin-chuhai-you-xin-zhaor-ma-cong-dingtaifeng-yu-gongcha-naxia-meiguo-chudai-xiaoguan-liaoqi-3bb14dd4-0b13-4173-9d5b-0e63bf98ae1d]] says older-style milk tea can still sell in the [[UnitedStates|U.S.]] with local taste, franchise execution and early sites, despite newer tea formats in China; leases, permits, landlord preferences and mall exclusivity are U.S.-specific growth constraints for [[HeyTea|喜茶]], [[MoliNaiBai|茉莉奶白]] and [[Chagee|霸王茶姬]] too.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Korea and U.S. accounts describe tea's local positioning, not coffee operators crossing into tea. Source lanjian-hangtian-wancheng-zhongguo-shouci-ludi-huojian-huishou-yushu-keji-shizhi-chaoguo-3000-yi-1007302506 is a broad news roundup, and its office entry is a reported attempt. Source 141-kafei-zhanzheng-2026-jigouhua-yu-bentuhua-feng-tou-quan-1-141-1 offers industry interpretation; no causal measurement of category substitution or displaced incumbents appears in these notes.

## What Changed
- Separate observed network entry from hypothesized displacement and geographically specific tea opportunities.
