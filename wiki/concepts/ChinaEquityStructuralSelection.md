## Definition
China equity structural selection is a source-dated investing thesis that after broad repricing, sector and company returns can diverge according to earnings, orders, capital returns and valuation rather than simply following a China index.

## Current Synthesis
The four related macro episodes revise emphasis across January, March and July 2026. January favored selective A/H opportunities after the 2025 rally; the March pullback shifted attention to A-share support, Hong Kong liquidity exposure and investor holdability; July's narrow AI-led gains called for concentration control and cash. These are changing perspectives, not a single enduring stock recommendation.

## Key Claims
- Sentiment-led index gains can precede earnings recovery and later give way to company-level dispersion.
- Innovation drugs, AI hardware chains and selective consumer segments need product, order and profit evidence beyond labels.
- A shares and Hong Kong shares respond differently to domestic yields and overseas liquidity.
- Crowded technology leadership and expensive private-market themes can worsen entry risk despite real technological progress.
- Ordinary investors' cash, index defaults, liabilities and human-capital risk matter as much as a favored sector.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All four sources come from the same show and recurring voices. January's Hong Kong optimism, March's A-share relative support and July's cash-first stance reflect different dates and risk conditions. None proves structural selection outperforms a broad index. This page is not personal investment advice; [[PortfolioSuitability]] can override a compelling market narrative.
