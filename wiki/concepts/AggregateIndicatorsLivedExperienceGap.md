# Aggregate Indicators Lived Experience Gap

## Definition
The aggregate-indicators/lived-experience gap arises when accurate broad measures of inflation, output, employment or markets fail to describe the price level, distribution, sector and timing experienced by particular households and workers. It is a measurement and interpretation gap, not automatic evidence of falsified statistics.

## Current Synthesis
The notes offer four complementary checks: distinguish inflation *rate* from high price *level* and housing payments; split growth and market returns across occupations and owners; test weak sentiment alongside spending and income segments; and disaggregate ambiguous trade/consumption indicators by industry. Geopolitical memoir offers only an analogy about macro narrative versus individual life, not direct evidence about economic aggregates.

## Key Claims
- Falling inflation can coexist with continued affordability pain because the accumulated price level and monthly mortgage costs remain high.
- GDP, unemployment and equity-index gains can mask entry-level job pressure, caregiving interruptions and very different sectors or asset ownership.
- Consumer sentiment supplies information about anxiety and expectations, but weak mood neither negates resilient hard data nor proves official numbers false.
- A falling import or consumption aggregate admits multiple mechanisms, including weak demand, domestic substitution and changing consumption baskets, which require sector evidence to distinguish.

## Evidence
- **Prices and missing cuts:** [[would-you-trust-an-economist-with-your-economy]] has [[DianeKPMGChiefEconomist]] respond to [[EconomistTrustCrisis|economist distrust]] by distinguishing disinflation from high price levels, housing payment strain and income catching up. GDP or jobless rates may miss care disruptions, internships and entry-level hiring; direct conversations can locate useful disaggregations without replacing data. This distinguishes [[OfficialStatisticsCredibility|accuracy]] from incomplete coverage.
- **Mood and spending:** [[indicators-of-2025-and-what-to-watch-in-2026]] has [[KennyMalone]] cite the [[UniversityOfMichigan]] [[ConsumerSentimentIndicator|sentiment index]] near the 50s in 2025 versus roughly 100 before the pandemic, alongside concern about prices, jobs, housing and uncertainty. The episode contrasts weak confidence with stronger expenditure and differing consumer-income tiers; sentiment is a complementary expectation measure, not a direct falsification test.
- **Sector and asset split:** [[146-meiguo-jingji-zheme-cha-meigu-hai-neng-jixu-zhang-ma-chuantai-meilun-meihuan-lrshkxgofprel-e-7lkiqu-en8gu]] juxtaposes technology, finance and AI-infrastructure gains with weak media, biotech, agriculture, nonprofit and novice-job conditions, ordinary food prices, stock buybacks and unequal equity ownership. [[USEconomicExperienceSplit|U.S. sector divergence]], [[EmployerPowerReassertion|labor bargaining]] and [[EquityRetirementAssetBinding|retirement exposure]] show why an index is not every household's balance sheet. These were source-time 2025 assessments, not live market readings.
- **Ambiguous China indicators:** [[133-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2025-nianzhong-pandian-zhongguo-de-sange-wencha-he-meiguo-de-sange-yuqicha-lhj-qcswiuqskvzn-f693vti6xsc]] says a fall in imports might reflect weak domestic demand, [[TechnologyInnovationAsScaleEconomy|domestic substitution]], or both. [[ChinaMacroTemperatureGaps|Consumption differences]] between quality-low-price and reasoned-premium choices, and concerts/tea drinks/[[DigitalEconomyMeasurementGap|new consumption]], may be missed by an undifferentiated measure. The episode offers interpretations, not a quantified decomposition; [[AnnualReportMacroReading|sector accounts]] would be needed to test them.
- **Adjacent analogy, not economic corroboration:** [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]] contrasts [[MacroNarrativeMicroLifeGap|Europe-wide narratives]] with [[Ukraine|Ukrainian]] workers, mixed [[Russia|Russian]]-Ukrainian families, renters and visa holders; [[EurozoneInternalImbalance|cross-country differences]] matter, but the note is principally about politics, memory and lived war, not an independent test of GDP or inflation measurement.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Sentiment measures have sampling, partisan, recall and expectation effects; sour mood and resilient spending can both be true. Political pressure on statistical institutions is a different question from whether broad measures omit subgroups.
- The 2025 U.S. and China sector descriptions are dated and interview-based. The notes do not estimate the share of China's import decline due to substitution versus weak demand, nor prove stock appreciation caused a specific household's hardship.

## What Changed
- Grouped price-level lag, distribution, sentiment and sector disaggregation as separate tests.
- Kept the European memoir as a bounded analogy rather than economic-statistics evidence.
