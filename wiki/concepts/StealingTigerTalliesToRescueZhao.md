# Stealing Tiger Tallies to Rescue Zhao / 窃符救赵

Stealing tiger tallies to rescue Zhao / 窃符救赵 is the action sequence completed in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-6-lteqe-0ykjtv01cmxv1fhx6z0vdi]]. It begins from a coalition failure: [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] has sent [[JinBi|晋鄙]] with troops, but Qin pressure keeps that army stopped at [[YeWarringStates|邺城]] while [[Handan|邯郸]] remains in danger.

The rescue depends on [[HouYing|侯嬴]]'s conversion of [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]]'s personal honor into an effective plan. Rather than let Xinlingjun and his retainers die uselessly, Hou Ying points him toward [[RuJi|如姬]], the king's half of the [[TigerTallyCommandSystem|tiger tally]], and [[ZhuHai|朱亥]] as the coercive answer if Jin Bi resists.

The action succeeds politically and militarily. Xinlingjun takes the Wei army, filters out soldiers with direct family obligations, coordinates with [[JingYang|景阳]]'s Chu force, and helps break Qin's pressure on Zhao. The episode presents the result as a restored [[HezongAlliance|合纵]] moment: Qin's [[ZhengAnping|郑安平]] surrenders with 20,000 troops, [[WangHe|王龁]] retreats, and [[HanState|韩国]] joins the recovery.

The same success carries an ethical problem. Jin Bi is not treated as a villain in the episode; he is a commander suspicious of an irregular transfer of authority. His death makes the story an example of [[MoralCostOfEffectiveRescue|成事的道德代价]], where a necessary or effective rescue action still leaves an officer loyal to duty dead.

## Key Claims
- Private heroics become consequential only when they are converted into command over real troops.
- A successful rescue can depend on violating the normal chain of authority when the ruler is paralyzed.
- The plan is multi-channel: aristocratic reputation, retainer advice, palace access, command credential, and violence all have to align.
- The episode refuses a clean heroic reading because Jin Bi's killing makes the successful action morally compromised.
