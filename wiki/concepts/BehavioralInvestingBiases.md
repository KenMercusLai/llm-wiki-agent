## Definition
Behavioral investing biases are decision distortions from reference points, fear, regret, authority, herd signals or post-hoc explanations; fraud machinery and leveraged-product mathematics are separate risks that these reactions can amplify.

## Current Synthesis
The source notes distinguish late-cycle FOMO, actual-loss aversion, socially engineered trust, narrative hindsight, product mechanics and fit between temperament and strategy. More news or AI assistance can reinforce an existing thesis without a written invalidation rule; safeguards focus on position limits, cooling-off, platform checks and consistent decision records, not claims that every loss is a cognitive error.

## Key Claims
- Loss, missed upside and paper gains create different reference points that can drive selling winners, holding losers or chasing late rallies.
- Confirmation, herd and authority shortcuts can replace due diligence with agreeable feeds, famous investors, groups or AI-fluent justifications.
- Fraudsters exploit early payouts and social proof, but fake venues and counterparty control are external mechanisms, not merely victim psychology.
- Post-hoc market narratives and recent price extrapolation can make contingent paths look inevitable; a random-market demonstration does not prove actual markets are wholly random.
- Leverage changes both payoff paths and investors' expectations: daily-reset decay is a mathematical mechanism distinct from the taste for amplified gains.
- Liquidity, action delay, sizing and worldview fit shape whether investors can actually follow a strategy across market regimes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Scam losses cannot be blamed entirely on victims: deceptive platforms, seller incentives and money routes require independent validation. Leveraged ETF path dependency is not a bias even if its fast gains intensify one. The random simulation in E144 is illustrative, not proof that real prices lack economic causes; the late-cycle 2026 portfolio and A-share 4,000-point discussion are dated, not general forecasts. - Separate mental reference points, social manipulation, mechanical product losses and personal suitability.
- Retain source-scoped market indicators and personal trading rules without presenting them as advice.
