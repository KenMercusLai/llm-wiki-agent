# Geopolitical Cycle Macro

## Definition
Geopolitical-cycle macro is an interpretive investment frame in which shifts in interstate order, energy institutions and trade constraints can alter the assumptions under which ordinary macro forecasts and portfolios are built.

## Current Synthesis
It is a hypothesis about changing boundary conditions, not a proven deterministic geopolitical cycle. A dated market commentary, an energy-institution history, a long-wave guest's model and a household-investor analogy offer different levels of evidence and application.

## Key Claims
- A war shock can jointly affect oil, inflation, interest-rate expectations, liquidity and appetite for risky assets.
- Producer coordination and shipping chokepoints can change usable energy supply independently of a simple demand forecast.
- Long-wave theories propose structural regime changes but require mechanisms and risk controls, not calendar prophecy.
- Ordinary investors may respond to uncertainty with diversified, holdable allocations rather than heroic predictions.

They connected potential [[StraitOfHormuz|Strait of Hormuz]] disruption to oil and [[StagflationRiskRepricing|stagflation worries]]; Ricky favored caution on U.S. tech and more support for A shares, while both kept a longer-term Hong Kong case. - Energy institution: Planet Money traces producer-country frustration with the Seven Sisters' price power, a 1959 Cairo-side meeting, 1973 supply-shock learning, 1982 quota rules and [[SaudiArabia|Saudi Arabia]]'s disproportionate [[SwingProducerRole|swing-producer burden]], including cheating and the 1985 market-flood response. It reports that Hormuz disruption can separate paper supply from usable delivered oil. [[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]]
- Long-wave proposal: a Mianji guest distinguishes Schumpeterian economic cycles from technology-installation phases and argues that AI may anchor a sixth Kondratiev wave while remaining part of the information revolution. In the guest's view a unipolar-to-multipolar shift changes macro boundaries; the same source warns that liquidity stress can raise asset correlations and that any narrative must be translated into position size, duration, commodities and risk budgets. - Household analogy: the vol.110 host invokes Ken Follett's 《世纪三部曲》 to argue for a global view, cash and non-all-in choices through historical turbulence; he says human earning capacity and portfolio suitability matter as much as copying a celebrated investor. Fiction offers an analogy, not empirical proof of geopolitics driving returns. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither an investor's March snapshot nor a long-wave theory establishes a universal cycle. The OPEC source's consequential claim about UAE departure has not been independently checked here and is not used to infer current membership. Reserve currencies, sanctions, AI export controls and correlations have different causal channels; gold's role as a possible hedge is conditional, not a constant safe-asset result.

## What Changed
- Anchored the macro-shock account to its recording time and separated history, long-wave interpretation and household analogy.
- Withheld the unverified OPEC-membership assertion rather than presenting it as a current fact.
