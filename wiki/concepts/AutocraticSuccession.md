## Definition
Autocratic succession concerns how authority built around a dominant ruler passes to another claimant, including control of selection, public legitimacy, rivals and the new ruler's effective command. Imperial dynasties, a theocracy and an electoral system provide different—not interchangeable—cases.

## Current Synthesis
The Qin death-on-tour story has directly conflicting source traditions: a forged edict after concealed death versus a deathbed choice of a nearby prince. The Song and Wei examples expose ambiguities in heir designation; recent Turkey and Iran podcast reporting tests electoral challengers, rituals and command balance. These are attributed historical/program interpretations, not proof that one mechanism universally explains succession.

## Key Claims
- Unclear designation can shift factional conflict into the transition, whether an heir is not named or a brother's claim competes with descendants.
- Control of the ruler's death announcement, seals and written orders can define an accession narrative, but the Qin accounts disagree over forgery versus consultation.
- Successful accession may trigger another contest over legitimacy, with new rulers purging surviving alternative claimants and officials.
- In formally electoral Turkey, term and early-election routes, candidate appeal and treatment of opposition affect succession differently from imperial hereditary rules.
- Public funeral rituals may dramatize unresolved leadership, and loss of a personal mediator may change command balance; neither alone proves how a regime will evolve.

## Evidence
- Undesignated and contested heirs: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] says [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]] did not clearly designate a successor and [[LiangHuiWang|魏罃]] fought [[GongzhongHuan|公中缓]]; the episode cautions that the chronology is displaced. [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]] describes [[ZhaoKuangyin|宋太祖]]'s sudden death, [[ZhaoGuangyi|赵光义]]'s brotherly claim, [[SongEmpress|宋皇后]]'s unsuccessful request for [[ZhaoDefang|赵德芳]], missing original [[GoldenCabinetPledge|金匮之盟]] and divergent one- versus three-transfer versions; 烛影斧声 is not proof of murder. The later fates of [[ZhaoDezhao|赵德昭]] and [[ZhaoTingmei|赵廷美]] bear on competing heir claims.
- Rival Qin accounts: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg]] narrates [[LiSi|李斯]] concealing [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s death on tour, maintaining food/business routines, controlling letter and seals with [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] and [[QinErshi|胡亥]], sending a forged order that [[FuSu|扶苏]] obeys against [[MengTian|蒙恬]]'s plea to verify, then returning through 秦直道 to [[Xianyang|咸阳]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]] instead reads [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]]'s deathbed consultation, where Li Si and [[FengQuji|冯去疾]] prefer nearby Hu Hai to waiting for distant Fusu; the note flags a difficulty placing Feng at the scene and does not settle between traditions.
- Consolidation after accession: [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-128-1-buxiang-cuanwei-de-taijian-bushi-ge-hao-jianchen-lh23xj9qxvubj5rlfa9fxmddgbiu]] describes Hu Hai in 209 BCE asking to enjoy rule and Zhao Gao invoking his insecure accession to persuade him to use harsh laws against old ministers and kin: twelve princes executed at Xianyang, ten princesses in Du County, [[YingJianglu|嬴将闾]] and brothers driven to suicide and [[YingGao|嬴高]] seeking burial with his father to spare family. [[170-songtaizu-zhisi-zhuying-fusheng-yinmoulun-zhong-de-renxing-mituan-xia-958158115]] likewise treats宋太宗's alternative heirs as a later legitimacy problem, not confirmation of a murder theory.
- Electoral incumbency: [[stock-options-how-to-hedge-an-ai-bubble-698efe7e7301331f1f674bd5]] says [[RecepTayyipErdogan|埃尔多安]]'s current term would ordinarily end in 2028 but a late-2027 snap election was discussed as a route to another run; [[HakanFidan|费丹]] and [[BilalErdogan|比拉尔]] offered contrasting experience/party and family-loyalty claims. [[brave-new-whirl-turkeys-opposition-overhaul-6a69c7766043035f89eb097f]] describes court and police pressure around [[EkremImamoglu|伊马莫卢]] and [[OzgurOzel|奥泽尔]]'s [[NewPartyTurkey|新党]], including reported 91 CHP defections and favorable polling—an episode's dated account, not a completed succession.
- Ritual and effective authority: [[the-mourning-show-the-politics-of-khameneis-funeral-6a4b75084955fc4dc12ffcc7]] reports [[AliKhamenei|阿里·哈梅内伊]]'s funeral as both Shia rite and state mobilization, with supposed successor [[MujtabaKhamenei|穆杰塔巴]] absent from public prayers. [[peace-fire-further-us-iran-strikes-6a4f7acffe878dc8e27e6fbf]] attributes to [[NicholasPelham]] the argument that after Ali's death generals faced fewer balancing checks amid renewed Iran–U.S. strikes and stalled negotiations; this is an observer's assessment, not independent proof of settled command hierarchy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The [[Shiji|《史记》]]-style forgery account and 《赵正书》's consultation version conflict directly; Feng Quji's location complicates the latter. Song palace rumors do not establish fratricide, and pledge versions change the alleged order of succession. Wei dating is uncertain. Turkey retains elections and legal constraints, unlike Qin/Song monarchy; episode-reported Iran war and funeral detail remain source-scoped. The Iran contributor's name appears in inconsistent spellings in adjacent notes; this page uses the registered note's Nicholas Pelham.

## What Changed
- Present rival Qin accession accounts side by side rather than promote either to fact.
- Distinguish initial designation, successor consolidation, electoral contest and public command display.
