# Political Venture Investment / 政治风险投资

Political venture investment / 政治风险投资 is the episode's analogy for [[LuBuwei|吕不韦]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-3-lvbuwei-lishishang-fengtou-di-yi-ren-lswa9b864oc-s8vaxtdu15arydu2]]. The source calls him "history's first venture capitalist" because he sees [[QinZhuangxiangwang|异人 / 子楚]] not as a poor hostage but as a high-upside political asset.

The investment logic has several parts. First, Yiren's current price is low: he is materially poor, socially mocked, and weak in the Qin succession field. Second, the upside is large because he is still a Qin royal grandson. Third, there is a plausible intervention path through [[HuayangFuren|华阳夫人]], whose favored but childless position creates [[MaternalStatusSuccessionLeverage|子以母贵式继承杠杆]]. Fourth, Lu Buwei must spend real resources and accept personal cost, including the episode's claim that he gives his beloved woman to Yiren.

This concept is an analogy, not a literal claim that Warring States politics had modern venture-capital institutions. Its value is explanatory: it shows how commercial optionality, asymmetric payoff, and political access can be mapped onto a succession problem.

## Key Claims
- A political actor can be treated as "mispriced" when current weakness hides future institutional leverage.
- The payoff is asymmetric: ordinary support costs can become enormous if the backed person reaches the throne.
- Succession politics creates investable optionality when maternal status, adoption, patronage, and court access can alter rank.
- The analogy should stay source-scoped because modern venture capital and Warring States succession are structurally different systems.
