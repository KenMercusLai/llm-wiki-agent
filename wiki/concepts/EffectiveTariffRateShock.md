# Effective Tariff Rate Shock

## Definition
An effective tariff-rate shock is a time-bound change in the import-duty burden as measured or estimated across trade, with separate legal, price, supply-chain and market channels.

## Current Synthesis
The year-end 2025 reported rate, the April 2025 proposed-rate estimates, and subsequent judicial limits are different snapshots, not numbers to combine. Tariffs change importers' costs and bargaining options; exemptions and rerouting mediate household effects, while other countries' export controls and leveraged asset markets can transmit the news beyond customs. The revenue and growth upside promoted by an official remains his contested policy account.

## Key Claims
- A measured effective-rate increase must be separated from a forecast tariff schedule and from later legal changes.
- Consumer pass-through depends on who imports, pays, reroutes or receives exemptions; a headline rate is not a household bill.
- Statutory authority and refund procedure can change the shock even after duties were collected.
- Allies and strategic-input suppliers can respond to tariffs, making the exposure bilateral and supply-chain-specific.
- Tariff announcements can catalyze financial liquidation, while levered positioning and liquidity determine its scale; claimed fiscal revenue is not net welfare.

## Evidence
- Rate and formula: [[indicators-of-2025-and-what-to-watch-in-2026]] has [[GregRosalsky|Greg Rosalsky]] report an effective U.S. rate of 2.5% in 2024 and 16.8% in 2025, which the episode calls the highest since 1935, a bounded rate comparison to the [[AmericanProtectionistTradition|historical protectionist tradition]], not evidence that employment or bargaining effects were the same; [[Costco|Costco]] litigation was pending in that year-end account. This tests the [[BlanketTariffLimit|limits of blanket tariffs]] as a price and legal-authority event, without proving manufacturing recovery. [[vol-123-telangpu-de-duideng-guanshui-an-buzhi-shi-yi-chang-daxing-fucongxing-ceshi-lm6bkiqgyyefwompxon7lo6cnqp6]], published April 6, 2025, describes [[TradeReciprocityProtectionism|reciprocity-framed protectionism]] through a proposed deficit-based reciprocal formula (bilateral goods surplus divided by U.S. imports, then halved) and early estimates of roughly 5%→24% weighted rate and 2.4%→25.1% in another effective-rate estimate. Those are contemporaneous forecasts on different definitions, not realized 2025 measurements. The hosts interpret the schedule as a [[TariffComplianceTest|compliance test]] for negotiating partners, not a measured compliance outcome.
- Price and trade: [[vol-128-guanshui-zhan-xiayibu-zouxiang-hefang-meiguo-renmin-ruhe-kandai-telangpu-bairi-xinzheng-kuangxi-bokejie-duihua-zhongshu-talich-ltivoqhue1t-q2ejjlzlenormkhb]]'s May 11 pre-Geneva live conversation names toys, strollers, car seats, fashion and imported brands as exposed, while predicting exemptions and [[DeglobalizationTradeIntermediation|routing via Southeast Asia, Canada or Mexico]]; the unexpectedly favorable later talks caution against its forecast. [[elbows-up-again-a-us-canada-trade-flare-up-6a8c0fa3a89d26398ee7a5db]] reports about $20 billion of new [[Canada|Canadian]] export exposure on top of aluminum, wood and vehicle tariffs, with a roughly 90,000-job risk estimate; Hal Hodson describes Mark Carney's Quebec/Alberta exposure and need for [[UnitedStates|U.S.]] trade stability before diversification. [[battlefield-rare-earths-how-the-us-lost-to-china]] reports [[China]] restricting some rare-earth exports after April 2025's [[DonaldTrump|Trump]] administration tariff move, but [[RareEarthProcessingBottleneck|processing dependence]], not simply the tariff rate, creates leverage. The note describes grants, loans and equity intended for [[StateBackedRareEarthRebuilding|state-backed rebuilding]]; it does not document completed replacement capacity.
- Law after imposition: [[the-supreme-court-struck-down-a-bunch-of-trumps-tariffs-now-what]] reports the [[SupremeCourt|Supreme Court]] rejecting broad IEPA tariffs under the power to “regulate importation,” leaving other Section 301/232/201 tools and a time-limited Section 122 route. Over $100 billion collected under invalidated duties had no automatic refund path; Wes Harrell describes a [[TariffRefundClaimsMarket|market in potential claims]] priced near 20% before versus 40% after the ruling. Kara Dyer's Storytime Toys case illustrates [[TariffPolicyPlanningRisk|order, supplier and legal-cost uncertainty]] rather than guaranteed repayment.
- Market and fiscal narratives: [[bu-xi-deng-e02-bi-quan-shan-beng-mei-guo-zheng-fu-guan-men-first-brands-po-chan-wa-ha-ha-feng-bo-lt-ghzgmiroo9a-odaih4xkhsulu]]'s hosts attribute a crypto flash crash to a sudden 100% China-tariff message but describe [[CryptocurrencyMarketStructure|leverage and thin altcoin liquidity]] as amplifiers. [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-howard-lutnick-how-america-can-hit-6-gdp-growth-in-2026-39668255]] records Commerce Secretary [[HowardLutnick|Howard Lutnick]]'s claims of roughly $500 billion annual tariff revenue rising toward $1 trillion, $550 billion Japanese [[TradeDealCapitalStructure|project-financing commitments]] and 5%–6% growth aspiration; these are his interview claims, not independently verified net fiscal or GDP effects. His [[TradeDeficitOwnershipFrame|trade-deficit ownership frame]] is a rationale for capital-structured deals, not a demonstrated deficit reduction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The 2026 ruling concerned IEPA, not all tariffs. Refund rights, timing and Section 122 replacement are distinct from measured 2025 rates; court outcome should not be backdated into April 2025 predictions.
- A Canadian tariff job estimate is prospective, not observed layoffs. Cohen-style bilateral bargaining analogies do not establish price pass-through. Rare-earth restrictions and crypto liquidations also have non-tariff causes.
- The May conversation predates Geneva results; both Qizhulou episodes share a programme. Lutnick is an advocate for the policy whose gross revenue numbers do not net out consumer, importer or legal costs.

## What Changed
- Ordered the indicator, April proposal, consumer channel and court aftermath by their actual source dates.
- Kept the administration's revenue thesis separate from measured rates and market outcomes.
