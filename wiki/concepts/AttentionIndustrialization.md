## Definition
Attention industrialization is the scaling of systems that classify, produce, rank and monetize mental intake and behavior. The analogy to industrialized food describes abundant stimulation that can be easy to consume without nourishing independent judgment; it is a source-attributed framework, not a medical diagnosis.

## Current Synthesis
[[WeiQing|韦青]] calls free algorithmic feeds “industrialized mental food”: more intake can coexist with weaker volition, and cheap personalized AI output may intensify that pattern. His argument is about [[HumanAgencyUnderAI|human agency]] and literacy, not a measured population rate of “brain obesity.” The production chain starts before a feed reaches a user. [[TarletonGillespie]]'s algorithm framework, as interpreted by [[DavidWeng|大卫翁]], distinguishes inclusion/exclusion from data, behavioral prediction, ranking or relevance, apparent objectivity, creator adaptation and model-assembled publics. [[AlgorithmicPredictionLoop|Clicks, skips and watch time]] and [[AlgorithmicRelevanceAssessment|ranking weights]] update profiles while [[AlgorithmicEntanglement|creators]] adjust titles and timing to become legible. The [[AlgorithmicCaveAllegory|cave]] and [[AlgorithmicPublicAppearance|Arendtian appearance]] are philosophical analogies about selected reality and the [[LanguagePrecision|linguistic pressure]] to show a label instead of a full person, not empirical measurements of any platform.

Distribution can redirect attention without a sealed [[InformationCocoon|bubble]]. [[HuangShengchun|黄圣淳]] describes incidental news exposure and weaker evidence for universal filter bubbles, while a source-scoped YouTube audit found recommendation chains more often moving news toward entertainment than the reverse. [[PlatformAffordance|Autoplay, infinite scroll, likes and antagonistic comments]] provide [[PlatformFeedbackLoop|feedback]]; cross-viewpoint contact can produce [[AffectivePolarization|affective polarization]] instead of understanding. In a Japan/China youth discussion, [[AlgorithmicLabeling|social labels]] can precede knowing classmates or roommates as people. In another Japan-workplace conversation, feeds showing promotions, travel, bodies and possessions can pre-consume [[AlgorithmicDesirePreemption|desire]] through [[SocialTemplateDesire|social templates]] and intensify [[LowDesireDefensiveContraction|defensive withdrawal]] alongside housing and work insecurity; it would be wrong to blame low desire wholly on algorithms.

The pressure reaches knowledge and mood. [[BookCreatorWork|Book creators]] [[QinZong|秦总]], [[LiWuya|李乌鸦]] and [[ChaoGe|超哥]] describe familiar titles and [[AlgorithmicAngerEngagement|anger winning quicker comments]] than unfamiliar reading; [[PodcastAsAsynchronousMedia|long audio]] and [[ReadingAsCognitiveRestoration|books]] can preserve context. A social-psychology episode presents the [[GilbertSpinozaHypothesis|believe-first, reject-later]] hypothesis alongside [[EmotionalContagion|emotional contagion]]: noisy, fast spectacle may leave falsehoods influential when verification needs attention. An episode on forgetting distinguishes genuinely [[FocusedDiffuseThinkingBalance|restorative diffuse thought]] and [[ForgettingAsCognitiveFunction|healthy forgetting]] from constant scrolling; a personal midlife account from David Weng treats frequent phone pickups, roughly eight hours' use and answer-first AI as [[AutonomyUnderInformationFlow|autonomy concerns]] rather than clinical findings. [[LengJianguo|冷建国]] says fragmented attention rather than clock time is the reading problem, so [[BedtimeReadingRitual|sleep and long-form books]] can supply chosen [[AIUsePacing|pacing]] without requiring print purism.

Countermeasures work at several levels. [[LiJigang|李继刚]] ties [[WetStateHumanAgency|human intention and embodied life]] to input choice: he limits WeChat contacts, public accounts and RSS input to raise signal density while keeping human value choice and [[AILiteracyAgainstWorship|AI literacy]]; this is [[FeedCuration|feed curation]], not abandoning tools. To counter [[AddictiveInteractionDesign|engagement design]], [[DigitalDetoxEconomy|digital-detox products]] such as [[Clearspace]] and physical blockers impose [[ScreenTimeFriction|friction]] through prompts, lockboxes or even a proposed six-pound case, but [[TanyaSujohn]] notes that streaks, dashboards and social features can imitate the same engagement design. The source cites an average U.S. phone-use estimate of five hours sixteen minutes daily and a possible nearly $20 billion digital-detox industry by 2032; both are source-scoped forecasts or survey figures, not facts of universal exposure. Public or educational AI and value-aligned personal agents should be judged by their incentives and user control rather than grouped with every commercial feed.

## Key Claims
- Classification, prediction and ranking determine what is available and salient before a user actively chooses it; [[AlgorithmicMediaLiteracy|media literacy]] asks whose standards drove that selection; feedback also trains creators and users.
- Abundant, low-cost content can capture attention or shape desire and social labels, but total bubbles and uniform manipulation are not established.
- News-to-entertainment drift, outrage engagement and fast spectacle can reshape public judgment without requiring explicit censorship.
- AI-generated answers and constant phone access may externalize thinking; long-form reading, genuine diffuse rest and deliberate feed limits protect different parts of autonomy.
- Digital-detox products can add friction but may reproduce metrics and engagement cues from the systems they counter.
- Platform design and user agency coexist; neither individual willpower nor one tool fully solves the industrial incentive problem.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The YouTube audit is not a finding about every platform. Multi-platform use and [[IncidentalExposure|incidental exposure]] complicate strong cocoon claims, and some marginalized creators gain visibility from recommendations even while labels flatten them. The 167 philosophical reading is explicitly analogical. Social-psychology and popular-neuroscience stories are source-specific explanations, not universal clinical effects; healthy forgetting and diffuse attention should not be equated with distraction. AI may aid research or reading when users preserve direction and judgment. Digital-detox market and screen-use numbers are episode-cited, not independently audited here.
