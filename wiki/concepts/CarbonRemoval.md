# Carbon Removal

## Definition
Carbon removal extracts already-emitted atmospheric carbon dioxide and stores it for a meaningful duration; it differs both from preventing new emissions and from reflecting sunlight.

## Current Synthesis
Direct-air limestone cycling, biomass-to-bio-oil injection and ocean alkalinity have different capture, storage and measurement chains. Early plants, contracted purchases and field trials show activity but not cheap, verified climate-scale net removal. Mitigation still requires emissions cuts.

## Key Claims
- Removal changes atmospheric greenhouse-gas stock, unlike emissions avoidance or albedo modification.
- Direct air capture has an operating example, but its present cost and scale differ sharply from targets and plans.
- Biomass conversion and underground injection move the constraint toward feedstocks, transport, geological permanence and permitting.
- Ocean alkalinity projects require field chemistry and lifecycle/ecological verification before a company estimate becomes demonstrated net storage.
- Early corporate purchases and selective public support can finance learning while also risking offset moral hazard.

## Evidence
- **Stock versus reflectivity:** [[OliverMorton]] separates removing accumulated CO2 from [[SolarGeoengineering]]'s proposed sulphate-based reflectivity intervention; the latter responds to [[EarthEnergyImbalance]] but does not take carbon out of the atmosphere. [[taking-the-shine-off-albedo-and-global-temperatures-6a730fdaca067b295bab9760]]
- **Limestone loop:** [[HeirloomCarbonTechnologies]] heats crushed limestone to release CO2 and lets the remaining calcium oxide absorb ambient CO2 again. Company representative [[ChristianToya]] describes the Tracy, California facility at about 1,000 tons/year; Louisiana up to 200,000 tons/year was planned with uncertain timing when reporter [[AmyScott]] visited. Current high-hundreds-per-ton cost is distinct from a roughly $100-per-ton aspiration. [[tech-20260713-tech-pod-128-tech-20260713-tech-pod-128]]
- **Bio-oil pathway:** [[PeterReinhardt]] describes [[CharmIndustrial]] making bio-oil from biomass such as wildfire residue and injecting it underground. He attributes 200,000–300,000 contracted tons and an approximately $50m [[FrontierCarbonRemoval|Frontier]] commitment to early demand, while reporting roughly four years to settle a permitting pathway and fourteen months for its first Louisiana permit. These are founder-reported figures from a structured export. [[peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted-peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted]]
- **Ocean test:** Near Duck, North Carolina, [[Vesta]] placed more than 8,000 metric tons of olivine on the seabed to test conversion of CO2 to bicarbonate. Its roughly 5,000-ton net removal figure is a company estimate; ocean sampling, speed, scalability and marine-life effects are unresolved. [[tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260728-0728-mp-tech-pod-128]]
- **Financing and politics:** [[Carbon180]] representative [[AaronBurns]] distinguishes Congress-protected Louisiana/Texas hubs from other U.S. funding cuts; named corporate DAC buyers coexist with concerns that fossil-fuel offsets prolong extraction. Heirloom says it does not take fossil-fuel investment. [[tech-20260713-tech-pod-128-tech-20260713-tech-pod-128]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Purchased tonnage, planned facilities and projected ocean bicarbonate are not verified atmospheric drawdown or permanent net sequestration. Heat/energy, biomass sourcing, transport and ocean chemistry matter in lifecycle accounts. Carbon removal complements rather than substitutes for emissions reductions; solar geoengineering is a separate contested intervention.

## What Changed
- Separated three physical pathways and their verification boundaries instead of treating category expansion as evidence of proven removal.
