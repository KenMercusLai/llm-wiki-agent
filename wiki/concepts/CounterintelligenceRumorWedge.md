## Definition
A counterintelligence rumor wedge inserts a plausible but damaging story into an opponent's existing trust fracture so that its ruler or court discards useful people or changes strategy.

## Current Synthesis
The episodes describe several distinct rumor forms rather than one continuous operation. [[YanZhaowang|Yan Zhaowang]] publicly defended [[LeYi|Le Yi]] against earlier slander; after succession [[YanHuiwang|Yan Huiwang]]'s prior hostility let [[TianDan|Tian Dan]]'s later claim that Le Yi wanted to rule Qi gain traction. At [[ChangpingBattle|Changping]], [[ZhaoDan|Zhao Dan]]'s impatience with defensive [[LianPo|Lian Po]] let Qin praise [[ZhaoKuo|Zhao Kuo]] as the commander it supposedly feared. After [[Xinlingjun|Xinlingjun]] checked Qin, insinuating congratulations activated [[WeiAnxiWang|Wei Anxi Wang]]'s fear of a rival king. Other episodes contrast bribed Zhao-court reporting, Li Si's alleged discrediting of envoy [[YaoJia|Yao Jia]], and Chen Ping's funded Chu-Han intrigue. Each case needs its own prior vulnerability, carrier, target decision, and outcome; Tian Dan's later atrocity bait and Guo Kai's corrupted personnel report are adjacent but not identical wedges.

## Key Claims
- A wedge succeeds through a prior suspicion or reward grievance, not simply repetition of a false statement.
- Patron succession can reverse a ruler's protection of a capable commander, as the Yan Zhaowang/Yan Huiwang contrast shows.
- Enemy praise of an inexperienced replacement or innuendo about a victor's ambition can induce removal without direct battlefield defeat.
- Bribed court intermediaries can redirect commander and envoy decisions, especially when disaster or political insecurity makes suspicion plausible.
- Agents can use money, staged hospitality and the target's own messenger to carry an insinuation against a trusted faction.
- Rumor bait that induces atrocities or an internal envoy's false report shares an information pathway but is not automatically an enemy-run commander wedge.

## Evidence
- Succession and trust: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-78-qiguo-mingyun-jing-bei-chengguan-gei-jiule-lpt3reyr6ok8g2lhcxrnd5qctyug]] contrasts Yan Zhaowang's public defense of Le Yi with Yan Huiwang's inherited resentment. The latter believes Tian Dan's self-rule rumor and sends [[QiJie|Qi Jie]] to replace Le Yi at [[JimoWarringStates|Jimo]], interrupting Yan's non-storming occupation strategy. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]] follows with a *different* operation: baiting Qi Jie into mutilating captives and desecrating graves, provoking [[AtrocityBackfireMobilization|defender mobilization]] before false surrender and the [[FireOxNightRaid|fire-ox night raid]]. The night raid, not the first rumor alone, defeats Yan.
- Commander choice under pressure: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-94-shashen-baiqi-changping-zhi-zhan-zhong-de-caobao-mingjiang-1-loxyybm3njrtnudydafwzu9jp9jz]] reports [[FanJu|Fan Ju]] paying agents to tell the [[ZhaoState|Zhao]] court that [[QinState|Qin]] did not fear Lian Po and feared Zhao Kuo instead; Zhao Dan replaced the defensive veteran despite objections, while Qin secretly installed Bai Qi. The [[PaperWarCommandTrap|untested commander's competence problem]] is distinct from the rumor's effect on selection; this note stops before the battle's later outcome. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-2-simaqiande-idol-weihe-siyu-jiuse-lvmqg1ojmka9pqagfhgvo4albpjx]] instead describes Qin money and spies recruiting [[JinBi|Jin Bi]]'s former retainers, then staged congratulations that recast Xinlingjun's coalition victory as usurpation risk. Wei Anxi Wang removed him from command; the episode reads his subsequent withdrawal and final four years of drinking as political frustration, not a direct battlefield defeat.
- Court channel and diplomacy: In the 229 BCE Zhao campaign, [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-113-limu-ruhe-yong-yiji-zhili-zu-qinguo-tongyi-luymqpcolsvyz5miodit3wdlx0ob]] names [[DunRuo|Dun Ruo]]'s disaster-era panic and ominous-sign work, then separately Qin's bribery of [[GuoKai|Guo Kai]] to tell [[ZhaoYoumiuWang|Zhao Youmiu Wang]] that [[LiMu|Li Mu]] and [[SimaShang|Sima Shang]] plotted rebellion. Zhao replaced both; Sima Shang yielded, while Li Mu refused and was arrested or killed. In the episode's [[Zhanguoce|Zhanguoce]] reading, [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] attributes to [[LiSi|Li Si]] a rumor of Yao Jia's alleged theft and enrichment; Zhao expelled the envoy, whom Qin then sponsored on a multi-year anti-coalition mission. Earlier, [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-104-yinmou-shisuan-rang-qinguo-bantu-you-you-you-pengzhang-le-lgnyhbduqutegtksrjwcvhmn8-t6]] describes Guo Kai bribing *Zhao's own* envoy to misreport Lian Po's fitness to [[ZhaoDaoxiangwang|Zhao Daoxiang Wang]] after Lian Po had already been replaced and fled. It is not the 229 BCE Qin-run Li Mu operation.
- Reward fracture and messenger: During the Xingyang siege, [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-1-lujcothn8aunydmznta1tkglqt-q]] says [[LiuBang|Liu Bang]] gave [[ChenPing|Chen Ping]] forty thousand gold without an expense audit to target the small trusted Chu core: [[FanZeng|Fan Zeng]], [[ZhongliMo|Zhongli Mo]], [[LongJuChuGeneral|Long Ju]] and [[ZhouYin|Zhou Yin]]. Its first named rumor claimed Zhongli Mo's unrecognized merit and lack of kingship might draw Chu generals to Han; it reports weakened [[XiangYu|Xiang Yu]]–Zhongli Mo trust, not all four men defecting. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-2-lmjiydb2weahtauisouw5mdiwtoo]] describes the staged grand meal followed by a slight when Chen Ping learns the visitor is Xiang Yu's envoy rather than Fan Zeng's. The envoy's own report makes Fan Zeng's separate channel look plausible; in the host's account Xiang Yu resists Fan Zeng's renewed attack advice and the adviser asks to retire. [[XiangBo|Xiang Bo]]'s earlier conflict with Fan Zeng over Liu Bang at Hongmen illustrates the pre-existing factional crack, not his participation in Chen Ping's reception.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
All nine notes are episodes of one historical commentary series, not independent corroboration. The host notes skepticism about the Fan Zeng envoy-feast's historicity; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-1-lujcothn8aunydmznta1tkglqt-q]] alone omits the middle causal chain, supplied in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-160-chenping-gongxinji-bi-zhenhuanchuan-jingcai-2-lmjiydb2weahtauisouw5mdiwtoo]] as the podcast's account. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]]'s Yao Jia story is the episode's reading of Zhanguoce; [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-104-yinmou-shisuan-rang-qinguo-bantu-you-you-you-pengzhang-le-lgnyhbduqutegtksrjwcvhmn8-t6]] is internal sabotage, not a confirmed Qin rumor campaign. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p]]'s provoked violence also required Tian Dan's deception and night attack. Removing a commander may have several causes; Qin's own secret promotion of Bai Qi illustrates that replacement is not intrinsically bad.

## What Changed
- Organized cases by prior vulnerability, transmission channel and target decision.
- Distinguished classic enemy wedges from adjacent atrocity bait and corrupted internal reporting.
