# AI Capex Return Window

## Definition
The AI capex return window is the interval in which spending on data centers, chips and compute must produce sufficiently legible business returns to sustain investors’ willingness to finance it. It is an investor test, not a universal engineering payback period.

## Current Synthesis
The sources disagree about how quickly demand will mature: [[AaronWhatsNext]] argues for a one-to-three-year public-market test, while [[ZhangKun]] cautions that present compute scarcity may reflect a low installed base rather than a permanent annual spending trajectory. Earnings, installed capacity and credit conditions must be read together, company by company.

## Key Claims
- Spending becomes investable evidence only when installed capacity converts to attributable revenue, savings or strategic control.
- Physical delivery and scarce capacity can justify early spending without proving enduring demand.
- Debt financing tightens the test through interest costs and bond-market appetite, beyond equity patience.
- Quarterly market confirmation is provisional; cash flow and the next spending cycle can reverse investor judgment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The January and July 2026 outlooks are dated investment opinions, not live forecasts; earnings reactions are the episode’s report, not an independent audit.
- Financing claims distinguish corporate bonds from project-level private credit; a higher capex number alone neither proves a bubble nor a profitable buildout.

## What Changed
- The return-window frame now separates capacity delivery, revenue visibility and financing cost instead of treating capex volume as the result.
- Later 2026 earnings and manager views qualify, rather than retroactively confirm, the earlier one-to-three-year thesis.
