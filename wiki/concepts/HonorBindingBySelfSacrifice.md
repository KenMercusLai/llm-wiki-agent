# Honor Binding By Self-Sacrifice / 以死绑定的侠义压力

Honor binding by self-sacrifice / 以死绑定的侠义压力 is the mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-114-2-yanguo-cisha-qinshihuang-weihe-hui-xuan-jingke-lsewjrmgup1yanf8ipn-i0ykqwjx]] extracts from [[TianGuang|田光]]'s suicide. After [[YanTaiziDan|燕太子丹]] asks for secrecy, Tian Guang presents the request as a slight against his trustworthiness and kills himself.

The source reads the act as more than personal shame. Tian Guang's death seals the secret, guarantees the seriousness of the plan, and morally pressures [[JingKe|荆轲]] to accept the mission because Tian Guang has already recommended him as a义士. The suicide therefore binds another person's future action by making withdrawal look like failure to live up to an honor identity.

This concept is adjacent to [[RetainerReciprocityEthic|士为知己者死]] and [[AristocraticHonorOverLife|贵族名誉高于生命]], but its focus is narrower. It is not just dying for one's own patron or family name; it is using one's death to create an obligation for a friend within a political assassination plot.

## Key Claims
- Self-sacrifice can operate as a public or semi-public guarantee of secrecy and seriousness.
- An honor death can pressure survivors by redefining what their identity now requires.
- The mechanism depends on a shared moral vocabulary of侠义; without that vocabulary, the act would not bind another person in the same way.
- The source treats the mechanism as effective but morally uneasy, because the friend being bound has not freely designed the mission himself.
