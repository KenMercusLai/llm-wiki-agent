# Chronicle Chronology Drift / 编年错位

## Definition
Chronicle chronology drift / 编年错位 is the problem of a preserved historical notice placed in a doubtful year, reign, office sequence, or territorial setting; preserving the account is not the same as accepting its date.

## Current Synthesis
The [[RuiqiJiangtouZizhiTongjian|芮淇]] episodes compare [[SimaGuang|司马光]]’s [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]], [[Shiji|《史记》]] and sometimes [[Zhanguoce|《战国策》]] to flag unstable early Warring States dates and one Han office promotion. Their alternative dates and biographies are host/source judgments, not a newly established master timeline. A blank year is instead [[ChronicleSourceGaps|a source gap]].

## Key Claims
- Sparse early annals can preserve useful events inside erroneous succession or ruler-year placement.
- Biographical victory duration and later explanatory supplements must be checked against the annalistic sequel.
- Repeated Qi succession notices show how ruler identity, accession and death dates can drift together.
- Zhang Yi's Wei strategy survives several narrative layers that cannot all fit the same ruler-year frame.
- Office and land-ownership notices also drift; a plausible event cannot be dated before its territorial or personnel prerequisites.

## Evidence
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-3-lsbx8l7wxeioae2lwuwkkkutpdwn]] flags the 387 BCE notice of [[WeiWenhou|魏文侯]]'s death and [[WeiWuhou|魏武侯]]'s accession as too late, though the dialogue with [[WuQi|吴起]] remains useful for [[VirtueOverNaturalBarriers|virtue over terrain]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-2-lmnqfjmydygqnfec6xhzpixralvi]] questions Wei Wuhou's succession dispute placement and [[QiWeiwang|齐威王]]'s visit under [[ZhouLiewang|周烈王]] year six after the [[ZhouAnWang|King An]] annals; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-14-qiweiwang-buming-zeyi-yiming-jingren-lrs6rbifyiqrojxg0mccpg7sf9rv]] argues a 370 BCE visit cannot be literally to Zhou Lie Wang and calls the received accession about twenty-one years too early and proposes Qi Wei Wang acceding in 357 BCE, first year 356. These are the host's corrections, not independently settled dates.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] distinguishes [[TianHe|田和]], [[TianYan|田衍]], [[TianQiHuanGong|田武]], and [[GongziXiQi|公子喜]] in a disputed Qi succession instead of conflating names. Its source note explicitly keeps the earlier [[TianYingqi|田英齐]] branch source-scoped rather than resolving the competing genealogy. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-29-suqin-lizhi-shuangwen-zhishi-gaibian-mingyun-lrf5cqvoriyep2lkbozju-lyjaxv]] doubts the chronicle's 333 BCE Qi Wei death / [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] accession and simultaneous [[SuQin|苏秦]] six-state triumph; it proposes Qi Xuan's first year as 319 BCE. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-37-suqin-liuguo-jituan-ceo-de-bukan-qingshi-fj2kdqlaxzyy2nqtcutktuwrq5fo]] still uses a 324 BCE Qi Xuan appointment of Su Qin for [[StrategicLuxurySabotage|luxury inducement]], but the chronology stays marked unstable.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-33-lishi-diyi-shuangwen-nanzhujiao-suqin-lvebjyhvzctjgaevwwoddpvg9nr0]] records [[Shiji|《史记》]]'s claim that Su Qin kept Qin behind [[HanguPass|函谷关]] for fifteen years; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-34-qinguo-zongli-ruhe-wanzhuan-liuguo-lsenzwp0kr5wscb0btvuzmkibriq]] traces renewed [[QinState|Qin]] action in 332–328 BCE, [[GongsunYan|公孙衍]]'s alliance disruption and [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]]'s [[PuyangWarringStates|蒲阳]]-for-[[Shangjun|上郡]] exchange. The latter challenges the literal duration, not the existence of a coalition story.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-38-gao-jianzhi-de-zhangyi-bu-yiban-lobhcpz679psy54k0iduccqkfcrn]] embeds a post-[[YingSi|秦惠文王]] [[QinWuwang|秦武王]] and [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] supplement in a c.322 BCE [[ZhouXianwang|King Xian]] frame about Zhang Yi in Wei, including [[FengXiWarringStates|Feng Xi]] as diplomatic intermediary; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-39-lishishang-zhangyi-shi-zenyang-nudui-qiangdi-de-lv2gc2wtcxuhugfgixk7rbbhpa6x]] instead keeps Ying Si as active patron with [[GraceDebtLoyaltyPressure|reward-based loyalty pressure]] in that adjacent year. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]] narrates the Qi attack, diplomatic disclosure and Wei exit after Ying Si's death in 310 BCE, naming [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] and [[WeiXiangwang|魏襄王]]: the later continuation gives a cleaner setting, while Zhang Yi’s [[SelfPreservationAsStateStrategy|self-preservation argument]] and the [[YinmouYangmouConversion|disclosure mechanism]] remain source-scoped.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-49-mengzi-yue-wo-jiao-ni-zhiguo-ni-que-ba-wo-dang-huaping-lhy14pbf9thcfdiaulcmf8dwcef8]] calls the [[QiXuanWang|齐宣王]] death / [[QiMinWang|齐湣王]] accession notice about twelve years early; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-61-shui-shi-lishishang-daner-zui-fei-de-diwang-lvpoaszanascnlrmu2atinlxzmk1]] places it in 301 BCE and disputes immediate succession of [[LouHuan|楼缓]] to [[Chulizi|樗里疾]], preferring the [[WeiRan|魏冉]]–[[Mengchangjun|孟尝君]]–Lou Huan–Wei Ran sequence in 300–295 BCE. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-67-zhanguo-mieba-baiqi-de-junshi-caineng-jiujing-ruhe-ljw0lltowxd4qz75v-wcnlkwqskv]] distinguishes Chu- and Han-held portions of [[YuanjunWarringStates|垣郡]] in 292/291 BCE and says [[Dingtao|陶]] was then tied to [[SongState|Song]]/Qi, not yet Qin-held for Wei Ran's purported fief.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-2-lutkapdijrgpwfrz3saaowxtk-i2]] contrasts [[XiaoHe|Xiao He]]'s early xiangguo promotion in the chronicle with the host's preference for the [[Shiji|《史记》]] biography's later Han eleventh-year setting after [[HanXin|韩信]]'s death. This shows drift need not involve Warring States rulers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- All fifteen notes derive from one podcast series; variant-source comparisons are relayed by the host. Do not silently correct all cross-linked pages or treat the proposed year as independently proven. A story's strategic mechanism may remain useful while its dramatic timing or cast is disputed.

## What Changed
- The initial Qi visit warning expanded into ruler, diplomatic, office-title and fief prerequisites; Zhouji 55 provides a later frame for a previously imported Zhang Yi supplement.
