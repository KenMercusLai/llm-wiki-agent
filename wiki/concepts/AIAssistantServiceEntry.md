# AI Assistant Service Entry

## Definition
AI assistant service entry is the attempt to make an assistant the first interface for booking, buying, scheduling, finding or completing work, rather than a separate question-answer box. A recommendation is not fulfilled until identity, permissions, payment, service quality and recourse work.

## Current Synthesis
[[AITranslation]] and [[MundaneAIUseCases]] show smaller, observed assistant tasks short of commerce. The competition has distinct routes: vertically owned commerce ecosystems, enterprise work contexts, phone/OS distribution, and body-worn ambient interfaces. Their reach does not prove users have delegated consequential actions or that recommendations are neutral.

The Apple route depends on [[Apple]]’s system privileges and [[GoogleCalendar]]-like account actions, and [[Google]]’s model and services participate through Gemini; [[MetaAI]] instead relies on a different social-app and glasses distribution path. [[Qwen]] is Alibaba’s proposed assistant front door, not evidence that all [[AITravelCommerceTrust]] problems are resolved; [[Tencent]] faces a different internal ownership problem and [[ByteDance]] tests a reported commerce funnel. [[AgentFacingInterfaces]] such as [[ModelContextProtocol]] require [[AgentIdentityAndAuthentication]] and [[AgentPermissionBoundaries]] before purchases become safe.

## Key Claims
- Owning callable services and transaction recourse matters more for commerce fulfillment than generic chatbot traffic.
- Office, OS and wearable channels each supply context and distribution but carry different permission and adoption constraints.
- Ranking and commissions can turn an assistant's simplified choice interface into a conflict between user fit and monetization.
- Low-stakes actions and human confirmation provide a narrower observed foothold than unconstrained autonomous commerce.
- Product ownership and privacy boundaries can block a shared model from becoming a unified entry point.

## Evidence
- **Commerce is a service chain.** [[ep117-doubao-yuehuo-guoyi-ali-zaizao-qianwen-shibushi-wanle-lmp0pzdig2ijow5k3cnnnvvqq6sa]]'s Alibaba/Qwen discussion connects [[Taobao]], [[Fliggy]], [[Damai]], [[Gaode]] and [[DingTalk]] to travel, tickets, maps and shopping; hosts cite over 100m [[Doubao]] monthly users and past acquisition costs of roughly RMB45–65 against under RMB3 monthly revenue per user, all source-reported figures. [[Alibaba]]'s services supply potential fulfillment, but plans are not completed transactions. [[dang-kekaode-daima-biancheng-le-ou-er-fafeng-de-openclaw-women-weilai-de-gongzuo-fanshi-bianqian]]'s speculative [[Meituan]] MCP-style milk-tea ordering shows how [[AgenticCommerce]] could route demand while hiding most merchants and options; it is a thought experiment, not proof of deployment.
- **Who owns work context?** [[270-da-chang-yazhu-ai-bangong-feishu-he-dingding-que-xian-chengle-peijue-lmb4dgcgov3mr4cn7cikbghpfro4]] says [[Feishu]], DingTalk, [[DoubaoEnterpriseEdition]] and [[TencentWorkBody]] may be stronger office task entrances than generic chat because documents, approvals and enterprise knowledge create clearer paid outcomes. [[yao-shunyu-laidao-tengxun-300tian-1-176-1]] describes [[Yuanbao]] and [[TencentWorkBuddy]] as product feedback loops for [[TencentHunyuan]], but [[WeChatVLM]] remains separate for [[WeChat]] privacy, data and scale reasons. The internal organizational split limits any claim of seamless cross-product action.
- **System and physical interfaces.** [[tech-20260116-0116-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260116-0116-mp-tech-pod-128]] reports Apple's [[Gemini]] partnership for future [[Siri]] capabilities; [[tech-20260202-0202-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260202-0202-mp-tech-pod-128]]'s [[ChristopherMims]] cites a [[GooglePersonalIntelligence]] calendar entry as a small, reviewable service action. [[wwdc-26-bu-shang-le-ai-dan-li-zhenzheng-de-ai-zhushou-hai-cha-shenme-s10e15-9ab1512e-a4a8-4ea6-81b5-0ac7ec677d2d]]'s [[DongHongguang]] of [[GuangfanTechnology]] proposes earbuds/watches over phones for all-day context but says WWDC 26 was catch-up, not reliable multi-step Siri fulfillment; fine-grained permissions are unresolved. [[tech-20251225-1225-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20251225-1225-mp-tech-pod-128]]'s [[WillGottsagen]] finds [[Meta]] glasses promising for recognition/translation yet cloud connectivity, camera privacy and social awkwardness remain. [[tech-20260219-0219-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260219-0219-mp-tech-pod-128]]'s [[MikeIsaac]] frames [[MarkZuckerberg]]'s [[PersonalSuperintelligence]] and [[RayBanSmartGlasses]] as a Meta distribution bet, not proven replacement for a phone or map app.
- **Monetization tests neutrality.** [[vol-172-codex-mai-zhongzhi-taocan-deepseek-fenggu-tiaojia-pingguo-chonghui-5-wanyi-deng-1-6685-1]] describes a roughly 12% combined fee in a [[Doubao]] hotel-routing example and explicitly asks whether commission eligibility displaces user-best recommendations. It is an episode report, not an audited platform-wide rate; [[AISubscriptionEconomics]] and [[ProductLedWillingnessToPay]] remain open questions. Transparent ranking, order confirmation and refund/support boundaries are not solved by model capability; [[AIProductFragmentation]] can persist even when entry traffic consolidates.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Examples span proposals, small calendar actions, product announcements and reported commerce funnels. Do not infer generalized autonomous booking or device adoption from them. Source dates and platform terms matter.
- Ambient sensing creates bystander privacy and consent issues; local agents with account access also amplify prompt-injection and payment risk. The economic case for broad consumer assistants differs from paid enterprise workflows.

## What Changed
- Search, fulfillment, office, OS and wearable paths are separated by actual action depth; commission conflict is made explicit.
