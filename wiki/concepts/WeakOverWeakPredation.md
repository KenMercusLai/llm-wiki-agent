# Weak-Over-Weak Predation / 弱者向更弱者转嫁压迫

Weak-over-weak predation / 弱者向更弱者转嫁压迫 is the episode's pattern for a state or ruler who is vulnerable upward but coercive downward. In [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-100-1-huangdi-zhong-de-chaochang-daiji-wang-guoshi-le-lt75scavlm8qjn6ulpd-b2kwzwjf]], [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] must accommodate [[QinState|秦国]] after repeated western pressure, yet he kills [[WeyHuaijun|卫怀君]] and installs [[WeyYuanjun|卫元君]] over [[WeyState|卫国]].

The concept explains why "weak" is relational rather than absolute. [[WeiState|魏国]] is weak before Qin, but not weak before Wey. Pressure from above does not automatically produce solidarity among weaker actors; it can produce compensatory domination, in which a middle-ranked state converts humiliation into violence against a smaller neighbor.

## Key Claims
- Weakness at one level of hierarchy can coexist with arbitrary power at another level.
- Subordination to a stronger state may intensify, rather than soften, pressure on a weaker client.
- The pattern gives [[WeakStateStatusDemotion|弱国名分降格]] a coercive sequel: reduced title and autonomy can become exposure to succession interference or ruler killing.
- The episode uses Wei's behavior to show how Warring States hierarchy transmits violence downward.
