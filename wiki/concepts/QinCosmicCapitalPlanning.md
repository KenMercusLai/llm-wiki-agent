[[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|秦代宇宙化都城规划]] is the synthesis introduced by [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p|Qinji 121]] for Qin Shi Huang's Xianyang-area construction after his first post-unification journey. The episode links [[QinJimiao]], likely [[Taiyi]] worship, the Wei River as an earthly counterpart of the Milky Way, roads to [[Lishan]], covered passages to [[Xianyang]], and Ganquan-front-hall geography into one symbolic capital layout.

The concept marks a shift from winning the world to staging the world. Qin political order is no longer expressed only by conquest, law, measures, and script; it is also built into ritual architecture, road alignments, and a capital landscape that imitates celestial order.

[[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-122-1-jiemi-qinshihuang-fengchan-weishenme-xuan-taishan-lkcyayuqrtsgy-k4bhdvy-l-lapo]] extends the same logic beyond the capital region. The source's [[Taishan|泰山]] and [[LiangfuMountain|梁父山]] ceremony is not Xianyang planning, but it belongs beside this concept because Qin Shi Huang again makes rule visible through cosmic geography, prepared routes, inscription, and communication with Heaven. The difference is spatial: Qinji 121 centers the capital landscape, while Qinji 122-1 carries sacred staging into conquered eastern territory.

## Key Claims
- [[QinJimiao]] is read as an earthly ritual center corresponding to the heavenly pole or supreme celestial order.
- [[Taiyi]] is the likely deity behind this reading, though the source treats the identification as an inference.
- The Wei River/Milky Way analogy turns natural geography around [[Xianyang]] into imperial cosmology.
- Roads to [[Lishan]] and passages to Xianyang connect practical movement with symbolic mapping.
- The concept extends [[ImperialSymbolicStandardization]] and [[QinAdministrativeStandardization]] into built space.
- Qinji 122-1 extends the pattern outward: sacred geography in the east becomes another site where Qin stages cosmic legitimacy.
