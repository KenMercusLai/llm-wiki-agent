## Definition
Culinary grammar is a metaphor for the sensory, social, production and ethical rules through which a community makes food intelligible; it is learned, not a universal natural law.

## Current Synthesis
The sources move from encountering unfamiliar mouthfeel and table practices to learning beverage vocabulary, recognizing regional innovation and tracing species, processing and welfare behind a menu description. Context can delay snap judgment without obliging approval of harm or turning taste into a status test.

## Key Claims
- Embodied repetition and knowledge of local markets can reveal why unfamiliar textures and freshness norms matter.
- Tasting vocabulary and comparison help people articulate preference rather than perform expertise.
- Creative restaurants can change form and technique while preserving recognizable regional flavor logic.
- Species labels, feed, seed supply, processing and animal welfare form a production grammar not apparent from taste alone.

## Evidence
- Encounter and ethics: [[66-yuchi-yu-huajiao-liao-chi-de-wo-ke-jiu-bu-kun-le-659957536]] reads [[FuchsiaDunlop|Fuchsia Dunlop]]'s [[SharkFinAndSichuanPepper|《鱼翅与花椒》]] through Chengdu cooks and live markets: rabbit heads, offal, duck intestine, sea cucumber and [[FoodTextureAesthetics|texture]] unsettle a supermarket-centric observer. [[FoodCulturalBias]] runs both ways when Chinese food is dismissed as cheap takeout or Western food as bland and raw. The episode rejects rare-animal waste and suffering rather than excusing them as cultural difference.
- Sensory learning: [[sanlian-chuantai-shen-ci-gei-ren-putao-shijie-weixun-le-585125156]] has [[Heimai|黑麦]] treat [[Wine|wine]] as agricultural and human [[WineTerroir|terroir]]—grape, climate, fermentation, family and market fashion. Comparing sweetness, acidity, aroma and body with tea, [[Coffee|coffee]] or perfume supports [[TasteTraining]] and [[FlavorAsSelfKnowledge]], not a hierarchy of worthy drinkers; natural wine can lower barriers while still tasting odd or volatile.
- Regional variation: [[ep245-yinian-yidu-xunwei-zhilv-jinnian-qu-chi-chuangyi-cai-li9wtavemokcckialbbj7unpmqsz]] traces [[GuizhouCuisineFlavorTransfer|Guizhou]] fermented sour, chilies and 木姜子; [[YunnanCuisineMenuNarrative|Yunnan]] landscapes and fermentation; [[SichuanCuisine|Sichuan]] soup, brining and heat-control renewal; and [[GuangxiCuisineLegibility|Guangxi]] beyond luosifen through 老友鱼 and 柠檬鸭. [[ChineseBistroLocalization|Bistro formats]], stir-fry revival and local wine, rice wine or tea pairings alter [[CreativeChineseCuisine|creative cuisine]] presentation without guaranteeing every novelty is legible.
- Production truth: [[ep260-yikou-feimei-zhifang-cangzhe-zhongguo-yangzhi-de-xin-gushi-linjq2fkzhqvzjcgrmhudq12vs4j]] asks a diner to distinguish imported Atlantic salmon from domestic [[RainbowTrout|rainbow trout]] in the “三文鱼” label, to understand wild-caught [[Eel|eel]] seedlings and their price risk plus killing, deboning, saucing and freezing, and to evaluate [[FoieGras|foie gras]] force-feeding alongside worker, farmer and disease constraints. [[PreparedHighValueFoodFormats|Smoked fish, grilled eel and small-pack prepared formats]] are processing knowledge, not automatically inferior shortcuts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are cultural conversations and regional reports, not controlled taste studies. Guangxi's “1.0” description is a speaker's restaurant observation. Reduced brutality or mortality in foie-gras production does not settle [[FoodAnimalWelfare|animal-welfare]] objections. Transparent trout labeling is more defensible than treating salmonid names as interchangeable.

## What Changed
- Linked cultural interpretation to concrete supply-chain legibility without treating them as identical.
- Preserved ethical limits rather than using cultural context to excuse harm.
