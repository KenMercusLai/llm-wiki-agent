# Global Sports Sponsorship

## Definition
Global sports sponsorship purchases rights to associate a brand with an event, league or host city; its value depends on actual audience access, activation scope and product or market fit, not simply logo exposure.

## Current Synthesis
World Cup global partnerships, technical integration, local city packages, broadcast negotiations and a brief Premier League policy item illustrate distinct inventories. The episodes describe sponsor strategies and reported rights economics, not audited increases in brand trust or sales.

## Key Claims
- Repeated global activation can give exporters visibility across markets where product consideration takes time.
- An in-event product role makes some technology sponsorships more concrete than signage alone.
- Broadcast availability is a separate contract yet conditions the exposure a global sponsor can obtain.
- Host-city support rights create local hospitality and fan-zone value while city costs and category restrictions differ from FIFA rights.
- League policy can redirect shirt inventory without proving new sponsors' returns.

## Evidence
- Worldwide exposure: the World Cup discussion names Yingli and Wanda among Chinese sponsors and describes Hisense, Lenovo and Mengniu as overseas-facing brands, particularly in Europe and the Americas, without measuring conversion. Adidas reportedly staged over 40 offline events and localized a China-specific shirt/content campaign. [[vol-264-ba-shijiebei-zuowei-fangfa-999416048]]
- Working technology: the same episode describes Hisense's VAR-display cooperation and Lenovo's position as FIFA's first global technology partner, showcasing AI, visualization, media and tactical-analysis tools. These are presented as partnership activities, not independent proof of superior product performance. [[vol-264-ba-shijiebei-zuowei-fangfa-999416048]]
- Audience distribution: Silicon Valley 101 recounts CCTV–FIFA negotiations, reporting an agreement spanning men's 2026/2030 and women's 2027/2031 tournaments with undisclosed price. It says Lenovo had an incentive to help the parties reach agreement because unavailable Chinese coverage would diminish sponsor exposure; sublicensing to platforms such as Migu or Douyin is a separate monetization question, as are public-access obligations. [[e237-yangshi-he-fifa-tanpan-fenzheng-beihou-tiyu-saishi-zhuanboquan-de-boyi-yu-shengyi-c3607c08-6eb6-48bd-8250-b41b5b8272e6]]
- Local packages: the live-attendance episode reports FIFA receiving tickets, global sponsors, merchandise and core media rights while host committees fund security, transport, venue changes and fan festivals. City supporters obtain hospitality, suites, tickets and fan-zone activation subject to category limits, not automatic stadium rights. The same first-person account reports a $900-plus ticket loss and 15% fees on both sides of official resale, illustrating the fan-side cost context rather than sponsor ROI. [[vol-267-ruhe-xianchang-kan-shijiebei-bing-da-kui-yi-bi-feat-yingyan-shijian-1002986145]]
- League inventory: a short Shengdong Zaokafei roundup says Premier League front-of-shirt betting restrictions are pushing clubs toward other international categories; it supplies no league-wide pricing or conversion evidence. [[qishui-yinyue-chongji-tengxun-wangyi-tesila-deng-cheqi-zai-guonei-kaizhan-daguimo-zhaohui-1008396395]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Sponsor rights, broadcast sublicensing and host-city packages are legally and economically distinct. The sources' campaign figures, fees and city finances are podcast reports rather than audited accounts; repeated exposure alone is not causal proof of trust. The league item is a side story of a multi-topic news roundup.

## What Changed
- Separated FIFA global and city entitlements, broadcast distribution and league shirt policy instead of presenting one undifferentiated sponsorship effect.
