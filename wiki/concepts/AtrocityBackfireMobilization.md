## Definition
Atrocity backfire mobilization / 暴行反噬式动员 is the reversal in which coercion or humiliation intended to secure surrender instead makes resistance safer, more legitimate or more emotionally compelling than submission. The mechanism depends on how victims and allies interpret visible violence, not on a rule that every harsh act must backfire.

## Current Synthesis
At [[JimoWarringStates|即墨]] of [[QiState|齐国]], [[TianDan|田单]] spreads rumors that Qi defenders most fear nose-cut prisoners displayed and ancestral graves dug up. [[QiJie|骑劫]] does both to frighten them; the resulting bodily and ancestral insult hardens resistance within Jimo and makes already occupied Qi cities harder for [[YanState|燕国]] to hold. This reverses [[LeYi|乐毅]]'s [[OccupationPacificationStrategy|pacification]] and prepares—but does not alone win—the false-surrender and [[FireOxNightRaid|fire-ox]] counterattack. The episode reports Qi Jie's death and the return of seventy-plus cities after the raid; Tian Dan's staging, bribery, 5,000 warriors and Yan command change also matter.

[[XiangYu|项羽]] supplies both a household-scale and an occupation-scale comparison. He holds [[WangLingMother|王陵母亲]] to force [[WangLingHanMinister|王陵]]'s loyalty; she asks his envoy to remain with [[LiuBang|刘邦]] and kills herself, removing the leverage. Xiang Yu's subsequent order to cook her body becomes, in the source's reading, a cruelty signal pushing Wang Ling more firmly toward Liu Bang. In Qi, even after [[TianRong|田荣]] is killed and local people appear ready to placate Xiang Yu, killing surrendered troops, burning cities and plundering civilians destroys a path to submission. Liu Bang's simultaneous territorial gains, Xiang Yu's earlier partition grievances and Qi's own politics also shape the campaign; renewed resistance cannot be assigned to one act alone.

The late-Han case adds regional legitimacy. In 193 CE in [[YouzhouLateHan|幽州]], [[GongsunZan|公孙瓒]] defeats the popular but militarily ineffective [[LiuYuLateHan|刘虞]], then uses [[DuanXunLateHan|段迅]]'s court-edict visit to accuse Liu Yu of imperial ambition and execute him and his family publicly at Ji. Later [[XianyuFu|鲜于辅]], [[YanRou|阎柔]], [[LiuHeLateHan|刘和]], [[Wuhuan|乌桓]], [[Xianbei|鲜卑]] and [[YuanShao|袁绍]]-aligned forces mobilize around the grievance. Hanji 998 reports a nominal 100,000 coalition and more than 20,000 Gongsun troops killed, followed by local officials' defections. His later isolation behind [[YijingFortress|易京]]'s iron gates is a separate failure of initiative and feedback. [[TianChou|田筹]]'s loyal memorial mission, rebuke at Liu Yu's tomb and later ordered Xu Wu Mountain settlement show Liu Yu's authority survived death in forms other than simple revenge.

## Key Claims
- Public mutilation or ancestral violation can turn submission into a worse perceived outcome and give defenders a shared moral injury.
- A hostage's bodily desecration can convert failed elite coercion into a reputational and alliance cost.
- Post-victory violence can forfeit an already emerging occupation settlement and renew local resistance.
- Executing a locally respected rival under coerced legal theater can create a coalition's revenge and legitimacy axis.
- Atrocity backfire is conditional: command changes, partition grievances, armed allies and follow-on tactics also shape outcomes.

## Evidence
- Fear converted to siege resolve: [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-79-shishang-yong-gengniu-da-shengzhang-diyi-ren-lsrl0posblloqwugox160xsicp2p|周纪 79]] records Tian Dan's rumor bait, Qi Jie's mutilations, grave digging and later fire-ox assault.
- Coercion and pacification reversal: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-2-peng-le-wangling-muqin-xiangyu-sharen-zhuxin-ljyv8bow4rj1l5ths3znx2evgvtm|汉纪 148-2]] traces Wang Ling's mother's refusal and corpse treatment; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-149-xichu-bawang-xiangyu-weihe-shijun-2-lpohbb3prsgvokpjipfd7i1ntiwn|汉纪 149-2]] describes Xiang Yu's punitive Qi campaign after Tian Rong's death.
- Public execution and coalition aftermath: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-972-sanguo-liuyu-zhisi-taisha-taitianzhen-lvawrcandbh628rckukosgarh7v2|汉纪 972]] supplies Liu Yu's defeat and marketplace killing; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-998-baima-jiangjun-gongsunzan-cong-sanqian-baima-dao-kunshou-gucheng-lp-1l2gvbsbfnyznutdghxwwvqna|汉纪 998]] traces Yan Rou and Xianyu Fu's coalition, regional defections and Gongsun Zan's retreat.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Liu Yu's humane restrictions and loose troops contributed to his battlefield defeat; his moral standing alone was not military security. The large Hanji 998 coalition figures are episode-reported and its moral portrait of Gongsun Zan is interpretive. Wang Ling was already wavering as Liu Bang's strength rose; the corpse treatment did not create a preference out of nothing. Tian Dan deliberately baited Qi Jie's acts, and success also depended on false surrender and surprise. Do not generalize these episodes into a claim that all violence reliably produces rebellion.

## What Changed
- Compared the same backfire mechanism at siege, hostage, occupation and regional-coalition scales.
- Kept alternative military and political causes explicit beside the moral-injury signal.
