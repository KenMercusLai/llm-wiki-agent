## Definition
Founder-investor learning is the conversion of experience building a company—including fundraising opacity, product judgment, customer work and organizational mistakes—into an investor's or accelerator's selection, advice and support practices.

## Current Synthesis
The strongest accounts describe a feedback loop rather than a claim that former founders automatically make better investors. [[PaulGraham]] and [[TrevorBlackwell]] retrospectively connect [[Viaweb]]'s opaque fundraising to the early [[YCombinator]] experiment; [[RonConway]] describes learning by observing [[DonValentine]]'s board work and narrowing an internet-software thesis. Later alumni made selection, investor access and community infrastructure repeatable. These are participant accounts, not a comparative test of investor returns.

## Key Claims
- Founders can turn the gaps they experienced in early financing into a more legible seed-investor offering.
- Practical operator judgment needs a selection process and repeatable feedback channels to scale beyond personal advice.
- Board observation and customer-facing work can teach an operator how to advise founders without managing their companies.
- Accelerator alumni can translate founder needs into investor access and peer-community infrastructure.
- An investor's product conviction can require sourcing, technical validation, syndication, distribution and recruitment, not just writing a check.
- Useful advising preserves founders' own decision authority and tests their claims against customer behavior rather than the advisor's preferences.

## Evidence
- Seed-investor design: [[socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll-socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll]] recalls [[JulianWeber]]'s $10,000 check, legal help, board service and 10% stake as Graham's example of a deal useful to both sides. [[tsr-s2-trevorblackwell-v2]] recalls opaque Viaweb fundraising and the early [[SummerFoundersProgram]], including more than 300 printed applications and quick interview judgments. Graham's essays formed a [[StartupEssayDistribution]] route to applicants, while [[JessicaLivingston]] and [[RobertMorris]] were collaborators rather than passive beneficiaries.
- Repeatable institutional work: [[tsr-s4-harj-v3-tsr-s4-harj-v3]] describes [[HarjTaggar]] moving from YC Winter 2007 founder to a Winter 2010 founder-support and investor-relations role; his joking “bus inspector” assignment filtered distracting meetings, while [[YuriMilner]]'s batch-wide offer changed access to capital. [[socialradarspod-garrytan-final]] follows [[GarryTan]] from [[StartupSchool]] and [[Posterous]] to [[Bookface]], [[InitializedCapital]] and back to YC; YC's launch and signup critique for Posterous illustrates the advice he received, while [[FounderHonesty]] names his later insistence that advisers be candid before avoidable problems become fatal. Community memory and candid feedback became part of his institutional work.
- Learning by observation and focus: [[tsr-s3-ronconwayep1-v4final-tsr-s3-ronconwayep1-v4final]] and [[tsr-s4-ronconway-v4-tsr-s4-ronconway-v4]] record Conway's operating path through [[NationalSemiconductor]] and [[AltosComputer]], [[BandOfAngels]]' pitch meetings and individual angel checks as an early [[OrganizedAngelInvestorNetworks|organized angel network]], Valentine's board invitations, first angel stake in [[NaturalLanguageIncorporated]], partnership with [[BenRosen]], and an [[InternetSoftwareThesis]] supported by a [[StartupInformationLayer]] of analysts, journalists and conferences. He says he preferred founder help to large-company HR; his account also recognizes Altos missing a subsequent PC/Ethernet shift.
- Network execution: [[tsr-s4-ronconway-part3-v2-tsr-s4-ronconway-part3-v2]] gives Conway's [[Google]] account: [[DavidCheriton]] and [[StanfordStartupSourcing]] led to [[PageRankSearchRelevance]], [[BobBozeman]] tested the product, and [[RamShriram]] helped arrange [[KleinerPerkins]] and [[SequoiaCapital]] together because [[LarryPage]] and [[SergeyBrin]] valued their respective [[AOL]] and [[Yahoo]] routes. Conway reports $12 million from each firm at $70 million pre-money; [[AdWords]] monetization came later. His investor role also included personnel introductions and distribution work.
- Advising without substitution: [[socialradarspod-paulb-final]] has [[PaulBuchheit]] describe YC work as advising startups while founders decide whether to follow advice; his [[PreProductSelling]] test asks for customer sacrifices such as a letter of intent or payment over polite interest, and he acknowledges outlier-driven investing. [[socialradarspod-brianarmstrong-final]] supplies the recipient view: [[BrianArmstrong]] says YC's initial check helped him commit to [[Coinbase]], [[PaulBuchheit]] helped with an early co-founder mismatch, and Garry Tan later coached him as CEO; that does not establish Armstrong's own investor practice.
- Career transition boundary: [[socialradarspod-davidlieb-final]] says [[DavidLieb]]'s work on [[Bump]] and [[GooglePhotos]], then leukemia treatment, changed his view of time and founder support. The note establishes motivation for leaving Google but not a detailed post-transition investment method; his Bump user conversations and failed [[Flock]] launch are product-learning experience, not proof of advice outcomes.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These episodes are chiefly founder and investor recollections, with overlapping Conway and YC perspectives. The Google financing account is Conway's, not an independent audit; [[FriendFeed]]'s disputed Like-button priority has no bearing on investor learning. Early money may transfer confidence, but that alone neither proves business viability nor predicts founder outcomes. An investor's own founder experience can mislead if a new founder's market or constraints differ.

## What Changed
- Reorganized a chronological collection of profiles around financing design, selection, alumni infrastructure, network execution and advice boundaries.
- Separated founders receiving help from investors learning to provide it; kept the Lieb transition narrow.
