# Fieldwork As Knowledge Method / 田野作为知识方法

## Definition
Fieldwork turns sustained, situated contact—observation, measurement, participation, records and comparison—into revisable knowledge, not scenic travel or a guarantee of neutrality.

## Current Synthesis
Historical scholarship, contemporary reporting, worker archives and anthropology use different methods, but all must distinguish what was observed from how it is explained. Fiction provides a method analogy only.

## Key Claims
- Site visits can test written claims when routes, measurements and material traces are compared with documents and chronology.
- Ordinary labor and backstage participation reveal mechanisms that public-facing narratives miss, provided records preserve context.
- Environmental and technological constraints become evidence when field observations are cross-checked against systems knowledge.
- Ritual and journalism require attention to organizers, access, source trust and what remote reporting cannot see directly.
- The observer's body, translation needs, institutional access and intervention shape the evidence and its limits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Several reporting accounts from [[TalkSanlian|Talk三联]] are linked productions, not independent statistical replications. [[36-heian-de-zuoshou-liufang-yu-ai-de-weilai-wangshi-605254522]] is fiction. Field contact cannot by itself establish causation, represent everyone in a community, or dissolve translation and power asymmetries; [[35-tianzhende-renleixuejia-zuo-tianye-hui-shangyin-de-603418624]] explicitly exposes these constraints. [[19-huangye-shang-de-dashi-wo-bei-yu-luanshi-qiu-yanjiu-ben-wei-niliu-zhi-wangju-565412437]]'s modern-scholarship account is a book discussion, not direct verification of every historical attribution or automatic [[CosmopolitanChineseScholarship|cosmopolitan]] consensus.
