## Definition
Customer evidence strategy orders customer statements, observed work, purchase signals and technically constrained commitments by what each can actually establish about a product decision.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded founder interviews show a progression from agreeable feedback to concrete workflow and buying behavior. Segment's original classroom idea and analytics launch, Amplitude's first price conversation, Hightouch's enterprise architecture and Boom's pre-delivery airline intent occupy different evidence levels. Customer evidence disciplines founder judgment; it neither replaces judgment nor makes intent equal to paid, delivered revenue.

## Key Claims
- Broad conversations should identify a buyer's actual workflow and priority rather than solicit polite approval of a proposed feature.
- Usage, repeated requests and distribution after launch are stronger product signals than favorable interviews or stars alone.
- An unsolicited price question or paid contract tests urgency more directly than a hypothetical willingness to use.
- In hard tech, credible buyer intent can precede a working final product but cannot prove certification, delivery or recognized revenue.
- Customer context can justify a deliberate strategic exception to generic startup advice without proving every risky expansion was right.

## Evidence
- Buyer workflow and exception: [[tsr-ycoffsite-kasishgupta-v1-audioonly-tsr-ycoffsite-kasishgupta-v1-audioonly]] has [[KashishGupta]] describe [[Hightouch]]'s pivot from YC travel into activating [[EnterpriseDataActivation|customer data activation]] already in [[Snowflake]] and [[Databricks]], with an early enterprise focus despite advice to start small. He says co-founders pressed one another to identify which customer actually supplied a claim; later marketer AI direction and sales hiring still required judgment and co-founder alignment, not a customer vote.
- Saying versus doing: [[peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted-peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted]] reports that [[PaulGraham]] and [[RobertMorris]] were skeptical of [[ClassMetric]] despite accepting the team into YC; classroom use also revealed distracted laptops. [[PeterReinhardt]]'s [[Segment]]-origin [[AnalyticsJS|analytics.js]] release drew [[HackerNews]] interest, stars and signup requests; demand for a hosted routing product and sales diagnosis by [[ChrisSurdean]] are stronger evidence than launch attention alone. Accelerator acceptance of a team was not validation of its first product. The source is a converted structured export, not a verbatim transcript.
- Pricing urgency: [[tsr-s5-spenserskates-v2audio-tsr-s5-spenserskates-v2audio]] follows [[SpenserSkates]] and [[CurtisLiu]] from the impressive [[Sonalight]] voice-demo/poor-retention gap into internal [[ProductAnalytics|analytics]]. About 30 conversations were a discovery target, but [[TwelveGigs|12gigs]] asking for a price and treating $1,000 per month as inexpensive supplied a clearer buying signal for [[Amplitude]]; this was an early customer account, not a market-wide pricing estimate.
- Hard-tech intent: [[tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio-tsr-s5-blakescholl-v3-finalaudio]] describes [[BlakeScholl]] using spreadsheet economics, expert recruitment, a [[VirginGroup|Virgin]] statement and subsequent airline orders to establish possible demand for [[BoomSupersonic]]. [[XB1SupersonicDemonstrator|XB-1]] was a separate technical proof step; supplier and engine issues, certification and the proposed [[OvertureSupersonicAirliner|Overture]] remain distinct from buyer interest and delivered revenue.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The four notes are founder interviews in one podcast milieu, not independent audits. Stars and signups can be curiosity; a single pricing reaction does not prove durable retention. Boom's orders and LOIs are not certified aircraft or realized income, and its passenger timeline is conditional. Gupta's enterprise-first choice rests on a specific warehouse-data architecture, not a general rejection of small-customer testing.

## What Changed
- Ordered signals by their evidentiary strength and domain rather than founder chronology.
- Kept hard-tech demand proof separate from technical and regulatory proof.
