# Appeasement Logic / 绥靖逻辑

## Definition
Appeasement logic is the risk that avoiding an immediate conflict with an expansionist or persecuting power can be described as prudence or peace while transferring costs to others and narrowing one's own future options. The label is an interpretation of a policy's incentives and outcomes, not a synonym for every negotiated compromise or cautious neutrality.

## Current Synthesis
The sources offer different scales rather than one identical historical mechanism. In the IBM episode, 'trade brings peace' is criticized as a moral and business rationale for continued cooperation with Nazi Germany; the source alleges that tailored data systems materially assisted persecution. In the Qi episodes, Jun Wang Hou's distant, low-posture diplomacy is credited with decades of peace but may have mistaken Qin's temporary reassurance for lasting security as neighboring states fell. Qi Wang Jian's final surrender exposes the strategic endpoint and an important humanitarian tension: lost sovereignty and an unhonored promise coexist with a reported low-casualty conquest. Modern EU–US tariff bargaining belongs to a separate security-dependence analogy, not evidence of classical appeasement.

## Key Claims
- **C1 — Peace rhetoric can legitimate harmful cooperation:** A commercial actor can frame continued ties with a violent regime as stabilizing trade while contributing tools or legitimacy to that regime's coercive capacity.
- **C2 — Short-run peace and strategic erosion can coexist:** Neutrality toward a distant expansionist power can spare a state immediate war even as that power removes neighboring buffers and changes the terms of later bargaining.
- **C3 — Surrender can spare lives without redeeming the earlier strategy:** Qi's terminal bargain reportedly avoided a major final battle, yet ended its independence and did not deliver the promised land.

## Evidence
- **C1 — Peace rhetoric can legitimate harmful cooperation:** The IBM/Dehomag episode criticizes Watson's trade-and-peace defense while describing ongoing punched-card services and administrative infrastructure under Nazi rule; it also links prewar accommodation and isolationist rhetoric to tolerating aggression against others. These are the episode's historical and moral claims, not an independent audit of corporate decisions. [[133-ibm-yu-nacui-weishenme-putongren-bu-ying-zanmei-jizeizhuyi-815684110]]
- **C2 — Short-run peace and strategic erosion can coexist:** The Qi account attributes more than forty years of relative peace to Jun Wang Hou's cautious policy but warns that Qin's distant-friendly posture reflected its nearer conquests; the linked-jade-rings story depicts her asserting a boundary rather than simple submission. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-119-3-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhenzheng-yiyishang-chuilian-tingzheng-taihou-liq9d3itmbar00ij43zzed-mulul]]
- **C3 — Surrender can spare lives without redeeming the earlier strategy:** The account says Qin induced Qi Wang Jian to surrender with a five-hundred-li land promise it did not keep, while crediting Qi's long peace and low-casualty final campaign. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-1-shui-shi-huangdi-zhong-de-mabaonan-lhw8vsj6ta6rjdkugcvfohaqqxc0]]

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- Jun Wang Hou's policy preserved decades of peace and, in the jade-rings account, an explicit diplomatic boundary. The host calls it appeasement, but this is an interpretive comparison, not proof that Qi's decision makers had no defensible alternatives. Its low-casualty endpoint matters even though sovereignty was lost.
- IBM's particular degree of knowledge, control, and culpability requires evidence beyond this podcast note for a definitive historical finding. The source's moral criticism of commercial enabling should not flatten individual, corporate, and interstate conduct into a single causal chain.
- The Europe episode describes EU economic size, NATO/security dependence, and tariff bargaining. It does not substantively establish a Chamberlain-style appeasement claim in its own summary or key claims; modern alliance bargaining is not equated here with fascist conquest. [[139-cong-zuo-ri-de-shi-jie-dao-ru-jin-de-ou-zhou-mei-hao-nian-dai-belle-epoque-ta-hai-neng-hui-lai-ma-ltvsgov8ht9-l7yp9qamdgjmyrf]]
- The corpus supports comparison of trade rhetoric, neutrality, and surrender outcomes, but not a universal rule that concessions necessarily cause renewed aggression. The sources are podcast interpretations of different periods and actors.

## What Changed
- Separates the corporate cooperation case from Qi's interstate neutrality and final surrender rather than forcing one chronological narrative.
- Keeps Qi's long peace and low-casualty surrender as substantive qualifications to a strategic-loss judgment.
- Restricts the EU–US/NATO material to a bargaining-dependence analogy, not direct evidence of appeasement.
