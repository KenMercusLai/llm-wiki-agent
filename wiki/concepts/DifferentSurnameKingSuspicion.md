## Definition
Different-surname king suspicion describes the early Han center's distrust of powerful non-Liu territorial rulers whose wartime troops, prestige and local networks remained after unification. It also describes how a king's fear of removal could itself push resistance. These are interpretations of one historical-podcast series, not a universal law of kingship.

## Current Synthesis
[[LiuBang]] initially depended on autonomous contributors. The episode reads [[ZangTu]]'s rebellion as possibly defensive rather than a bid for the throne, and his replacement by childhood intimate [[LuWan]] in distant [[YanState]] as a shift from reward by merit to personal trust. Even a minor figure, [[LiJiChuHan]], reportedly feared a summons enough to rebel. [[HanXin]]'s Chu kingship and protection of [[ZhongliMo]] looked strategically dangerous to the center but also served a local-network need. [[ChenPing]] therefore proposed the [[RitualTourSummonsEntrapment|Yunmeng ritual-tour summons]] at [[ChenCountyChuHan]] rather than a costly direct attack; Zhongli Mo died, Han Xin presented his head, and was reduced to Huaiyin marquis. Demotion did not remove fear around his contacts with [[FanKuai]] and [[LuZhi]].

The formal change from [[ConcededKingshipStabilization]] to [[CommandAuthorityRecapture]] was never instantaneous: [[HostileTerritoryRoyalContainment]] describes why Han Xin needed Zhongli Mo's Chu network, while [[PublicDisavowalSelfProtection]] is only one interpretation of his Fan Kuai insult. Containment also took territorial and institutional forms. Han Xin's former Chu land was split among Liu kin; [[HanWangXin]] was relocated toward the [[Mayi]] frontier for defense and possible political containment before his pressured surrender to [[Xiongnu]] and later armed opposition. [[ZhangAo]] was cleared personally by [[GuanGao]]'s testimony, yet the Zhao court's actual assassination plot led to loss of kingship and replacement by [[LiuRuyi]]. The host argues this was also part of a wider rollback, not a simple proof of Zhang Ao's guilt.

The greatest danger to consolidation was the safety signal received by other merit-holders. [[ChenXi]], initially a frontier commander rather than king, became exposed through [[ZhouChang]]'s warning, investigation and a funeral summons; the episode narrates a capital plot involving Han Xin, while a subsequent installment contests that plot as likely framed and proposes that Liu Bang valued a living demoted Han Xin as evidence surrender was survivable. [[LuanBu]] protested [[PengYue]]'s execution as making all contributors unsafe; the shipment of Peng Yue's corpse-paste to [[YingBu]] preceded his preparations, [[BenHe]]'s accusation and [[XiaoHe]]'s cautious inquiry, which Ying Bu interpreted as a death threat. Finally Lu Wan, despite intimacy with Liu Bang, responded to [[ZangYan]]'s Xiongnu-side persuasion by protecting [[ZhangShengWesternHan]] and sending [[FanQiLuWanEnvoy]] to prolong Chen Xi's resistance; Liu Bang investigated and offered Yan residents pardon while sending [[FanKuai]]. Personal closeness did not overcome the structural fear, but the source stops before a full account of Lu Wan's final defection.

The earlier [[MeritBasedRewardPunishment]] and [[RewardAllocationBacklash]] frames help explain why a merit-holder expects title security; [[PunitiveSurrenderDeterrence]] names the feared reversal when demotion fails to protect life. [[HuainanKingdomEarlyHan]]'s own officers and troops made Ying Bu's fear consequential, whereas [[LiuYing]]'s succession raised a different risk around Han Xin's prestige. [[XiangYu]]'s prior partition explains Zang Tu's title rather than proving a postwar rebellion motive.

## Key Claims
- Postwar royal territories and retainers could look threatening even when wartime rewards had been legitimate.
- Ceremony, relocation, demotion and replacement could recapture power without initially attacking every king in open war.
- A court's genuine plot need not prove a king's personal guilt; a case may still become the occasion for institutional replacement.
- Inquiry and summons may seem prudent centrally while convincing an exposed commander or king that peaceful compliance is unsafe.
- Executions can damage the credibility of demotion and surrender for other merit-holders, potentially making rebellion more likely.
- Personal intimacy is not an enduring substitute for enforceable safety when non-Liu territorial autonomy is being rolled back.

## Evidence
- Founding reward and trusted replacement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-171-zhangliang-jiao-ni-ruhe-mingli-shuangshou-2-lm1iff0cadjapwf2mggwyo8wcvxn]] gives Zang Tu, Lu Wan, Li Ji and Han Xin's Chu network; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-174-liubang-weihe-dafeng-zijide-chouren-1-lnzzvpieqtgatmvgogz3msyhs60u]] describes splitting Chu among Liu kin and relocating Han Wang Xin to Taiyuan/Mayi; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-177-beikun-baidengshan-liubang-mingxuan-yixian-ljdmxf6bej8ju0gmvm9cp48gy0on]] reports his later Xiongnu-linked campaign, not proof of his original motive.
- Non-battle containment and lingering ties: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-172-liubang-yangyou-yunmengze-zhiqin-hanxin-lrpkye8y6ibqdqs9pz7artk-6chv]] details Chen Ping's Yunmeng protocol trap and Zhongli Mo's dilemma; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-173-hanxin-taoguo-kuaxia-zhiru-mei-duoguo-niaojin-gongcang-1-ljowpzxo9u0evorhzbdyi2o0shlx]] reads Han Xin's public insult of Fan Kuai as possibly self-protective distancing after demotion, not settled intent.
- King versus retinue: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-1-li5u-dmsmb1lp4ny3rk02kaiyu4k]] records Guan Gao's torture-resistant exoneration of Zhang Ao and the king's demotion; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-180-xihan-diyi-yinghan-guangao-de-gushi-2-lutkapdijrgpwfrz3saaowxtk-i2]] distinguishes Guan Gao's guilt, Liu Bang's arrogance and the host's broader rollback interpretation, with [[TianShuHan]] and [[MengShuHan]] retained for Zhao pacification.
- Investigation and disputed plot: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-182-weishenme-shuo-chengye-xiaohe-baiye-xiaohe-1-lhe3h27wdbju3cgsvukdvgkri1a9]] presents the chronicle-style Chen Xi/Han Xin inside-outside scheme, Zhao warning and funeral summons; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-183-hanxin-zhisi-zhiyu-le-liubang-de-jingshen-neihao-lrbucdibto5zezrhl7ue0y9vhb-l]] explicitly disputes Han Xin's alleged plot and infers Liu Bang's interest in keeping a demoted king alive. Neither account resolves the conflict independently.
- Fear propagation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-184-lishi-kongbupian-liubang-de-pengshi-roujiang-2-liebzrdrvo1okonpdjvrjatuwx9i]] preserves Luan Bu's attributed defense of Peng Yue and Liu Bang's pardon; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-186-yi-chang-yin-bajie-lingdao-laopo-yinfa-de-miemen-can-an-lqx14uzf60oi6wnnzdkottd6kx1n]] reports the corpse-paste, Ben He's accusation, Xiao He's proposal to investigate and Ying Bu's decision; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-187-yingbu-panluan-liubang-weihe-tuobingti-qinzheng-lvjafotlnvmyqmwaeav3novz1dox]] follows Ying Bu's open revolt and [[XueGongHanAdviser]]'s forecast of a bounded strategy.
- Intimate trust tested: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-191-hanchao-kaiguo-wujiang-zhishou-weihe-pantao-xiongnu-ljaqjcu5w9jmfb-4hco5ps1irvwr]] describes Zhang Sheng's persuasion, Lu Wan's covert delay, refused summons, Liu Bang's investigation and amnesty to separate the Yan public from its king.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
[[Yunmengze]] was the pretext for an imperial tour, not evidence of an actual sightseeing itinerary. [[DeathDefyingRemonstrance]] describes Luan Bu's public protest, not a settled verdict on Peng Yue's innocence; [[VeteranMeritCliqueSupervision]] helps explain why Fan Kuai's military and Lu-family ties mattered after Han Xin's demotion. [[DaiState]] was among the frontier kingdoms redistributed to Liu kin, distinct from the individual charge against Han Wang Xin.

All thirteen notes belong to one historical-podcast chain; serial installments are not independent corroboration. Zang Tu's defensive motive, Fan Kuai's possible Lu-family outreach, Liu Bang's alleged wish to preserve Han Xin and the “rollback policy” explanation are host inferences. Hanji 182 presents a plot; Hanji 183 calls it likely framed, and the page does not resolve that disagreement. Guan Gao did plot despite Zhang Ao's exoneration. Xiao He urged investigation before punishment in Ying Bu's case, so the center was not uniformly acting without evidence. The Hanji 187 account stops before Ying Bu's final defeat; Hanji 191 stops before fully narrating Lu Wan's defection.

## What Changed
- Integrated territorial replacement, ceremonial capture and frontier transfer as different forms of central recapture.
- Preserved the contradictory Han Xin conspiracy readings and distinction between Guan Gao's crime and Zhang Ao's innocence.
- Traced fear from Peng Yue's death through Ying Bu's and Lu Wan's separate decisions without asserting one inevitable purge.
