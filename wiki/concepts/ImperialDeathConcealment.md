# Imperial Death Concealment / 秘不发丧式过渡控制

Imperial death concealment / 秘不发丧式过渡控制 is the transition mechanism [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg|Qinji 127-2]] draws from [[QinShiHuang|秦始皇]]'s death on tour. [[LiSi|李斯]] fears that an immediate announcement away from [[Xianyang|咸阳]] could trigger princely disorder or popular unrest, so the traveling court preserves the appearance that the emperor is still alive.

The source's details matter because concealment is not merely silence. Food continues to be sent in, officials still submit reports, and the dead emperor remains associated with the travel vehicle. The living administrative routine becomes a screen over the ruler's death until the convoy can return and the succession result can be announced.

The concept extends [[ImperialItinerarySecrecy|帝王行踪保密]] but is not identical to it. Itinerary secrecy hides the ruler's location while he is alive; death concealment hides the fact that the ruler no longer exists as a decision-maker. That makes it especially vulnerable to [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|伪诏继位政变]], because whoever controls the dead ruler's physical setting, attendants, seals, and written commands can speak in his name.

## Key Claims
- Concealing a ruler's death can be framed as temporary stability management before it becomes succession manipulation.
- The method depends on routine performance: meals, reports, vehicles, attendants, and silence all help preserve normality.
- A narrow information circle can stabilize the convoy while also excluding possible veto actors.
- Death concealment becomes dangerous when written commands can still be issued as if the ruler had personally authorized them.
- The pattern belongs near [[AutocraticSuccession]] because personalized power leaves an immediate command vacuum when the central person dies.
