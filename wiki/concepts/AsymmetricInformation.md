## Definition
Asymmetric information is a difference in what participants know about quality, risk, value or process before exchange or evaluation. It can deter trade, misprice risk, create a trust service or even become a game mechanic.

## Current Synthesis
In luxury resale, buyers struggle to verify a secondhand [[Chanel]] bag. [[TheRealReal]]'s [[LuxuryResaleAuthentication|authentication]] as [[AuthenticationLedMarketplaceTrust|trust infrastructure]] moves judgment to trained inspectors using construction, materials, hardware and brand identifiers, reducing [[MarketplaceFrictionReduction|buyer friction]] while creating liability if “100% the real thing” outruns proof. Chanel's seven allegedly counterfeit bags remain allegations in the episode: the March 2020 ruling allowed reasonable mark use and lawful resale but left authenticity claims contestable if the bags were proven fake. The RealReal's first-sale and competition arguments do not eliminate the brand's counterfeit concerns or grant Chanel exclusive authority over all resales.

Insurance can reverse who knows more. An applicant may know health or behavioral risk the insurer has not captured; [[MaryPatCampbell]] treats underwriting as regulated, domain-constrained selection using [[ActuarialDataQuality|clean data]] and [[InsuranceModelRegulatoryConstraint|regulated-variable limits]], not a license to use every predictive variable. On the other side of the market, a policyholder can know the face value yet not the price investors would pay in the [[LifeInsuranceSecondaryMarket]] for a [[LifeSettlement]]. [[FrankLifeSettlementSeller|Frank]]'s source-reported $1.5 million coverage drew a $470,000 highest bid from [[CoventryLifeSettlements|Coventry]], brokered by [[EvergreenSettlements]], with a $40,000 commission leaving about $430,000. His spreadsheet's roughly 12% annual-return hurdle over 12 years and health history are decision-specific, not a general sell recommendation. Brokers and competing bids may narrow [[LifeSettlementPricingOpacity|seller opacity]] while adding commissions.

A published research result also hides analysis choices. [[AbelBrodeur]]'s [[ResearchIntegrityIncentives|inspection-incentive]] approach through [[ReplicationGames]] inspect [[ReplicationPackage|code and data]] beyond a polished paper, with [[RobustnessChecks|robustness testing]]; in Montreal 71 replicators and 14 main teams found most papers without major issues but two with more serious problems. A cartel study's sensitivity to excluding one cartel remains a dispute because the authors say that case was central. In [[SellMeASasquatch]], [[MarketForLemons|hidden quality]] is instead made into bluffing and distrust for a party game: the information gap is the play, not a defect to eliminate.

## Key Claims
- Verification intermediaries can make hard-to-check goods tradable, but a categorical authenticity promise creates its own legal exposure.
- Insurance has both applicant-side private risk information and seller-side uncertainty about a secondary-market price; data, bids and regulation address different gaps.
- Research transparency requires inspectable code, data and robustness choices, not just the published result or unused disclosure requirements.
- Hidden quality can be deliberately designed into play rather than treated only as market failure.

## Evidence
- Product quality and competing rights: [[seven-allegedly-fake-chanel-bags-vs-the-realreal|Planet Money: Chanel/RealReal]] records authentication practice, litigation, the seven disputed bags and reseller/brand positions.
- Underwriting and sale-price discovery: [[data-risk-and-actuarial-science-in-insurance|Data Science With Sam]] covers applicant selection, data validation and regulated variables; [[you-bet-your-life-insurance|Planet Money: life insurance]] follows Frank's brokered bids and commissions.
- Research choices and experiential design: [[dont-hate-the-replicator-hate-the-game|Planet Money: replication]] distinguishes reproduction from contested robustness; [[board-games-3-whats-in-a-name|Planet Money: board games]] turns information gaps into a lemons-market card-trading mechanic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
No finding here settles whether the seven Chanel bags were counterfeit or whether brand-only certification is necessary. A platform's inspection and presentation may reduce uncertainty but do not guarantee authenticity; these notes do not establish standardized warranty or dispute-handling practices. Frank's transaction depends on premium obligations, health, family needs, taxes and risk tolerance; insurer selection risk differs from his seller-side price uncertainty. Replication fragility is not necessarily fraud, and the cartel exclusion illustrates a legitimate disagreement about the hypothesis, not an adjudicated error.

## What Changed
- Distinguished buyer quality, insurer selection, policyholder pricing and researcher-method information gaps.
- Preserved both trust-service benefits and legal, regulatory and interpretation disputes.
