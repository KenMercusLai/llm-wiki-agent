# AIInvestmentResearch

## Definition
AI investment research is the use of models and agents to collect, structure, compare and monitor financial evidence while keeping thesis, price, risk and final decisions accountable to an investor.

## Current Synthesis
Faster summarization is only the first step. Effective research starts with a question and data provenance, checks cash and accounting against narrative, tracks catalysts and expectations, tests model failure under regime shifts, and records human decisions for later review. Access to AI does not erase institutions' data and execution advantages.

## Key Claims
- Professional analyst AI support should broaden and verify research without recycling institutional confirmation bias.
- AI can reduce filing and news-processing labor when questions target margins, cash conversion, capex and discrepancies rather than generic summaries.
- Research agents can maintain watchlists, event signals and thesis updates, but require clean sources and explicit objectives.
- Quantitative inference must withstand overfitting, changing regimes and price expectations; more information alone is not alpha.
- Human judgment, position sizing and fiduciary responsibility remain outside automatic extraction or buy/sell recommendation, although assistants can teach the underlying concepts.
- Context-rich personal workflows may improve coverage, yet public tools can compress information advantages without equalizing proprietary data or execution.
- Routine junior research tasks may shrink even as sector expertise, compliance and client-facing responsibility remain valuable.

## Evidence
- [[WuWeizhi]] describes [[ZhongouRuibo]] analysts using AI for breadth but checking common sense, logic and public evidence rather than trusting an internal database that reinforces existing beliefs. His [[MAPERInvestmentResearchFramework|MAPER]] and [[ResearchIndexPortfolioConstruction|research-index]] process connects differentiated coverage with measured portfolio use; his source-dated 15–20% AI-theme exposure and liquidity/exit discipline are not general portfolio advice. [[ZhuNing]] warns that individuals may mistake fluent AI analysis for institutional-grade data and process; easier retrieval does not make bubble timing or suitability knowable. Wu's explicit checks illustrate a professional practice, not a claim that institutions cannot err. ## Counterevidence & Qualifications
- The Simons episode is dramatized; its institutional strategy discussion should not be treated as a replica of confidential trading records or a retail recipe. [[ep88-chuanyue-lianghua-zhifu-ximengsi-ai-hui-rang-putongren-geng-rongyi-zhuanqian-haishi-geng-nan-lhvigzza2ugmayezkbrxufkmp4l1]]
- [[Freda]]'s automation optimism depends on access to clean feeds, objective functions and market microstructure; a fast agent can magnify bad sources and emotional trading. [[141-freda-de-touzi-zhaji-di-2-ji-tokenmaxxing-ba-dianji-sai-jin-zhengqiji-jielisai-bian-lanqiusai-gudu-ren-de-lianjie-lmeczs2jtkze79rkpvm-rc5yw22m]] [[ep69-ai-shidai-lailin-touzi-buzai-shi-danji-moshi-lppxmdl3wlrrtswxwpzekz-qzygn]]
- AI-assisted information gathering does not establish actual excess return; price, catalyst, user risk tolerance and clients' realized timing outcomes remain distinct. ## What Changed
- The research workflow moves from one-off document summaries to question-led collection, accounting checks, signal follow-up and human decision logs.
- Automation promises are bounded by data quality, market competition, regime shifts and client responsibility.
