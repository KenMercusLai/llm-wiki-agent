## Definition
Founder proximity is a leader's continuing direct exposure to customers, workers, suppliers, technical work and operational exceptions as an organization grows. It is a learning and diagnostic practice, not a mandate to make every decision personally.

## Current Synthesis
The accounts move from founders physically serving marketplace users to executives restoring customer contact, reviewing high-stakes product work and diagnosing unfilled organizational gaps. They disagree on intensity: close review can reveal missing signals, but only if it improves expert work and leaves scalable ownership with a team. Most claimed performance effects are founders' own retrospective judgments, not independent comparisons.

## Key Claims
- Direct work with multiple marketplace participants can reveal friction hidden by aggregate metrics.
- Customer and product contact must be modeled by senior leaders even after organization layers form.
- Crisis-period review can concentrate decisions but requires a bounded review surface and clear authority.
- Technical or scientific proximity can raise questions and accountability without substituting the founder's judgment for specialist evidence.
- Founder intervention should diagnose and staff organizational gaps, not become permanent ownership of every task.
- Proximity is productive collaboration only when people doing the work can say it improved rather than degraded that work.

## Evidence
- Three-sided fieldwork: [[socialradarspod-tonyxu-final]] says [[TonyXu]] and the [[DoorDash]] founders first helped merchants with chores, tested [[PaloAltoDelivery]] with eight PDF menus and founder-delivered orders—a [[JankyMVP]] that isolated whether customers would order from otherwise non-delivering restaurants—and later encountered dasher queues, school-lunch surges, batching and locker problems. Direct demand listening also informed their [[SuburbanDeliveryStrategy]] where families had fewer nearby options. Xu reports continuing deliveries and daily support contact to check consumer, merchant and driver realities; these are practices, not a measured causal effect on profits. [[socialradarspod-brianchesky-final]] recounts [[BrianChesky]] hosting guests, visiting New York hosts and improving listing photos; awkward cash exchange with a host prompted platform payments, linking fieldwork to [[PeerToPeerMarketplaceTrust]].
- Crisis and later-stage review: [[socialradarspod-brianchesky2-final]] has Chesky describe personal review of employee and expense details during an 80% eight-week COVID business collapse, roughly $1 billion in guest refunds, $250 million host support and $2 billion emergency debt. He then describes one roadmap, functional teams and CEO visibility into major releases. His recovery/performance assessment is a participant account, not proof that universal CEO review is superior.
- Executive customer contact: [[tsr-ycoffsite-sajith-v2final-audio-tsr-ycoffsite-sajith-v2final-audio]] says [[SajithWickramasekara]] and senior [[Benchling]] leaders lost customer contact amid fast hiring; he made direct conversations and weekly sharing of customer learning an executive norm while retaining layers and senior managers. Benchling's reported 200,000 scientists, 7,000 universities and 1,300 companies are interviewee figures, not validation of management effects.
- Science and hard tech: [[tsr-ycoffsite-jenherbach-v1-audio-tsr-ycoffsite-jenherbach-v1-audio]] describes [[JenHerbach]] moving closer to [[AdventrisPharmaceuticals]]' proposed [[CancerVaccinePlatform]] lab work with daily end-of-day standups and two personnel departures; increased productivity is her assessment and no cancer-vaccine efficacy follows from it. [[tsr-ycoffsite-andylapsa-v1-audio-tsr-ycoffsite-andylapsa-v1-audio]] instead has [[AndyLapsa]] say that at [[Stoke]]'s reported 260-plus-person scale, repeated founder firefighting reveals an unfilled role; technical and fundraising proximity should help decide whether to staff, execute or reject adjacent opportunities.
- Retail and research limits: [[all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-gamestop-ceo-ryan-cohens-56b-plan-to-take-over-ebay-41752370]] supports [[RyanCohen]]'s narrower “game of pennies” operating judgment at [[Chewy]] and his stated shift from imported ecommerce talent to internal operators at [[GameStop]]. Its note does not establish his claimed personal AdWords management, direct supplier negotiation or gift anecdote; the eBay acquisition proposal remains a contested transaction. [[ai4s-xuyao-kuangren-yu-yexinjia-duihua-yinglingdian-odin-ruguo-shen-cunzai-wo-zenneng-rongren-ziji-bushi-shen-gonglu-boke-lhceyip6dqomrwk38uvqjwoomxyz]] supports [[HaotianOdin]]'s attention to molecular-model experiments, pharma interfaces and platform-versus-pipeline focus at [[YinglingdianAI]], **not** the old no-private-office or [[BakerLab]] mentor-proximity anecdote.
- Collaboration criterion: [[tsr-ycoffsite-pg-audioonly-final-tsr-ycoffsite-pg-audioonly-final]] has [[PaulGraham]] describe founder mode as unfinished: [[SteveJobs]] and [[JonyIve]] are his example of intense collaboration, while intervention that worsens a specialist's work is micromanagement. That is a boundary for proximity, not evidence that every founder's close review works.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The two Chesky accounts are related first-person narratives, not independent tests. Herbach's scientific timelines and Cohen's acquisition cost claims are unrelated to proof of proximity. Odin's old office/Baker anecdote and Cohen's supplier/advertising stories lack substantive support in the registered notes and are not repeated as facts. Proximity may increase bottlenecks and override specialists; organizational design, delegation and the worker's perspective are necessary counterweights.

## What Changed
- Reframed a founder-by-founder arrival log as fieldwork, executive routines, crisis review and technical-gap diagnosis.
- Removed unsupported colorful proximity anecdotes while retaining the source inventory and narrower documented contexts.
