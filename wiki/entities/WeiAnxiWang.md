# 魏安釐王 / King Anxi of Wei

## Overview
魏安釐王 / King Anxi of Wei: Qin pressure forced repeated Wei concessions and made stable alignment difficult.

## Current Profile
Qin pressure forced repeated Wei concessions and made stable alignment difficult. Royal fear froze Zhao relief and made Xinlingjun both a necessary commander and a rival. Subordination to Qin did not prevent coercion against weaker Wey. Moral judgment and suspected violence against Xinlingjun remain contested interpretations, not established royal intent.

## Key Characteristics
- Qin pressure forced repeated Wei concessions and made stable alignment difficult.
- Royal fear froze Zhao relief and made Xinlingjun both a necessary commander and a rival.
- Subordination to Qin did not prevent coercion against weaker Wey.
- Moral judgment and suspected violence against Xinlingjun remain contested interpretations, not established royal intent.

## Evidence
- **Qin pressure forced repeated Wei concessions and made stable alignment difficult:** [[WeiAnxiWang|魏安釐王]] succeeded [[WeiZhaowang|魏昭王]] in 277 BCE amid [[QinState|Qin]]'s [[QinEastwardPressure|eastward pressure]] after its Chu conquests; his younger brother Wei Wuji became [[Xinlingjun|信陵君]], one of the [[WarringStatesFourLords|四公子]]. In 276 BCE [[BaiQi|白起]] took two [[WeiState|魏]] cities; in 275 BCE [[WeiRan|魏冉]] twice attacked, defeated a [[HanState|韩]] relief force, extracted eight cities, then defeated [[MangMao|芒卯]] and pressed a further concession near Wen. The narrator treats this as a humiliating first lesson for the new king, not proof that any one concession ended Qin's pressure. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-82-daqin-de-hewuqi-baiqi-laile-lo0w8mifkijhzmx7fdwcimmv7ade]] Wei's weak alignment with [[QiState|齐]], then joint attack with [[ZhaoState|赵]] on Han at [[HuayangBattle|华阳]], brought Qin intervention and another defeat. Although [[SuDai|苏代]] called [[DuanGanzi|段干子]]'s land-for-peace proposal a [[LandForPeaceExhaustionTrap|抱薪救火 trap]], the king still ceded land; the episode uses this as failed [[ThreatenedDefectionLeverage|leverage]] rather than durable peace. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-83-zhuhou-bailan-jing-chengle-jingdian-ji-zhong-ji-ljp7nbqnrctr5mw6i9u982abmx-1]] Qin took Huai in 268 BCE under [[QinWangWan|绾]] and nearby Xingqiu in 266 BCE: submission did not stop attacks, a problem for [[YuanjiaoJingongStrategy|nearby-attack strategy]] from Wei's side. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-89-qinwang-jing-bei-ta-dui-mabaonan-ljugedx8qxhtuzd1ja-xiwxgse19]] During [[ChangpingBattle|长平]], ministers welcomed Qin's attack on Zhao as an opportunity, but [[KongBin|孔斌]] warned that Qin's appetite would later threaten Wei too. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-95-changping-zhi-zhan-hou-qinguo-weishenme-meiyou-mie-le-zhaoguo-2-lutsvwvnwzsezoorhe-vu7yjthyx]]
- **Royal fear froze Zhao relief and made Xinlingjun both a necessary commander and a rival:** During the [[Handan|邯郸]] siege, he sent [[JinBi|晋鄙]] with 100,000 men to aid Zhao but halted them at [[YeWarringStates|邺]] after [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] threatened Wei. The stalled army also obstructed [[ChuState|楚]] transit, a [[CoalitionTransitDependency|coalition transit]] and [[CoalitionSelfPreservationFailure|self-preservation]] problem; [[XinyuanYan|新垣衍]] proposed recognizing Qin's ruler as western emperor through [[SubmissionThroughStatusElevation|status elevation]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-2-ltlobdujy-hdipimfdupvwod2ech]] [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]] replied that such recognition would subordinate the ruler and ministers of Wei; the episode's [[CivilizationBarbarismFrame|moral framing]] and [[InterestReframingPersuasion|interest argument]] are persuasion, not proof all his precedents were accurate. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-3-lj-cr4vg4e1qdfjs6yez85-3zuiv]] The king's refusal to commit shifted attention to Xinlingjun's [[TalentAttractionThroughRespect|retainer network]] and [[HouYing|侯嬴]]; Hou Ying then proposed using [[RuJi|如姬]] to take the royal half of the [[TigerTallyCommandSystem|虎符]], with [[ZhuHaiWeiRetainer|朱亥]] as backup if Jin Bi resisted. The resulting [[StealingTigerTalliesToRescueZhao|窃符救赵]] killed a loyal officer, the [[MoralCostOfEffectiveRescue|moral cost]] of bypassing a frightened king. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-5-lp6yfcxoysxgcnmg8v1uljfyptof]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-6-lteqe-0ykjtv01cmxv1fhx6z0vdi]] Xinlingjun stayed in Zhao afterward as a [[FuChen|负臣]]: the king did not formally punish him and retained his title, but neither could the brothers simply resume their former relationship. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-97-2-jiemi-xinlingjun-ping-shenme-shi-si-gongzi-zhi-shou-lodqntthbil9o8ezppt66lywzd-f]] An episode later criticizes the king's inconsistent national strategy and emergency-only use of his brother; that is the host's retrospective judgment, not a counterfactual proven by the record. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-2-ruhe-kandai-xingbenshan-yu-xingbene-lmawsqkttjmitwkczhyfs7f7-mqt]] In 247 BCE [[MengAo|蒙敖]] took Gaodu and Ji, prompting the king to recall Xinlingjun from Zhao after ten years; [[MaoGong|毛公]] and [[XueGong|薛公]] persuaded him to return, and the king embraced him before giving him command of a [[HezongAlliance|coalition]] that countered Qin near [[GuanchengWarringStates|管城]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-1-simaqian-kanzhong-de-xinlingjun-suan-shi-xiaoren-ma-lvty1pdnibvqpk0m16kkxzw4t-ck]] Qin then used [[CounterintelligenceRumorWedge|rumors]] attributed to Jin Bi's old retainers and suggestive congratulations to insinuate Xinlingjun would usurp him; the king removed his brother from command, exemplifying [[RumorErosionOfTrust|trust erosion]] and the [[PowerExitTrap|difficulty of exiting power]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-103-2-simaqiande-idol-weihe-siyu-jiuse-lvmqg1ojmka9pqagfhgvo4albpjx]]
- **Subordination to Qin did not prevent coercion against weaker Wey:** Even after bowing to Qin, Wei Anxi Wang killed visiting [[WeyHuaijun|卫怀君]] and installed [[WeyYuanjun|卫元君]] over [[WeyState|卫]]. The narrator calls this [[WeakOverWeakPredation|downward predation]]: weakness against Qin did not entail restraint toward a smaller polity. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-100-1-huangdi-zhong-de-chaochang-daiji-wang-guoshi-le-lt75scavlm8qjn6ulpd-b2kwzwjf]]
- **Moral judgment and suspected violence against Xinlingjun remain contested interpretations, not established royal intent:** Asked about [[LuZhonglian|鲁仲连]]'s refusal of a reward after the [[LiaochengWarringStates|辽城]] affair, the king told [[ZiShun|子顺]] that such high-minded conduct might be a performance; Zi Shun argued that repeated gentlemanly performance can become [[VirtuePracticeInternalization|character]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-101-1-zhe-wei-daomei-junwang-dengji-santian-ji-cusi-lll-bcc7basaf6-nzwqtmcb5qw6w]] Another episode notes that [[Shiji|《史记》]] places Xinlingjun's death before the king's in the same year and speculates that the king may have removed a powerful uncle to secure [[WeiJingminWang|魏景湣王 / 魏增]]'s succession. [[XinlingjunDeathSuspicion|This suspicion]] is inference from chronology, not evidence of a royal killing order. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-106-jingren-faxian-xinlingjun-de-feizhengchang-siwang-lsj3kd2tb4tqtuvhwvsdwkh-h5el]]

## Qualifications
The host infers a possible killing of Xinlingjun from the order of same-year deaths; the notes do not establish murder. Military figures and court dialogues remain chronicle/podcast claims.

## What Changed
- The crisis profile now pairs Qin-driven concessions with the king’s changing treatment of Xinlingjun; murder remains a later hypothesis, not a known decision.
