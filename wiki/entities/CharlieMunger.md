## Overview
Charlie Munger is used across these podcast notes as a reference for inversion, durable business quality, avoidance of ruin, and recognition of personal limits. The episodes' applications and an imagined conversation are not all direct statements by Munger.

## Current Profile
The coherent thread is to test what could fail before pursuing upside, then distinguish durable customer behavior from a cheap price or compelling chart. The same weakness-aware stance is extended by other speakers to portfolio exposure and daily habits.

## Key Characteristics
- Inversion frames investment analysis as detecting irreversible failure before celebrating upside.
- Business quality rests on customer trust, habit and pricing power, not only visible assets or price movements.
- Crisis judgment asks whether the underlying network or demand survives temporary reputational damage.
- Long-horizon investing requires survivable position sizes and caution about leverage.
- Self-knowledge extends weakness avoidance beyond investing to sleep, preparation and planning.

## Evidence
- **Failure-first analysis:** The EP86 host applies a Munger-style question—how might the company die, mislead or disappoint?—to [[FinancialStatementAnalysis]]: compare profit with cash flow and inspect rising receivables, inventory, audit opinions and management profit pressure as [[AccountingRedFlags]], rather than treating reports solely as upside forecasts. [[ep86-mianzi-dizi-rizi-caibao-zhi-jiang-zhe-san-jian-shi-lukmnlkkuxvrvv12frosd9teg7bj]]
- **Durable demand:** EP80's dramatized conversation uses [[SeesCandies]]' gifting certainty and pricing power to explain [[ConsumerBrandMoat]], contrasting cheap-asset bargains with businesses customers repeatedly choose; [[CocaCola]] illustrates habitual demand, distribution and memory. It warns that [[TechnicalAnalysisLimits|chart-only analysis]] can mistake patterns for causes without dismissing every trading method. [[ep80-yu-chali-mangge-de-kuashikong-duihua-dang-yanjing-shiming-shi-women-kanjian-shenme-lh4q7vpcfqoynssw23dbwcerkn7i]]
- **Crisis tests:** In the EP80 account of [[AmericanExpress]]' salad-oil scandal, the question is whether cardholders, merchants, hotels and restaurants still trust its network, rather than whether the stock price fell. The episode links Munger's eye loss and its host's glaucoma surgery to seeing incentives and business reality, not to an independently documented conversation. [[ep80-yu-chali-mangge-de-kuashikong-duihua-dang-yanjing-shiming-shi-women-kanjian-shenme-lh4q7vpcfqoynssw23dbwcerkn7i]]
- **Survivable exposure:** EP80 uses an early real-estate setback involving leverage, delayed projects, financing changes and illiquidity to warn about irreversible errors; [[ZhuNing]] invokes Munger with [[WarrenBuffett]] as a slow-compounding, no-ruin reference, preferring [[PositionSizing]] to binary market calls. [[ep80-yu-chali-mangge-de-kuashikong-duihua-dang-yanjing-shiming-shi-women-kanjian-shenme-lh4q7vpcfqoynssw23dbwcerkn7i]] [[paomo-de-si-ge-biyao-bu-chongfen-tiaojian-duitan-jingjixuezhe-zhu-ning-jiaoshou-lo4xhk5fnw1dcorwacttcsfjjr78]]
- **Personal limits:** [[DavidWeng]] applies Munger-style weakness recognition to preparation for poor memory, seven to eight hours' sleep rather than short-sleep bravado, and even the control and planning costs of strong willpower. This is Weng's analogy, alongside his [[HabitPositiveFeedback|habit]] reflections, not Munger's own prescription. [[154-sishisui-ganyan-bu-zuo-nazhi-wenshuili-de-qingwa-lg-qj3p2lq-8sk-rn2vk209tq9x]]

## Qualifications
EP80 is an imagined cross-time dialogue: its staged words, emotional response to blindness and case interpretation must not be passed off as historical Munger quotations. EP86's accounting checklist belongs to its host, and the life applications to Weng and Zhu Ning. The financial-statement episode pairs a Buffett-oriented ROE/business-quality lens with the inversion frame; high ROE itself can reflect leverage. The cited investment examples are episode interpretations, not independently checked filings or a claim that every consumer brand has a moat.

## What Changed
- Inversion and business quality are treated as complementary tests rather than separate appearances in episodes.
- The self-knowledge and portfolio analogies are explicitly attributed to their speakers.
