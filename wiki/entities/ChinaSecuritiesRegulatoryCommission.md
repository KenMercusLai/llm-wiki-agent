## Overview
The China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) is the securities regulator appearing in five podcast accounts of A-share market development, structured funds, public-fund incentives, fund advisory and cross-border brokerage. These are source-dated interpretations, not CSRC notices.

## Current Profile
Across these accounts, supervision responds both to risks exposed by trading and to incentive structures that influence whether investor protection works. Exchange rules, fund-industry practice and multi-agency enforcement must not all be attributed to a single CSRC directive.

## Key Characteristics
- Market supervision developed after exchange trading began and reacted to fraud, speculative cycles and leverage.
- Structured-fund restrictions responded to retail losses and complexity, with exchange rules and public-fund policy playing different roles.
- Public-fund reform must address distribution incentives and holder outcomes as well as manager pay and fees.
- Fund-advisory pilot architecture affects trust through duties, transparent charges, suitability and conflicts.
- Cross-border securities supervision intersects with foreign-exchange, internet, data and public-security enforcement.

## Evidence
- **Market formation and leverage:** EP46 depicts early trading around [[ShanghaiStockExchange]] as preceding mature supervision; it associates fraud response, 1996's “twelve gold medals,” later price-limit arrangements and the 2014–2015 off-market financing cleanup with efforts to cool excess without extinguishing confidence. Cleaning up fragile leverage can also accelerate forced selling; [[PolicyDrivenMarketRally]] does not guarantee improved earnings. [[ep46-lici-niushi-zhongshengxiang-cuoshoubuji-de-xingfu-neng-chixu-duojiu-li10xwkla-3kk-zgde2i0uxbpot2]]
- **Structured products:** Vol.121 describes [[ChineseStructuredFund|A/B structured funds]] whose [[StructuredFundDownwardConversion|downward conversion]], price limits and premium collapse badly hurt B-share buyers in the 2015 reversal. It distinguishes 2016 investor-threshold rules issued by [[ShanghaiStockExchange]] and [[ShenzhenStockExchange]] from subsequent anti-layering public-fund policy, with the product category exiting by 2020. [[vol-121-cong-tanhua-yixian-de-fenji-jijin-dao-fengtou-zhengjing-de-ganggan-etf-yongyuan-buyao-digu-renxing-de-fengkuang-lusagcitdozvzm8wausvecvi-qmb]]
- **Fund economics:** Vol.126 argues that channel trail commissions, C-share sales-service fees, pressured new issuance, redemption needs and [[ContractualFundGovernanceGap|limited holder governance]] shape the [[FundInvestorReturnGap]]. It questions whether blunt pay penalties for loss or benchmark underperformance would fix [[FundDistributionIncentives]], rather than simply drive departures or indexation. [[vol-126-gongmu-jijin-hai-zhide-mai-ma-lojrz1qctaqzkc2rg0lqokos-o6v]]
- **Advisory trust:** The episode dates the [[FundInvestmentAdvisory|fund-advisory]] pilot to 2019-10-24 and says unsettled top-level management rules persisted as of its 2025-11-04 publication. It argues [[BuySideInvestmentAdvisory|buy-side advice]] needs client goals beyond thin questionnaires, post-purchase support, [[FundAdvisoryFeeTransparency|visible fees]], [[FundRecommendationConflictDisclosure|conflict disclosure]] and a credible [[InvestmentAdviserFiduciaryDuty|duty framework]] rather than renamed distribution. Its approximate 60 licensed institutions, 5 million households and RMB 200 billion assets are source-dated estimates. [[145-jijin-tougu-zhide-xinren-ma-lrckug0zjqolcczni8ajikb0k5mi]]
- **Cross-border scope:** EP89 frames the May 22, 2026 cleanup involving [[FutuSecurities]], [[TigerBrokers]] and [[Longbridge]] as multi-agency securities-licensing and investor-protection action. It distinguishes overseas brokerage status from permission to solicit mainland investors and stresses that a personal USD 50,000 FX convenience quota is not authority to buy overseas securities. The episode describes a two-year sell-only treatment for some existing users, not an unrestricted new-buy route. [[ep89-haiwai-quanshang-da-dizhen-kuajing-touzi-xinshidai-li8ya-r5cpz3sifdjby73vh9-rxs]]

## Qualifications
The accounts are podcast interpretations rather than regulatory texts or independently checked enforcement records. Specifically, the 2016 structured-fund entry thresholds came from exchanges, not a direct CSRC command; the cross-border case also involves foreign-exchange and other agencies. Fee-policy criticism is the interviewer's analysis, not an announced CSRC finding. The 2025 advisory and 2026 cross-border positions should not be extrapolated as current rules. Regulation cannot replace investor [[InvestmentRiskManagement|leverage and entry discipline]].

## What Changed
- Separate exchange product rules from CSRC and other agencies' oversight instead of collapsing them into one regulator action.
- Connect investor protection to fund-channel and advisory incentives, not only market-cycle interventions.
