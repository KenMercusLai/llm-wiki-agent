## Overview

段熲 / Duan Jiong is a veteran late-Han frontier commander whose current wiki profile now begins with Guo Hong's obstruction and wrongful punishment before later restoration, Western Qiang victory, hard-line anti-Qiang policy, political entanglement, and ethical critique.

## Current Profile

The earliest layer is [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]]. When [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]], [[ShendiQiang|沈氐羌]], and other Qiang groups attack Bingzhou and [[LiangzhouLateHan|凉州]], the court sends Duan Jiong with the Five Camps. [[GuoHongLateHan|郭闳]], the Liangzhou inspector, wants a share of the military merit, obstructs Duan's movement, and delays the chance to respond. Hu soldiers serving far from home desert during the delay; Guo Hong then blames Duan, and Duan is recalled, imprisoned, and sent to Left School hard labor.

Hanji 859 turns that punishment into the prehistory of restoration. Western-region officials and civilians plead for Duan at the palace gate, and strengthening Qiang pressure in Liangzhou forces the court to reappoint him as Protector of the Qiang. The page therefore now starts with a damaged command chain before it reaches the later public-demand and emergency-practicality layer.

The main military profile then intensifies. Hanji 867 presents Western Qiang victory, and Hanji 880-883 extends the portrait through battlefield leadership, campaign finance, and an increasingly harsh anti-Qiang line. Hanji 891-904 places him within promotion, court factional danger, and the later costs of coercive power. Hanji 915 and 917 preserve the afterlife of that model: rapid breakthrough and strong leadership can solve one problem while leaving moral and political damage unresolved.

The resulting profile is deliberately ambivalent. Duan is not a mere villain or simple savior; he is a commander whose tactical skill, endurance with soldiers, and frontier credibility make him indispensable under pressure, while his hard-line method and later political entanglements make him a warning about what emergency usefulness can license.

## Key Characteristics

- Frontier commander whose first current layer shows operational delay, blame-shifting, imprisonment, and hard-labor punishment.
- Restored to office after local officials and civilians petition for him during renewed Qiang pressure.
- Associated with Qiang and Liangzhou campaigns where emergency conditions favor proven military capacity.
- Tactically aggressive and capable of rapid battlefield breakthroughs.
- Shares hardship and command discipline in ways that can strengthen military credibility.
- Pursues a hard-line anti-Qiang policy that attracts later ethical critique.
- Moves from frontier usefulness into court and factional risk, where coercive skill becomes politically dangerous.

## Qualifications

The page remains bounded to the listed podcast source notes and does not resolve all historical debates about Duan Jiong's full biography or policy legacy. Transcript forms including "段颎" and "段炯" are normalized to 段熲 because the Protector of the Qiang, Liangzhou, and later Duan Jiong sequence match the established late-Han frontier commander. The obstruction, desertion, punishment, false-accusation, and palace-gate petition details are kept as source-scoped claims.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 855 as the new earliest layer: Guo Hong's obstruction, desertion, blame-shifting, imprisonment, and hard labor.
- Reframed Hanji 859's petition and restoration as a comeback after the Hanji 855 punishment layer.
- Expanded transcript normalization to include both "段颎" and "段炯".
