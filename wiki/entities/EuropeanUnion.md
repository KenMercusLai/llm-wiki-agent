# European Union

## Overview
The European Union is a shared market and regulatory institution whose integration is experienced through mobility, exchange and cross-border firms, yet whose national languages, budgets, borders and security dependencies limit unified action.

## Current Profile
Sources contrast the scale of EU rules for data, platforms and AI with fragmented industrial and political capacity. Border coordination, Britain's exit, climate adaptation and external supply/security pressure show different consequences of that shared-but-incomplete governance; not every European observation is evidence of an EU policy.

## Key Characteristics
- Shared mobility and industry coexist with language, class and housing tensions.
- Digital privacy, content and AI rules project market power across borders while facing speech and innovation objections.
- Border solidarity is conditional on national enforcement and diplomatic conflicts.
- Brexit shows simultaneous regulatory flexibility and trade/compliance friction.
- Energy, EV and AI industrial ambitions are constrained by uneven country conditions and security dependencies.
- External trade and illicit-traffic concerns require allies and enforcement beyond a single-country frame.

## Qualifications
- Member states differ in immigration, grid mix, languages and housing; European regional comparisons cannot all be attributed to the EU institution. The EU is not identical to Schengen, the eurozone, NATO or all of Europe.
- Rogers's “censorship tariff” is a participant's position; investigations are not infringement findings. AI-marketing disclosure summaries may simplify legal scope and timing; specific text watermark rollout is Anthropic's product response, not necessarily an EU-mandated method.
- The allied bloc, AI translation fix and regulatory dividends are proposals/expectations. The 2035 car-policy easing was planned in the reported snapshot, not a blanket end to electric-vehicle policy. West African transit warning describes a possible security consequence.

## What Changed
- Separates regulatory spillovers, lived integration, border coordination and source-dated forecasts; preserves the distinction between the EU institution and adjacent regional cases.
