## Overview

张奂 / Zhang Huan is a late-Han official, scholar, and frontier figure whose current wiki profile joins 167 CE frontier victory and reward distortion, frontier conciliation, compromised military use, post-coup guilt, direct remonstrance, and survival under pressure.

## Current Profile

Zhang Huan's profile is no longer only a remonstrance and survival case. Hanji 878 gives an earlier frontier-success and reward-gatekeeping layer: Zhang defeats [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] forces attacking Sanfu, but the expected marquisate is reduced because he refuses to flatter eunuchs. He refuses the reduced reward and asks instead to move his household registration to Hongnong. Hanji 883 then shows his frontier-policy side before the anti-eunuch aftermath: as Protector of the Xiongnu, he urges the court to accept surrender and peace from remaining [[DongqiangLateHan|东羌]] groups because elimination is difficult and [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]]'s hard line risks overreach. Duan answers by accusing him of exaggerating the Qiang threat and resenting Duan's success, making Zhang a policy rival in the Qiang field before he becomes a court-danger figure.

Hanji 887 places Zhang in the military reversal that destroys Dou Wu: he returns to Luoyang without full information, the eunuch side uses forged imperial authority and Zhou Jing's temporary command, and Zhang Huan moves with the remaining Five Camps troops into the confrontation. This helps explain why Hanji 889 treats his later marquisate as morally compromised rather than merely honorable.

Hanji 889 and Hanji 890 show the repair attempt after that compromised use. Zhang refuses the title, uses omen and loyalty language to ask for burial and relief around Dou Wu, Chen Fan, and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]], and then joins [[LiuMengLateHan|刘猛]] in recommending [[WangChangLateHan|王畅]] and [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]]. Hanji 902 shows a later and harsher risk environment: after [[WangYuLateHan|王寓]]'s accusation and Duan's pressure, Zhang survives by lowering his posture, withdrawing from office, teaching, and writing.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhang Huan can win frontier campaigns, but his reward can still be discounted by eunuch-side gatekeeping.
- His refusal of a reduced reward turns military honor into a statement against distorted court access.
- Zhang Huan is presented as hostile to eunuch-side injustice but vulnerable to being used by the side he opposes.
- His frontier policy favors surrender and peace when residual non-Han forces are hard to eliminate cleanly.
- His compromised military role in 168 CE precedes his refusal of a later reward, making guilt and reputation repair central to his profile.
- He uses omen and loyalty language to reopen dangerous anti-eunuch grievances around Dou Wu, Chen Fan, and Empress Dowager Dou.
- His later humbled letter and post-office writing turn survival into preserved agency rather than mere escape.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 887 and Hanji 889 make Zhang's knowledge and intention source-scoped: the stable claim is that he was used by the eunuch side, not that he deliberately supported the anti-Dou purge.
- Hanji 889 and Hanji 890 render the name as "张宦"; this page normalizes to 张奂 because the role, office context, and existing late-Han pattern match Zhang Huan.
- Hanji 878 also renders the name as "张宦"; this page normalizes that occurrence to 张奂 because the frontier role, anti-eunuch reward pattern, and existing route match Zhang Huan.
- Hanji 883 renders the name as 张焕; this page normalizes to 张奂 because the Protector of the Xiongnu role and Duan Jiong dispute match the established route.
- The current page is still episode-bounded and should not be treated as a full biography of Zhang's frontier service.
- The survival reading should not erase Zhang's earlier directness; the page intentionally keeps both the exposed remonstrant and the humbled survivor in one profile.

## What Changed

- Hanji 878 adds an earlier 167 CE military victory and reward-gatekeeping layer before Zhang's later conciliation and anti-eunuch-remonstrance arc.
- The page now distinguishes two reward refusals: Hanji 878's refusal of a reduced reward after frontier success and Hanji 889's refusal of a morally compromised marquisate after the Dou / Chen crisis.
- Added Hanji 883's frontier-conciliation policy before the anti-eunuch court evidence.
