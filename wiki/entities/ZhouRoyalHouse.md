# 周王室 / Zhou Royal House

## Overview
The Warring States [[ZhouRoyalHouse]] retained investiture, ritual and chronicle authority while losing coercive capacity and territory. These functions were neither an intact empire nor merely empty names: rivals still sought the court's symbolic recognition, used its capital as a meeting place, and eventually absorbed its remaining domains.

## Current Profile
This account follows the decline from the formal recognition of the Three Jin through the separate endings of [[WesternZhouPolity]] and [[EasternZhouPolity]]. A reign-date in [[ZizhiTongjian]] is a chronological label, not evidence that the king controlled the state armies described under it.

The investiture took place against the [[EarlyWarringStatesInterstateWar]] and eventually the disappearance of the old [[JinJinggong]] line; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] connects royal recognition to the [[Fengze]] meeting ([[ZhouHegemonRecognition]]). [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]] identifies [[MiHeng]] in the Chu persuasion context, while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] invokes the weakness of [[HezongAlliance]] coordination.

## Key Characteristics
- Royal investiture and insignia could ratify power already accumulated by regional states.
- The court and its [[Luoyi]] setting retained diplomatic and ritual value despite diminished command.
- Territorial contraction and the [[EasternWesternZhouSplit]] separated the kingly institution from small successor polities.
- Symbolic deterrence could avert an attack but could not reliably finance a late anti-Qin coalition.
- Qin absorption of the western and eastern remnants ended both material and ritual continuity in stages.

## Evidence
- **Recognition without monopoly.** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-04-yurang-wei-zhiyao-fuchou-ln34tydha6iqwlrgmw7iugqfgfik]] describes [[ZhouWeilieWang]]'s elevation of [[WeiWenhou]], [[ZhaoLiehou]] and [[HanJinghou]] to lords, bypassing residual [[JinState]] authority ([[ThreeJinVassalRecognition]], [[PartitionOfJin]]). [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] mark the transition through [[ZhouAnWang]] to [[ZhouLiewang]] around 402 and 376 BCE; these date markers are not separate victories. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]] has [[ZhouXianwang]] grant [[FufuRobe]] after [[QinXianGong]]'s [[ShimenBattle]], while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] records recognition of [[QinXiaogong]] as hegemon in 343 BCE. In each case [[RitualRecognitionOfMilitaryPower]] follows others' force, not royal military supremacy.
- **The court as a remaining diplomatic institution.** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-10-yi-jiazu-danwei-you-mayi-chi-daxiang-de-jingshen-1-lq3t7lwpmq1oyb8zmmmn2tywnu8d]] supplements a missing 374 BCE annal entry in [[ZizhiTongjian]] with [[Shiji]]'s Taishi Dan story: [[TaishiDan]] appeals to Qin through old Zhou-Qin ties ([[KinshipLegitimacyDiplomacy]]). The host's reading of his motive is interpretation, and Shiji is a text discussed by the registered podcast note, not a separately registered source. In 344 BCE [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] says lords gathered at Luoyi but distinguishes [[AudienceVsMeetingDiplomacy]] from a genuine royal audience; [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-72-shenme-yang-de-ren-cai-shi-zhongzhen-zhishi-logxegnqexgc7z-lodw6tr6-8f28]] again locates [[QinZhaoxiangwang]], [[WeiZhaowang]] and [[HanXiwang]] at the Zhou capital. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-2-lhngst3xsnyjrmzh1cvheocvvsqm]] describes [[SongState]] and [[WeiziQi]] as an older Shang line ritually accommodated under Zhou, a retrospective precedent for symbolic incorporation rather than a policy executed by these late kings.
- **Court and domain are not interchangeable.** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]]'s [[WangziDing]] exile illustrates princely vulnerability, not a recovered realm. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] describes an attack by [[HanState]] on the [[EasternZhouPolity]] after the royal lands split into small eastern and western polities; this is territorial contraction, not proof that every royal ceremony ended then. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-40-5-yue-chusheng-de-haizi-re-shui-la-ll9e-thidxl5cqoacfrbz-ygw8-t]]'s brief 321 BCE succession from Xian to [[ZhouShenliangwang]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]]'s reign of [[ZhouNanwang]] in the 320–298 BCE frame are chronicle markers, while [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]] tells of other states' wars. None proves command over those states.
- **Symbolic protection turns into an obligation trap.** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]] has [[DongzhouWugong]] argue against a contemplated [[ChuState]] attack on the small royal domain by invoking the Son of Heaven and [[Jiuding]]: Chu would gain little land but risk appearing illegitimate and drawing retaliation ([[ZhouRoyalSymbolicDeterrence]]). [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]] reports [[ZhangYiStrategist]] pitching [[QinWuwang]] an advance through [[SanchuanWarringStates]] toward royal maps and registers while Qi and Wei were distracted. The note interprets that state-benefit pitch as [[SelfPreservationAsStateStrategy]] for Zhang Yi after his patron's death; it is not a completed seizure or a Zhou court strategy. By [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] [[Chunshenjun]] and [[MiWan]] seek anti-[[QinState]] mobilization under [[ZhouNanwang]]'s name, but the lords do not deliver effective help; debt and [[ZhaitaiGaozhu]] turn nominal authority into [[NominalAuthorityMobilizationTrap]].
- **Two terminal episodes, not one collapse date.** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] describes flight, removal of treasures to [[Xianyang]], and displacement of the western lord after Qin's western conquest. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-1-lvbuwei-shi-jiangziya-houren-lveg-wsmggroaxjsnxopaevexqod]] attributes the final eastern attack to [[QinZhuangxiangwang]] and [[LuBuwei]] after an eastern plot, with a surviving ruler relocated and royal sacrifices said to end. Distinguish the royal house from its two territorial shells and keep the podcast's terminal-sacrifice wording attributed.

## Qualifications
- Several registered notes mainly date a reign ([[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-45-yi-shi-wei-jian-junwang-daodi-gai-bu-gai-sharen-chengxing-lo3cok7cfwzrxesp-i8miz1fc1db]], [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-40-5-yue-chusheng-de-haizi-re-shui-la-ll9e-thidxl5cqoacfrbz-ygw8-t]], [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-11-shijie-fengqi-yunyong-1-lt60b2gddiosf5kade35uopwohi5]], [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]], [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-09-zhanguo-shidai-de-shijie-dazhan-losmum91vjpqfymywelj60o7-vbl]]), or tell stories of unrelated state politics; they do not independently attest royal coercive power. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]]'s Qin plan and [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]]'s outreach are intentions or reported interpretation, not an achieved conquest or restored Zhou supremacy.
- The Zhou-origin story involving [[ZhouWenwang]], [[ZhouWuwang]], [[JiangTaigong]] and [[QiState]] in [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-102-1-lvbuwei-shi-jiangziya-houren-lveg-wsmggroaxjsnxopaevexqod]] is retrospective background for a different era; [[PosthumousNamePolitics]] and [[MandateOfHeavenLegitimacy]] are interpretive frames, not additional Warring States institutions proven by these notes.

## What Changed
- The chronology is now organized around investiture, court venue, territorial contraction, symbolic risk and two distinct Qin endpoints.

## Relationships
- [[ThreeJinVassalRecognition]] - formal royal recognition of states whose armies already commanded territory.
- [[EasternWesternZhouSplit]] - distinguishes the shrinking royal line from two late territorial polities.
- [[ZhouRoyalSymbolicDeterrence]] - nominal status could make an attack diplomatically costly even without a strong army.
- [[NominalAuthorityMobilizationTrap]] - invoking the royal name could incur debt without obtaining allied forces.
- [[QinState]] - the regional power that ultimately absorbed the western and eastern remnants.
- [[ShangRemnantStateLegitimacy]] - Song and Weizi Qi illustrate older ritual accommodation, not effective late Zhou command.
- [[AristocraticPoliticalAsylum]] - Wangzi Ding’s exile shows a prince could seek survival beyond the royal domain.
- [[TwelveInsigniaPoliticalSymbolism]] - the robe bestowed after Shimen was a rank signal rather than battlefield command.
