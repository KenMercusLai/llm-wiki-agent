## Overview

施丹 / Shi Dan enters the wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-626-waren-fenmu-wangmang-jing-ruci-sangxinbingkuang-lotwk6rbtl5pvbrnlfrysu5ol6rz|Hanji 626]] as a Western Han official retrospectively rewarded after [[WangMang|王莽]]'s posthumous prosecution of [[FuTaihouWesternHan|傅太后]] and [[DingJiWesternHan|丁姬]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-580-huigu-sizhou-shuishi-nide-kaoshan-liid3tymhojmajkyeqoq6vryprhz|Hanji 580]] backfills the earlier penalty context by saying [[ZhuBoWesternHan|朱博]] and [[ZhaoXuanWesternHan|赵璇]] had just persuaded [[HanAidi|汉哀帝]] to remove Shi Dan's title and fief before they turned against Wang Mang. His role is narrow but useful: he shows how one court judgment can be reversed when later ritual-political memory changes.

## Current Profile

Hanji 580 says Shi Dan's title and fief are removed before Zhu Bo and Zhao Xuan turn the same punitive logic against Wang Mang. Hanji 626 later says Shi Dan had previously opposed Fu Taihou and had been removed from office and stripped of ducal standing. After Wang Mang's 5 CE campaign against Fu Taihou and Ding Ji, Shi Dan is summoned to Chang'an, made a guannei marquis, restored to his former income, later made Yiyang marquis, and then dies soon afterward.

Shi Dan therefore functions as a posthumous-merit-vindication case. The reward does not come from a new achievement in the episode; it comes from Wang Mang recoding an earlier stance as correct once the court officially condemns Fu Taihou's old status.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han official whose earlier opposition to Fu Taihou becomes retroactively rewarded.
- Earlier target whose title and fief are removed before the attempted attack on Wang Mang.
- Example of Wang Mang turning posthumous ritual correction into living political sorting.
- Restored and re-titled shortly before his death.
- Narrow source figure rather than a fully synthesized statesman profile.

## Qualifications

Hanji 580 only names Shi Dan as the immediately preceding punishment case before the Wang Mang attack. Hanji 626 does not reconstruct the full earlier debate in which Shi Dan opposed Fu Taihou. The page therefore treats him as source-scoped evidence for removal and later retrospective reward, not as a complete biography or independent judgment on the earlier dispute.

- Added Hanji 580's earlier title-and-fief removal as the prehistory to Hanji 626's later restoration.
