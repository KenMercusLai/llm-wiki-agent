# Russia

## Overview
Russia appears in these source notes across distinct historical and contemporary contexts: imperial exploration, the war in Ukraine, European security, sanctions, intelligence, and infrastructure governance. These contexts should not be treated as one undifferentiated policy claim.

## Current Profile
The eighteenth-century expedition account describes North Pacific logistical expansion. Recent reporting describes Ukraine war pressure and European deterrence, alongside narrower discussions of drones, cables, crypto sanctions, Greenland and lunar governance.

## Key Characteristics
- **Historical expansion:** The Bering expedition account links imperial ambitions to difficult Siberian logistics and North Pacific extraction.
- **War and European security:** Accounts of drones, attacks, export routes and NATO grey-zone pressure describe distinct present-war mechanisms and uncertain future threats.
- **Transnational strategic exposure:** Cable security, a Russia-related stablecoin, Arctic geography, and lunar-rule nonparticipation are separate source-scoped concerns, not a single demonstrated operation.

## Evidence
- **Historical reach and intelligence afterlives:** In the eighteenth century, the [[RussianEmpire]] under [[PeterTheGreat]] sought a maritime outlet through [[VitusBering]]’s expeditions: [[ImperialExplorationLogistics|hauling shipbuilding materials across Siberia]] toward [[Okhotsk]] and [[Kamchatka]] preceded routes through [[BeringStrait]] and [[BeringSea]] toward [[Alaska]], where exploration enabled extraction, colonial violence and ecological damage as well as science. In an entirely different Soviet/post-Soviet intelligence context, the [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] account of [[OlegGordievsky|Oleg Gordievsky]] after [[OperationPimlico]] cites later attacks on Russian defectors to explain why escape from the [[KGB]] did not remove his perceived threat in the [[UnitedKingdom]]; neither account directly explains today’s war. - **War pressure and European security:** A discussion of European integration argues that energy interdependence did not prevent the Russia–[[Ukraine]] war; [[DanielFranklin]] likewise sees Russian assertiveness as a reason for a British defence role after [[Brexit]]. [[AntonLaGuardia]] separates present [[RussianHybridPressure]]—drone and airspace incursions, suspected undersea sabotage and disinformation below a clear NATO response threshold—from the forecast that a battle-hardened army might later threaten European states; [[EuropeanDefenseAutonomy|European autonomy]] and alliance credibility also depend on American command, intelligence, satellites and precision strike. Russian use of [[Shahed136]]-style armed drones and cheap decoys increases [[DroneDecoyEconomics|defensive costs]]. Ukrainian strikes on [[Crimea]], Moscow and oil infrastructure aim to make war costs visible, although the reporting says state spending and oil revenue still fund the conflict. [[AndreyMelnichenko]], a sanctioned industrialist rather than a democratic opposition figure, portrays [[VladimirPutin]]’s options as escalation and repression or de-escalation and greater inclusion. [[OliverCarroll]] reports Russian strikes on [[Kyiv]] and Black Sea export routes, ships, warehouses and civilian businesses combine [[AirDefenseSaturation]], scarce [[PatriotMissileSystem|Patriot]] interceptors and [[UkraineExportChokepoint|economic disruption]]; Ukrainian command reform cannot remove those pressures alone. ## Qualifications
Cable incidents and Russian-linked vendors are discussed as concerns, not proven Russian sabotage. Later-war risk is a forecast distinct from current hybrid pressure; oligarch remarks and crypto-activity estimates remain attributed to their speakers. The lunar note describes non-signature, not an established imminent conflict.

## What Changed
- The current profile groups the original source-scoped observations by role and mechanism rather than source arrival; no new source or later event is asserted.
