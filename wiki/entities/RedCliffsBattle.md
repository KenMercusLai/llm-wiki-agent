# 赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs

## Overview
赤壁之战 / Battle of Red Cliffs is the late-Han river battle in which the Sun-Liu side defeats [[CaoCao|曹操]]'s southern expedition and forces a retreat from the middle Yangtze.

## Current Profile
The current bounded source focuses on the battle's final fire-attack and immediate aftermath. [[HuangGaiLateHan|黄盖]] uses feigned surrender to bring fire ships close to the Cao fleet; wind spreads the fire into the ships and Wulin-side camp; and [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] follows with a direct attack. Cao Cao withdraws, destroys remaining ships and supplies, and leaves [[CaoRen|曹仁]] and [[XuHuangCaoGeneral|徐晃]] to hold the Jiangling-Fancheng line.

The battle does not end political competition. Cao Cao disputes the victors' account by emphasizing disease, deliberate ship burning, and withdrawal while minimizing Zhou Yu's credit. The same aftermath opens a contest over [[JingzhouLateHan|荆州]], so the alliance that defeats Cao Cao also acquires internal distribution problems between [[SunQuan|孙权]] and [[LiuBei|刘备]].

## Key Characteristics
- River battle resolved in this account through feigned surrender, fire, wind, and follow-on assault.
- Major check on Cao Cao's attempt to extend control south of the Yangtze.
- Retreat followed by deliberate denial of ships and supplies to the victors.
- Immediate transition from battlefield defeat into corridor defense around Jiangling and Fancheng.
- Political meaning contested through rival explanations of disease, fire, agency, and military credit.
- Alliance victory that creates a new dispute over Jingzhou's territory and revenue.

## Qualifications
This page is bounded to one popular-history episode and is not yet a complete military history of Red Cliffs. Exact troop losses, the relative causal weight of disease and fire, wind detail, route detail, the “three great battles” ranking, and the claim that Cao Cao's psychology was decisive all require broader evidence. Disease and self-destruction of materiel can coexist with an enemy-caused defeat; the contested issue is how the episode allocates cause and credit.

## What Changed
- Created the event page from Hanji 1107's fire-attack and retreat account.
- Recorded the shift from battle to corridor defense and alliance competition.
- Preserved the disease-versus-fire explanation as a qualified narrative dispute.

## Relationships
- [[HuangGaiLateHan|黄盖]] - commander whose feigned surrender enables the close-range fire attack.
- [[ZhouYuLateHan|周瑜]] - Sun-side commander credited with the fire attack and follow-on offensive.
- [[CaoCao|曹操]] - defeated northern commander who retreats, denies materiel, and contests the victory narrative.
- [[SunQuan|孙权]] - ruler whose coalition wins and who receives Cao Cao's reputational counter-message.
- [[LiuBei|刘备]] - allied actor whose post-battle territorial interests diverge from Sun Quan's.
- [[JianglingLateHan|江陵]] - downstream defensive strongpoint and next campaign focus.
- [[JingzhouLateHan|荆州]] - territorial prize that turns common victory into a distribution problem.
- [[PostDefeatNarrativeCounterattack|败后叙事反击]] - mechanism by which Cao Cao tries to limit the political consequences of defeat.
