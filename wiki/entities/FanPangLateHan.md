## Overview

Fan Pang is a late-Han party-prohibition figure whose current profile now begins inside the first Party Prohibition prison case. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] shows him shackled and interrogated by [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]], defending mutual moral esteem as the pursuit of good rather than criminal faction. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-877-ta-weihe-lengluo-jiuming-enren-loofs4rugl-q8kunfb-jpj5mgvhi|Hanji 877]] then shows release without rehabilitation through registry exclusion, tacit gratitude toward [[HuoXuLateHan|霍谞]], and retreat from dangerous public attention. Hanji 892-893 carry that reputation into later honor-list visibility, voluntary imprisonment, non-implication of helpers, and family farewell.

## Current Profile

Fan Pang's current evidence forms a four-stage profile: imprisoned moral defendant, released but politically disabled figure, visible reputation holder, and death-facing party victim. Hanji 876 supplies the first stage. Wang Fu asks why the prisoners mutually praise one another and behave like a "party"; Fan Pang answers through a good-and-evil distinction, presenting their association as ethical orientation rather than conspiratorial self-interest. His defense moves Wang Fu enough to remove heavy restraints from the prisoners, but it does not by itself end the political case.

Hanji 877 supplies the immediate aftermath. The amnesty frees Fan Pang from prison but leaves him inside [[RegistryBasedPoliticalExclusion|登记造册式政治禁锢]]: the party figures are sent home, registered, and barred from future office. His visit to Huo Xu is read through [[TacitGratitudeRestraint|心照不宣式感恩克制]] rather than ingratitude, while his discomfort with a large Runan reception shows how public fame remains dangerous after formal release.

Hanji 892 and Hanji 893 explain why that fame later matters. Fan Pang's name belongs to the士人 reputation field that turns into [[ReputationListPersecutionTarget|名望榜单迫害目标化]]. When the second party-prohibition disaster arrives, he recognizes the arrest order, goes to prison voluntarily, refuses [[GuoYiLateHan|郭揖]]'s escape plan to avoid implicating him, and accepts that a life of public名节 may not coexist with survival.

## Key Characteristics

- First Party Prohibition prisoner who defends moral association as learning from good and rejecting evil.
- Released party figure whose amnesty does not restore office eligibility or ordinary safety.
- Tacitly grateful actor who visits Huo Xu without turning rescue into public repayment.
- Reputation figure whose name and reception visibility become dangerous before the second crackdown.
- Party figure whose name appears in the arrest field created by [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]]'s framing.
- Voluntary prisoner who recognizes the arrest order and goes to prison instead of hiding.
- Non-implicating son and father who refuses to transfer danger to helpers or give his child a simple survival rule.

## Qualifications

This page is grounded in Hanji 876, Hanji 877, Hanji 892, and Hanji 893 and still does not reconstruct Fan Pang's full career outside those source windows. Hanji 876's claim that Wang Fu is moved by Fan Pang is preserved as an early prison-scene layer and does not erase Wang Fu's later role in eunuch-side repression. Transcript variants such as 范胖 are normalized to 范滂, and Hanji 877's Spring-Autumn allusion names remain source-scoped.

## What Changed

- Hanji 876 adds Fan Pang's pre-release prison defense before Hanji 877's amnesty and registry aftermath.
- Wang Fu now appears in Fan Pang's profile as an early moved interrogator, while later sources keep the broader eunuch repression field intact.
- The current profile links Fan Pang's moral defense, tacit gratitude, low-visibility conduct, honor-list exposure, and voluntary imprisonment as one reputation-risk arc.
