# 王允 / Wang Yun (late Han)

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-966-meiyou-yexin-budong-quanmou-jiu-bie-dang-lingdao-lldn3hi1gl5v3axvklwakgf6vx3u|Hanji 966]] backfills Wang Yun's final crisis before Hanji 967's hidden-merit contrast. After [[JiaXu|贾诩]]'s advice sends [[LiJueLateHan|李傕]] and other [[DongZhuo|董卓]] remnant commanders toward [[ChangAnHanCapital|长安]], Wang Yun tries to use [[HuWencaiLateHan|胡文才]] and [[YangZhengxiuLateHan|杨整修]] as Liangzhou influence channels but mishandles the appeal through arrogance. Once Chang'an falls, he refuses [[LyuBu|吕布]]'s invitation to flee because he will not abandon [[EmperorXianOfHan|汉献帝]].

The episode therefore gives Wang Yun a double shape. He shows genuine public loyalty at the end, but his earlier crisis judgment is treated as weak: he fails to ask useful intermediaries properly, fails to preserve outside military leverage, and becomes dependent on [[SongYiLateHan|宋翼]] and [[WangHongLateHan|王宏]] after Li-Guo forces have captured the court. [[ZhaoJianLateHan|赵戬]]'s later burial of his body preserves Wang Yun as an object of personal loyalty after the political position has collapsed.

Wang Yun is presented in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-967-ta-canyu-mousha-dongzhuo-jingneng-taoguo-sishen-weilie-lv24biyswj9ljdld5htynhauhph3|Hanji 967]] as the public owner of the anti-[[DongZhuo|Dong Zhuo]] deed after Dong Zhuo's killing. The episode contrasts his visible credit with [[ShiSunRuiLateHan|Shi Sunrui]]'s concealed participation: Wang Yun's prominence helps keep Shi Sunrui outside the immediate political spotlight, but it also makes Wang Yun the focal figure of the post-Dong-Zhuo settlement.

The host's source-scoped evaluation is severe: Wang Yun is treated as lacking political wisdom after success, becoming another high-centered authority figure rather than stabilizing the court. In this framing, his credit concentration illustrates why late-Han merit could become a liability when it placed a person too visibly at the center of revenge politics.
