# 芈完 / 楚考烈王 / Mi Wan

## Overview
芈完 / 楚考烈王 is the [[ChuState|Chu]] crown prince who returned from [[QinState|Qin]] [[HostageDiplomacyRupture|hostage status]] amid [[WarringStatesAllianceSwitching|shifting Chu–Qin alignments]] and ruled during mounting Qin pressure. The late succession story is contested: one source calls him childless, while a later genealogy-based episode argues he had multiple sons.

## Current Profile
His return depended on [[Chunshenjun|黄歇]]'s deception and negotiation; later accounts show coalition attempts, a defensive capital move and government through Huang Xie. The royal-lineage dispute must remain visible rather than be reconciled into a single certain pedigree.

## Key Characteristics
- Hostage status became urgent when his father became ill; escape enabled his accession.
- Anti-Qin coalition initiatives included both negotiation successes and failed mobilizations.
- Chu administration of conquered Lu/Lanling and a move to Shouchun occurred under his reign.
- His death triggered lethal court succession conflict, with incompatible accounts of parentage.

## Evidence
- **Hostage return and accession:** After nine years accompanying the prince in Qin, Huang Xie argues that [[QinZhaoxiangwang]] can gain a friendly Chu ruler by releasing him when [[MiHeng]] falls ill. Qin suspects a pretext and sends a teacher to verify; Huang Xie disguises Mi Wan as an envoy's driver while staying in Xianyang to claim the prince is ill. [[FanJu]] later persuades Qin to release Huang Xie rather than lose all future Chu leverage. Mi Heng dies three months after Mi Wan's return; the new ruler rewards Huang Xie with a chancellorship, Huai-region fief and Chunshenjun title. In his first regnal year, 262 BCE, Chu cedes large territory to Qin without battle. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-1-lmwf0q0-3hxb6d4hro8fdmc3zdxp]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-92-lishishang-di-yi-wei-zhuming-tiangou-shi-shui-2-lt1nwraiuuvpy2tzo-zwaoqsajzx]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-93-zai-mengli-cangzhe-toutian-gaiming-1-ljshdryvf9qgabtn-siontthzsok]]
- **Coalitions and court leverage:** During the [[Handan]] crisis [[Pingyuanjun]]'s negotiation stalls until [[MaoSui]] uses [[DignityBasedPersuasion|honor-based persuasion]] and invokes [[BaiQi]]'s capture of Ying, burning of Yiling and injury to Chu royal tombs; Mi Wan accepts the blood-oath alliance, though relief still depends on [[CoalitionTransitDependency|transit through Wei]] [[WeiState]]. Later he and Huang Xie try to mobilize lords using [[NominalAuthorityMobilizationTrap|nominal authority]], specifically [[ZhouRoyalHouse]]'s nominal prestige associated with [[ZhouNanwang]] after Qin's setback, but Zhou Nan Wang incurs [[ZhaitaiGaozhu|debt]] without an effective army. In the 241 BCE [[TerminalHezongFailure|last-hezong]] account in [[HezongAlliance]], Mi Wan is nominal leader and Huang Xie commands; the coalition fails. The competing [[PangNuan]] account should not be flattened into the same command list. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-96-maosui-zijian-2-ltlobdujy-hdipimfdupvwod2ech]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]]
- **Governance and defensive relocation:** After Chu occupies former [[LuState]] territory, Huang Xie appoints [[Xunzi]] to [[LanlingWarringStates]] as an attributed [[ConfucianIdealGovernance|scholar-governance]] example in [[OccupationPacificationStrategy|occupied-territory governance]]; the host dates a return to the magistracy to Mi Wan's twelfth year, 251 BCE, and says 238 BCE turmoil ends the position. The same source account has [[ZhuYingWarringStates]] argue that Qin's pressure via weakened Wei makes the old capital unsafe, leading to the move to [[ShouchunWarringStates]]. These are actions under Mi Wan's reign, not automatically his personal initiatives. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-2-mingming-yilian-baixiang-de-caize-ping-shenme-baixiang-lhfyb6hzcqkpbzdrwsxajb7hhmhe]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-107-zuihou-yici-hezong-faqin-fa-le-ge-jimo-lrzq1qks7z-oromd-5f-dgqjwjhw]]
- **Death and rival genealogies:** In the [[Shiji]]/[[Zhanguoce]]-style story, supposedly childless Mi Wan favors [[LiYuanSister]] while she is pregnant by Huang Xie, recognizes the boy as heir and gives [[LiYuanWarringStates]] court access. Seventeen days after Zhu Ying's warning, Mi Wan dies and Li Yuan ambushes Huang Xie; [[ChuYouwang]] succeeds, while the continuation mentions two sons of Li Yuan's sister. A later episode cites 《史记索隐》 on a Qin royal marriage during Mi Wan's hostage period and identifies [[ChangpingJun]] / 熊启 through [[PreQinSurnameClanIdentity]] as a son, with further lineage discussion of [[ChuFuchu]]; that directly challenges the childless premise and does not independently settle the son's exact parentage here. ## Qualifications
Huang Xie's initial loyalty and the later hidden-pregnancy account are source traditions, not verified access to anyone's private motives. Qinji 109's “无子” premise conflicts with Qinji 118-2's genealogy; [[TextualVariantPoliticalStakes]] remain and neither is silently preferred. The 241 BCE coalition has two incompatible commander traditions; Lanling prosperity and Mi Wan's role in appointments are host-framed, not proof of direct instruction.
