# 春秋时期 / Spring and Autumn Period

## Overview
The Spring and Autumn Period is presented through [[ZuoZhuan]] anecdotes and Qi state-building, not as a comprehensive period history.

## Current Profile
Across the registered episodes, ritualized aristocratic combat and constrained exile politics coexist with killing and displacement; [[GuanZhong]]’s Qi supplies a different strand of administrative and economic power. Later Warring States contrasts are the hosts’ interpretive frame.

## Key Characteristics
- Ritual, elite status, chariot skill and dramaturgy shaped some narrated warfare without turning actual violence into sport.
- Qi military examples connect [[SimaRangju]], [[YanYing]] and [[QiJinggong]] to discipline and morale rather than only aristocratic display.
- [[GuanZhong]] and [[QiHuanGong]] are portrayed as translating succession crisis into state capacity and hegemonic diplomacy.
- The Qi–Lu/Liang textile and Qi–Chu deer stories present economic disruption and food security as weapons, with figures left source-scoped.
- The early Warring States comparison identifies a claimed shift from lineage preservation toward factional murder, not a universal clean break.

## Evidence
- **Ritual did not make war a game.** The [[ZuoZhuan]] “sports meet” compares rule-conscious [[SongXiangGong]] and [[JinWenGong]] with exploits assigned to [[NangongChangwan]] (flight after regicide), [[CaiJi]] (a boat prank turned interstate pretext), injured [[WeiChou]] (jumping), [[GongziPengsheng]] (the killing of [[LuHuanGong]]), [[GaoGu]] (throwing), [[YangYouji]] (archery), and [[ShuliangHe]] and [[Confucius]] (strength and charioteering). These are illustrations of [[SpringAutumnWarfareRitual]] and the playful [[SportsAsHistoricalReadingFrame]], not evidence that humiliation, hostage-taking, revenge, displacement or killing were harmless. [[109-xianliao-zuozhuan-zhi-chunqiu-yundonghui-747508773]]
- **Command depended on discipline as well as rank.** [[YanYing]]’s recommendation brings the lower-born [[SimaRangju]] to [[QiJinggong]]; the episode compares his attention to discipline, morale and execution with [[WuQi]]’s shared hardship with troops. This military-command account qualifies any picture made exclusively of aristocratic display. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-06-weiwenhou-xuan-shui-dang-zaixiang-ne-2-lmfzo4sl9nd5kz3mrxdl1dl9efei]]
- **Qi’s succession crisis became a state-building opportunity.** After [[QiXiangGong]] and [[WenJiang]]’s scandal, [[LuHuanGong]]’s killing and [[GongsunWuzhi]]’s usurpation, [[GongziJiu]] lost to [[QiHuanGong]]; [[BaoShuya]] then recommended his friend and the winner’s former enemy [[GuanZhong]]. The episode credits Guan Zhong with [[HouseholdOccupationControl]], [[MilitaryCivilianIntegration]], penal redemption into arms, [[StateResourceMonopoly]] over resources including salt and iron, regulated 女闾 and arms provisioning: internal capacity underpinned [[ConcessionBasedHegemony]], associated with the 651 BCE Kuiqiu meeting. Its claims to institutional “firsts” are not independently established. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-2-qiongshe-jichi-de-zushiye-guanzhong-lo8uei2u1qwj9tszftel1b4lswoa]]
- **Economic pressure extended Qi’s power beyond the battlefield.** In the reported cloth scheme, Qi stimulated cloth production in [[LuState]] and [[LiangState]] and then reversed demand and shut trade after farming had been neglected, producing grain-price pressure and flight toward [[QiState]]. The deer-buying story likewise draws [[ChuState]] labor away from farming while Qi stores grain before closing the border. These episodes illustrate [[DemandInducedAgrarianDisruption]], [[GrainBorderCoercion]] and [[InterstateEconomicWarfare]] without independently measured magnitudes; the same account also attributes a constructive “first enrich the people” [[PeopleFirstEconomicStatecraft]] doctrine to Guan Zhong. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-75-3-gudai-de-jingji-zhanzheng-neng-you-duo-kepa-liybgoz8lspypax6dhuke2js0pxf]]
- **Political violence is a comparison, not a date-stamped rupture.** In the early Warring States [[ZhengState]]/[[ZiyangZheng]] story, the host contrasts factional killing with older [[AristocraticPoliticalAsylum]] and uses [[QinghuaJianXinian]] to explain an omission in [[ZizhiTongjian]]. Since this episode ends at part one, [[SpringAutumnToWarringStatesPoliticalViolence]] remains its provisional interpretive frame, not a universal law for either period. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-07-liangqi-cisha-1-lvkp-ju7pf5z5cz3thojv3s2fkyx]]

## Qualifications
The playful “games” metaphor is pedagogical, not a casualty estimate. Claims of state institutions’ first appearance and specific economic-war outcomes are episode narratives rather than independent institutional or quantitative verification. [[HorseAsCivilizationalInfrastructure]] is adjacent chariot background, not proof of any named battle. [[NaturalDisasterPoliticalOmen]] is another source-specific interpretive strand, not a period-wide fact.

## What Changed
- Ritualized battle conventions now sit alongside coercive casualties rather than standing for the entire period.
- Qi’s institution building and economic-pressure cases are distinguished from later Zheng elite-conflict transition.
