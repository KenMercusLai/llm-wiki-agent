## Overview

汉桓帝 / Emperor Huan of Han is currently a late-Han emperor whose wiki profile now spans his final harem and succession crisis, plus later posthumous memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] gives the living-reign endpoint: Huan favors [[TianShengLateHan|田胜]], installs [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]] as empress because of formal status, dies childless at thirty-six, and leaves the court to select [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. Later sources use Huan as a posthumous and memory node: his Xuanling burial anchors Dou Miao's status dispute, Ling compares himself with him, and [[LiGuLateHan|李固]]'s old opposition to Huan's accession remains part of later succession memory.

## Current Profile

Huan's current profile is not a full reign biography. The bounded sources make him a hinge between private desire, formal status, childless death, and posthumous legitimacy. Hanji 879 shows affection and rank splitting inside the palace: Tian Sheng is the favorite, but Dou Miao receives the empress title because her background is politically acceptable. Huan's death then converts private harem disorder into public succession pressure because he leaves no son.

After death, Huan matters as the ritual and comparison anchor around which later actors argue. Hanji 901 uses his tomb and empress relationship to decide whether Dou Miao should be buried with him rather than displaced by [[FengGuirenLateHan|冯贵人]]. Hanji 914 makes him the predecessor against whom Emperor Ling seeks validation. Hanji 929 recalls that Huan's own accession had been contested by Li Gu, making Huan a recurring late-Han succession-memory node rather than only a deceased ruler.

## Key Characteristics

- Huan's final court field separates personal favor from formal empress status.
- His childless death turns harem and body-politics into an immediate dynastic succession crisis.
- His posthumous status at Xuanling anchors Dou Miao's later burial legitimacy dispute.
- His reign remains a comparison object for Emperor Ling's self-image and advice reception.
- His own accession remains remembered as contested, especially through Li Gu's preference for Liu Suan.

## Evidence

- Final harem and succession crisis: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Huan favors Tian Sheng, appoints Dou Miao as empress because of status, dies on 167-12-28 at age thirty-six, and leaves no son.
- Posthumous burial legitimacy: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] says Li Xian and Chen Qiu argue that Dou Miao should share Huan's Xuanling burial and sacrificial status rather than be displaced by Feng Guiren.
- Predecessor comparison: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-914-xinren-de-ren-weihe-zong-shang-ni-zui-shen-lvwit-rzrkscn5a3h6wblntxppso|Hanji 914]] says Emperor Ling asks Yang Qi how he compares with Huan and reacts badly to an answer that does not flatter him.
- Earlier accession memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-929-duishi-buduiren-ni-xin-le-ma-lgaxj0jzopddmt3c5hibnexqlhb3|Hanji 929]] recalls that Li Gu had opposed establishing the later Emperor Huan and preferred Liu Suan.

## Qualifications

Hanji 879's claim that Huan's body was ruined by harem indulgence is preserved as the episode's moral and causal interpretation. The page should not infer a complete medical account or full reign evaluation from that source alone.

## What Changed

- Huan is no longer only a posthumous burial and comparison node; Hanji 879 adds his final living-reign harem and death sequence.
- The profile now ties Huan's childless death to the immediate accession path that produces Emperor Ling.
- Tian Sheng is now the page's source case for the gap between palace favor and formal protection.
