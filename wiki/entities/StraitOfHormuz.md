# Strait of Hormuz

## Overview
The Strait of Hormuz is a Gulf shipping chokepoint whose accessibility affects oil, insurance, regional confidence and diplomatic bargaining.

## Current Profile
The sources cover a 2026 Iran–U.S. conflict sequence: disruption, temporary diplomatic language, conflicting passage interpretations, alleged toll/permission procedures, rerouting and demand-side market adjustment. Traffic claims and figures are dated to those episodes.

## Key Characteristics
- Physical blockage and insurer/shipowner expectations can constrain trade even when governments call the passage open.
- Passage rules became a diplomatic conflict over who administers, authorizes and possibly charges ships.
- Oil-supply shocks can be partly offset by demand responses, stocks and alternative routes, without removing the chokepoint.
- The same concentration exposes Gulf finance and logistics but drives bypass and rerouting investments.
- Mines, fast craft and drones invite proposals for autonomous maritime persistence while exposing operational claims to scrutiny.

## Evidence
- **Disruption propagates through confidence as well as supply.** [[IranHorizontalEscalation]] describes how uncertain passage can raise marine insurance, halt Gulf plants and unsettle [[GulfStabilityRisk]] without an outright victory over the [[UnitedStates]] or [[Israel]]. In a live 2026-03-23 market discussion, [[Ricky]] distinguishes roughly 20 million barrels/day normally moving through Hormuz from roughly 6 million barrels/day of pipeline bypass; he sketches $70–80 oil with settlement and a higher-price [[StagflationRiskRepricing]] downside if the conflict persists. These are his dated scenarios, not measured postwar outcomes. [[ep251-yilang-kunju-sishinian-zhanlue-xuanze-de-daijia-yu-xianshi-lp8vyonyugtd2ozymgof-mim9zkd]] [[161-quanqiu-hongguan-he-ziben-shichang-2026-yijidu-fupan-yu-zhanwang-lgzjnsgkur1mcrmkqqxsvxhzb0bm]]
- **Demand and inventories mediate a physical bottleneck.** A later account says around 14 million barrels/day of crude was trapped in the Gulf but [[China]] reduced imports, drew stocks, limited refined-product exports and adjusted demand instead of prices reaching the anticipated $150/barrel; [[MathieuFavasse]] calls this [[DemandSideOilMarketPower]], not Chinese membership of [[OPEC]]. The distinct 20m flow, 6m pipeline capacity and 14m trapped estimates cannot be subtracted as if simultaneous measures. [[KateDurian]]’s producer-side comparison says extra [[UnitedArabEmirates]] supply still requires tankers, stocks and an open route, limiting [[OilProducerSupplyCoordination]] alone. [[pump-and-circumstance-is-china-the-new-opec-6a7c37287cf3c58886773a66]] [[the-secret-meeting-that-launched-opec]]
- **A nominal ceasefire left the governing rule unresolved.** An initial [[USIranNuclearDiplomacy]] MOU contemplated ending the blockade, unfreezing Iranian assets and negotiating uranium; another report described a 60-day pause on Iranian tolls and possible [[IranPostwarEconomicRelief]], not a permanent fee ban. During [[PoliticalFuneral]] observances Iran asserted its own lanes and procedures. After renewed U.S. strikes and Iranian retaliation, [[NicholasPelham]] reported stalled traffic and oil-price pressure. [[GregCarlstrom]] explains the incompatibility: Washington wanted mines cleared and unimpeded ships, Tehran the power to decide which ships moved. His later account reports only 11 vessels on July 12, against earlier days of 30–50, and oil moving from about $71 at the MOU to the mid-$80s; [[ChokepointShippingConfidence]] depends on insurer and shipowner expectations, not a government declaration that the strait is “open.” [[missing-peace-will-israel-imperil-iran-deal-6a3bae9fe1238e54b0e886c3]] [[coming-in-andy-britains-prime-minister-in-waiting-6a3512974a2a3be0f419384b]] [[the-mourning-show-the-politics-of-khameneis-funeral-6a4b75084955fc4dc12ffcc7]] [[peace-fire-further-us-iran-strikes-6a4f7acffe878dc8e27e6fbf]] [[strait-and-narrowing-the-iran-deal-crumbles-6a57608bf821a8210984481f]]
- **Permission and payment remain disputed, while narrower fixes failed.** [[HamidHosseini]] recounts an [[India]]-bound oil ship whose owner gave the [[SepahNavy]] vessel, flag, crew, cargo and destination information and, according to unnamed contacts, paid a $1-per-barrel crypto toll; the [[PlanetMoney]] report does not independently confirm a general [[MaritimeChokepointTolling]] policy or [[SanctionsEvasionCryptoPayments]]. [[RyanPetersen]] locates the larger cost in shaken [[FreedomOfNavigationTradeOrder]] and shared-loss/[[GeneralAverage]] exposure. [[Oman]]’s roughly ten-day truce and joint-management proposal without mandatory tolls was reportedly rejected by Iran, which wanted control over inbound traffic, illustrating why [[ThinDiplomaticFrameworks]] cannot settle an administrative-control dispute by wording alone. [[how-to-get-through-the-strait-of-hormuz]] [[omission-accomplished-why-the-iran-war-cycle-spins-on-6a6c7475aa1642a30f17f1cb]]
- **Neighbors and maritime operators seek different forms of resilience.** A Gulf interview says [[UnitedArabEmirates]] bypass plans and fiscal room may ease [[GulfStrategicDiversification]], unlike more exposed neighbors, but confidence in finance, aviation and logistics still matters. Another episode attributes tripled throughput at [[Istanbul]]’s largest port to Hormuz closure and describes cargo and overland routes moving toward [[Turkey]], a reported [[ChokepointLogisticsRerouting]] and possible [[WarSpilloverHubOpportunity]] rather than a lasting replacement. [[DinoMavroukis]] says a [[Saronic]] [[CorsairSaronic]] supported rescue of two pilots and argues that [[AutonomousNavalVessels]] could persist amid mines, missiles, fast craft and shore drones without extra crew exposure; this is company testimony with [[HumanAuthorizedAutonomousWeapons]] questions, not independent operational verification. ## Qualifications
The 20m movement, 6m bypass and 14m trapped measurements describe different scopes or moments and must not be treated as interchangeable. The crypto-toll story depends on Hosseini’s unnamed contacts; rescue claims are company-reported. “Open” and administratively controlled passage were disputed interpretations; the sequence does not establish a permanent settlement. [[DigitalInfrastructureWarRisk]] and [[RegionalNetworkTopologyRisk]] are adjacent concentration analogies, not direct evidence about the waterway.

## What Changed
- Passage is now evaluated through insurance confidence and administrative control, not only a declared blockade.
- Demand-side oil adjustment, Gulf bypass and external rerouting moderate but do not erase chokepoint risk.
