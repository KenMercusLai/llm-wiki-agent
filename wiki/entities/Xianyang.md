# 咸阳 / Xianyang

## Overview
咸阳 / Xianyang: Xianyang became Qin’s reform-era capital in 350 BCE.

## Current Profile
Xianyang became Qin’s reform-era capital in 350 BCE. As a court center, it mediated diplomacy, detention, assassination access and rivals’ military planning. After unification, relocation, confiscation, palaces and ritual routes concentrated power and display there. Competing succession accounts agree on Xianyang as the announcement endpoint, not on the actual deathbed decision. The conquered capital yielded archives to Liu Bang and palace plunder and destruction to Xiang Yu.

## Evidence
- **Xianyang became Qin’s reform-era capital in 350 BCE:** [[ShangYang|商鞅]] moved the [[QinState|秦]] capital from Liyang to Xianyang as part of his second [[ShangYangReforms|reform]] round in 350 BCE, making [[StrategicCapitalRelocation|capital relocation]] part of durable administrative change, alongside household separation and counties ([[QinAdministrativeStandardization|standardization]]). The source glosses its name through a position south of the Jiuzong mountains and north of the Wei River and says it remained Qin's center through unification and dynastic collapse. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]]
- **As a court center, it mediated diplomacy, detention, assassination access and rivals’ military planning:** Returning from a [[LianhengStrategy|连横]] mission, [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] learned on the road to Xianyang that [[YingSi|秦惠文王]] had died, exposing him to [[PatronSuccessionExposure|patron-succession risk]] under [[QinWuwang|秦武王]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-55-zhangyi-tongshi-guiren-qinhuiwang-lsclfbwn06fgzooipf5up9sdkedy]] [[ZhaoWulingwang|赵武灵王]], after entering Qin in disguise, imagined bypassing [[HanguPass|函谷关]] from Yunzhong/Jiuyuan to attack Xianyang; this was a plan, not an accomplished march ([[WarringStatesMilitaryTransformation|strategic context]]). [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-61-shui-shi-lishishang-daner-zui-fei-de-diwang-lvpoaszanascnlrmu2atinlxzmk1]] Under [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]], Qin seized [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]] at [[Wuguan|武关]], held him at Xianyang and demanded [[WuJunWarringStates|巫郡]] and [[Qianzhong|黔中]]; [[Mengchangjun|孟尝君]] also arrived there to serve as chancellor. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-62-ji-er-kuang-ni-jiujiu-de-tui-a-lumch-ibj8m94abjh5nvr3qhiqff]] Qin then replaced Mengchangjun with [[LouHuan|楼缓]], jailed him on suspicion of [[QiState|齐]] loyalties, and he escaped through the white fox-fur robe and retainer stratagem remembered as [[JimingGoudaoTalentCritique|鸡鸣狗盗]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-63-degao-wangzhong-mengchangjun-shi-weijunzi-lmd0owe3rq-faponbwzhkaigprin]] Chu Huaiwang's 297 BCE flight from the capital failed when Zhao would not receive him; Qin recaptured him, and he died in Qin custody in 296 BCE, with a [[MoralReputationPoliticalCapital|reputational]] cost for the court. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-65-wo-chuanyue-qu-gei-langman-de-chuwang-nian-shou-shi-lvyd7pve-tfu4ixsqrbdvhm6g64j]] In 227 BCE [[JingKe|荆轲]] and [[QinWuyang|秦武阳]] gained palace access with [[MengJia|蒙嘉]]'s help, [[FanWuji|樊无忌]]'s head, [[DuKangMap|督亢地图]] and a [[YanState|燕]] submission pretext; Qin Wuyang froze before Qin kin and ministers, leaving Jing Ke to proceed alone. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-115-1-jingke-ciqin-sihu-you-dianer-caoshuai-le-lgz1adsn1t8jjiaobmtfedpr-ixn]] Before the Chu campaign, [[WangJianQin|王翦]] repeatedly requested land and houses near the capital; the host interprets this as a [[GreedPersonaSelfProtection|performed greed]] intended to reassure [[QinShiHuang|嬴政]] while commanding a huge army, not merely ordinary property acquisition. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]]
- **After unification, relocation, confiscation, palaces and ritual routes concentrated power and display there:** The episode reports weapons collected from thirty-six commanderies and melted into twelve metal figures at Xianyang; 120,000 powerful households were forcibly relocated there to enrich and monitor the center while severing local networks ([[PostConquestDisarmament|disarmament]], [[EliteRelocationControl|elite control]], [[ImperialHomogenization|homogenization]]). Qin also copied conquered states' palaces near the Wei River. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-120-4-bing-liuguo-hou-qinshihuang-jiao-ni-ruhe-shou-jiangshan-lgwsd0c3wkl-cphma8sm3ftkowwr]] In 220 BCE the emperor departed from Xianyang through [[LongxiCommandery|陇西]] and [[BeidiCommandery|北地]] toward old Qin lands; on return, [[QinJimiao|极庙]], possible [[Taiyi|太一]] associations, roads toward [[Lishan|骊山]] and [[QinChidao|驰道]] made the city a [[QinCosmicCapitalPlanning|ritual and transport hub]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-121-yingzheng-zao-shijie-zuizao-gaosu-gonglu-2200nian-huangcao-busheng-ls1u8dmuvs5bbrzadf2dtd00js-p]] In 212 BCE crowding after the household relocation helped justify expansion south of the river and [[AfangPalace|阿房宫]], part of [[ImperialConstructionOverload|construction overload]]. The source also describes 270 linked palaces, hidden imperial residence and officials waiting at Xianyang Palace; [[ImperialItinerarySecrecy|itinerary secrecy]] was coercive rather than simply an architectural plan. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-1-qinshihuang-xiujian-shishang-zuida-lanweilou-afanggong-lhqc6jw4jzoyrg7jzc2mbxdebj4]]
- **Competing succession accounts agree on Xianyang as the announcement endpoint, not on the actual deathbed decision:** In the received [[ImperialDeathConcealment|death-concealment]] and [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|forged-edict]] account, [[LiSi|李斯]], [[ZhaoGao|赵高]] and [[QinErshi|胡亥]] hid the emperor's death through the return via [[QinZhidao|秦直道]] and announced death and accession only at Xianyang. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg]] The alternate [[ZhaoZhengShu|《赵正书》]] account posits Hu Hai was chosen in the emperor's last illness, an [[EmergencySuccessionLegitimation|emergency selection]] preceding the same public announcement; it does not settle the genuine succession decision. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]]
- **The conquered capital yielded archives to Liu Bang and palace plunder and destruction to Xiang Yu:** [[LiuBang|刘邦]] initially wanted Qin's palace wealth and women but [[FanKuai|樊哙]] and [[ZhangLiang|张良]] pressed him back to [[Bashang|霸上]], while [[XiaoHe|萧何]] saved maps, registers and administrative files from the chancellor's office ([[AdministrativeArchiveCapture|archive capture]]). [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-139-ta-zaici-yingxiang-hanchao-lp1zujuq0o3ohxc79xqi1phpb5zo]] After the Hongmen escape, [[XiangYu|项羽]] killed [[ZiyingQin|子婴]], burned Qin palace space and seized goods before going east, rejecting [[HanSheng|韩生]]'s advice to hold [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]]; the host reads that as [[CoalitionSettlementFailure|failure to turn victory into a durable settlement]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-142-zhechang-juhui-xiangyu-shudiao-le-yiqie-ln7dv2jxrk6lt1c4szylsrb41k3r]]

## Qualifications
The received forged-edict account and the Zhao Zheng Shu deathbed-selection account conflict. The capital’s announcement role does not settle the earlier decision.

## What Changed
- The city’s administrative and symbolic centrality is set against two different succession narratives and the contrasting occupations by Liu Bang and Xiang Yu.
