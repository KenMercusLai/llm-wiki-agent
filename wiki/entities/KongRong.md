## Overview

Kong Rong is a late-Han scholar-official whose current profile combines real moral courage and Confucian prestige with poor governing judgment, dangerous public speech, and a final prosecution in which political opposition is packaged through disloyalty and “unfilial” character accusations.

## Current Profile

Kong Rong first appears as a sixteen-year-old who shelters [[ZhangJianLateHan|张俭]] during the 169 CE party-prohibition danger and tries to bear the capital charge instead of [[KongBaoLateHan|孔褒]]. As an adult, he becomes a prestige actor: he helps legitimate [[LiuBei|刘备]] in the [[Xuzhou|徐州]] succession, honors [[ZhengXuan|郑玄]], recommends [[MiHengLateHan|祢衡]], carries imperial recognition to [[YuanShao|袁绍]], and confronts [[CaoCao|曹操]] over the weakly grounded prosecution of [[YangBiao|杨彪]].

The evidence also preserves serious limits. In [[BeihaiCommandery|北海郡]], Kong Rong does not convert lineage, literature, and moral reputation into grain, troops, strategic flexibility, or a safe advice channel; he kills [[ZuoChengzu|左承祖]], loses [[LiuYixun|刘义逊]], and is defeated by [[YuanTan|袁谭]]. Hanji 1090 completes the arc in 208 CE: after a capital-region enfeoffment proposal is read as threatening Cao Cao's base, [[LuCuiLateHan|路粹]] bundles old political, association, speech, and morality accusations into a death case. Kong Rong and [[KongRongChildren|his children]] are executed, while the “unfilial” label remains in tension with his earlier self-sacrifice and reported mourning conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Early moral-risk taker who shelters Zhang Jian and claims responsibility under party-prohibition pressure.
- Confucian-prestige actor whose lineage, childhood reputation, literature, and old-Han standing carry weight in legitimacy disputes.
- Public advocate who supports Liu Bei, recommends Mi Heng, carries court recognition, and challenges Cao Cao over Yang Biao.
- Beihai governor whose prestige does not become durable military, administrative, or advisory capacity.
- Direct speaker whose moral force and refusal to self-censor repeatedly create political exposure.
- Politically prosecuted critic whose attributed private-morality statements are used alongside security and hierarchy charges.
- Household head whose execution expands to his young children, turning political defeat into family destruction.

## Evidence

- Early courage and later filial-label conflict: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-895-xuexing-tudao-xia-de-gurou-qingshen-llsplyyrjxhqkpi2aiuqzulgm6w3|Hanji 895]] presents Kong Rong hiding Zhang Jian and trying to bear guilt; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1090-ta-ceng-maosi-cangxiong-wei-mu-kuxue-zhen-shi-buxiao-zhi-ren-ma-lg-z9lyx_y6dxbopsfrqxoyymdjj|Hanji 1090]] contrasts that conduct and reported mourning with the later “unfilial” accusation.
- Xuzhou legitimacy support: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-981-sanguo-zui-gang-moushi-chengyu-yi-ju-hua-rang-caocao-tanfu-lhlhlrmf2gvspwyvjkxg6kz44fth|Hanji 981]] and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-982-ba-yi-ba-liubei-ru-xu-beihou-de-liyi-zhi-zheng-ljajdc95xtylhkn5teefz26yyyz1|Hanji 982]] show Kong Rong supporting Liu Bei's Xuzhou acceptance and opposing the Yuan Shu alternative through prestige and Han-orthodox reasoning.
- Prestige-capacity gap: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1007-cong-shentong-dao-fuwang-kongrong-zhenshi-mianmu-puguang-lkiljj4yvl5fhslrc9gcxudxxklt|Hanji 1007]] uses Beihai failure, Zheng Xuan honor, Zuo Chengzu's execution, Liu Yixun's departure, and Yuan Tan's attack to ground [[PrestigeCapacityGap|名望能力落差]].
- Risky recommendation and court speech: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1011-miheng-zhisi-zuiju-beiju-secai-de-jiedao-sharen-ltrzpchwdfcyp4ntmum9giu-5mva|Hanji 1011]] shows Kong Rong recommending Mi Heng; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1016-sanguo-luanshi-li-chengui-jiazu-de-mingyun-jueze-ltuckdcxpyxk-xmzp8rt-2gjfzcs|Hanji 1016]] sends him with a tally to Yuan Shao; and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1020-sanguo-baijiazi-yuanshu-ruhe-ba-hao-pai-da-de-xilan-lhwr9hfmanr-zktleufy3kvl38u0|Hanji 1020]] has him resist reputation-destroying punishment of Yang Biao without secure evidence.
- Final prosecution and household destruction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1090-ta-ceng-maosi-cangxiong-wei-mu-kuxue-zhen-shi-buxiao-zhi-ren-ma-lg-z9lyx_y6dxbopsfrqxoyymdjj|Hanji 1090]] links the capital-region proposal to Cao Cao's threat perception, Lu Cui's accusation bundle, the execution of Kong Rong and his children, and [[ZhiXiLateHan|脂习]]'s public mourning.

## Qualifications

Kong Rong should not be flattened into childhood virtue, Beihai incompetence, or a pure free-speech martyr. Hanji 895 supports real early courage, while Hanji 1007 supports a real governance and advice-tolerance failure. Hanji 1090's political-prosecution reading is plausible inside its narrative but does not independently establish the exact proposal, charge text, sayings, dialogue, legal procedure, or Cao Cao's private motive. The “unfilial” label and the countervailing filial anecdotes are therefore recorded as a contested reputation field rather than resolved into a simple character verdict.

## What Changed

- Hanji 1090 completes Kong Rong's arc with the 208 CE conflict, accusation bundle, execution, and death of his children.
- The current judgment now distinguishes substantive governance failures from the political use of private-morality stigma.
- The “unfilial” label is qualified by the already-grounded Zhang Jian shelter case and the new source's reported mourning evidence.
- Zhi Xi's warning and mourning add an external witness to both Kong Rong's speech risk and surviving moral reputation.
