# Oracle

## Overview
Oracle links long-lived enterprise database economics to AI cloud commitments, debt-financed infrastructure, platform ownership and geographically exposed facilities.

## Current Profile
Relational database adoption made Oracle a durable enterprise-software actor, while acquisition and founder wealth connect it to startup and media ecosystems. Its newer cloud leases, reported AI contracts and capital-market borrowing expose different kinds of counterparty, buildout and physical-location risk.

## Key Characteristics
- Enterprise software and acquisition legacy.
- AI leases and financing exposure.
- Platform and physical infrastructure.

## Qualifications
- The $300 billion OpenAI services figure and $10 billion Michigan financing withdrawal are reported, not verified balances; bonds, developer project debt and private credit differ. Wartime target claims lack external verification; political influence is the source’s inference, not proven quid pro quo.

## What Changed
- Database lock-in and Endeca acquisition history are distinct from the newer AI cloud contracting role.
- A long customer lease may support project debt without eliminating refinancing, bond-market or buildout risk.
- Wartime cloud exposure remains an unverified source claim.
