## Overview

窦武 / Dou Wu (late Han) is a late-Han outer-relative and anti-eunuch official whose profile now starts before [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s accession, with [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] showing him as the high-leverage intercessor for imprisoned Party Prohibition figures under [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]]. [[JiaBiaoLateHan|贾彪]] targets Dou Wu because Dou is [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙]]'s father, holds public standing, and can speak closer to the emperor than frightened ordinary officials. Hanji 879 then shows Dou Wu helping select the twelve-year-old Liu Hong after Huan dies childless, while Hanji 882-887 trace his accession reward peak, anti-eunuch reform opening, delay, leaked plan, countercoup defeat, and death. Later sources preserve his memory as accession supporter, eunuch grievance target, and eventually a rehabilitated victim used by [[DongZhuo|董卓]].

## Current Profile

Dou Wu's current profile separates leverage, reward, and command. Hanji 876 supplies the leverage layer. After Chen Fan is removed and the court becomes afraid to plead for [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]], [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]], and other imprisoned士人, Jia Biao identifies Dou Wu as the person who can carry the rescue argument into the decision field. Dou Wu's memorial attacks eunuch favor, invokes Qin Ershi and Zhao Gao as a warning, defends the party prisoners as accused without evidence, argues that worthy people are the real auspicious sign, then claims illness and returns his seals. In this branch, Dou Wu converts relationship access into risk-bearing public argument.

Hanji 879 makes the next weakness moral and institutional at once: Dou can help solve a real succession gap, but the chosen child's governability tempts him and Dou Miao toward control. Hanji 882 then shows the public consequence of that accession role: Dou receives high reward, his family is honored, and [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]] warns that genealogy-based succession and public recommendation should not be mistaken for Dou's personal merit.

Hanji 884 and Hanji 885 show the next weakness before the leak. Dou has family access and reform allies, but he still depends on Dou Miao's consent, accepts a selective first purge, follows ordinary procedure after [[ZhengSaLateHan|郑飒 / 郑萨]] is arrested, and leaves the palace during a live danger window. Hanji 886 then presents secrecy and tempo failure: once the memorial path exposes the plan, the eunuch side moves first to control Emperor Ling, Dou Miao, gates, seals, tallies, and edict production. Hanji 887 carries that loss into military collapse, suicide, public humiliation, and a widened purge.

The later memory layer explains why Dou Wu remains politically useful after death. Emperor Ling remembers him and Dou Miao as accession supporters, while [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] remember him as an old enemy. The 172 CE burial dispute over Dou Miao reopens the moral question of Dou Wu and Chen Fan's destruction, and Dong Zhuo later uses rehabilitation of Dou, Chen Fan, and Party Prohibition victims as legitimacy packaging for coercive takeover.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou Wu is a high-leverage intercessor whose family access makes him useful before open reform power exists.
- His Hanji 876 memorial turns Party Prohibition rescue into risk-bearing anti-eunuch criticism and virtue politics.
- He later becomes an accession powerholder tempted by the controllability of a young emperor.
- His accession reward peak creates reputation and safety risk because the reward exceeds what Lu Zhi sees as bounded personal merit.
- His anti-eunuch project depends on converting family access and dowager consent into decisive palace action, which he fails to do.
- His exposed intent lets the eunuch side seize the emperor, dowager, gates, seals, tallies, and edict channel before open confrontation.
- His memory remains politically active because later actors can use the injustice around him for repair, factional defense, or coercive legitimacy cover.

- Accession choice and control temptation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Dou Wu consults on the successor, reports Liu Hong to Dou Miao, and favors the twelve-year-old partly because he is easy to control.
- Accession reward and warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] says Dou Wu receives Wenxi marquis status, his son and nephews receive honors, and Lu Zhi warns that this overstates Dou's personal merit in Emperor Ling's accession.
- Reform opening and limited first strike: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-884-zhichang-chujian-yao-dui-shui-xian-kaidao-lvtc3uqlso242olkyin3fvz5a7ys|Hanji 884]] says Dou Miao entrusts government to Dou Wu and Chen Fan, Dou agrees with Chen's call to remove Cao Jie and Wang Fu, but the first authorized punishment reaches only Guan Ba and Su Kang.

- Defeat and public humiliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-887-dang-zhuduizhang-pengshang-zhuduiyou-jieguo-taican-lvyh9a-kvsdqaokmylgg139bvhfk|Hanji 887]] says forged authority, Zhang Huan's uninformed force, Wang Fu's shouting, and reward promises break Dou's line before his suicide and head display.
- Accession and old-grievance memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-898-renzei-zuofu-kelian-zhiren-neng-you-duo-huangtang-lgfup6tyv85zhvnmzf6xlesmblcj|Hanji 898]] says Emperor Ling remembers Dou and Dou Miao as accession supporters while Cao Jie and Wang Fu resent Dou's earlier attempt to remove them.
- Burial and rehabilitation memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] says Dou Miao's posthumous status threatens to revive the injustice memory around Dou Wu and Chen Fan; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] pairs Dou Wu with Chen Fan in Cao Jie's negative legacy, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]] uses them as restored Party Prohibition-linked victims in Dong Zhuo's justice-packaging move.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 876 shows Dou Wu's effective access and moral courage, but it does not imply that access later becomes command capacity; Hanji 884-887 still show his failure to secure tempo, secrecy, and palace control.
- Hanji 879's claim that Dou Wu wanted Liu Hong because he was controllable is treated as the source's motive reading; the page does not deny that Liu Hong also had a legitimate Liu-clan genealogy.
- Hanji 886 and Hanji 887 give podcast-summary versions of the coup, so the claim that secrecy and hesitation were decisive remains source-scoped rather than a complete reconstruction of every palace and military channel.
- The page preserves Dou Wu's later moral grievance without treating every later appeal to his memory as sincere repair; Dong Zhuo's rehabilitation branch is explicitly coercive legitimacy packaging.

## What Changed

- Hanji 876 adds an earlier Emperor Huan-era intercession layer before the accession and anti-eunuch coup sequence.
- Dou Wu's current profile now distinguishes effective access for memorial rescue from later failed command execution.
- The source adds Dou Wu as a concrete bridge between Party Prohibition rescue, anti-eunuch criticism, and auspiciousness-as-virtue politics.
