## Overview

窦武 / Dou Wu (late Han) is a late-Han outer-relative and anti-eunuch official whose profile now starts with the accession choice itself. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] shows Dou Wu helping select the twelve-year-old Liu Hong, later [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], after [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] dies childless; the host reads the choice as a failure to resist the temptation of controllable power. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] then shows the reward peak and [[LuZhiLateHan|卢植]]'s warning. Hanji 884-887 trace the anti-eunuch reform opening, delay, leaked plan, countercoup, and Dou Wu's death, while later sources preserve his memory as accession supporter, eunuch grievance target, and eventually a rehabilitated victim used by [[DongZhuo|董卓]].

## Current Profile

Dou Wu's current profile separates accession influence from executable crisis command. Hanji 879 makes the first weakness moral and institutional at once: Dou can help solve a real succession gap, but the chosen child's governability tempts him and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] toward control. Hanji 882 then shows the public consequence of that accession role: Dou receives high reward, his family is honored, and Lu Zhi warns that genealogy-based succession and public recommendation should not be mistaken for Dou's personal merit.

Hanji 884 and Hanji 885 show the next weakness before the leak. Dou has family access and reform allies, but he still depends on Dou Miao's consent, accepts a selective first purge, follows ordinary procedure after [[ZhengSaLateHan|郑飒 / 郑萨]] is arrested, and leaves the palace during a live danger window. Hanji 886 then presents secrecy and tempo failure: once the memorial path exposes the plan, the eunuch side moves first to control Emperor Ling, Dou Miao, gates, seals, tallies, and edict production. Hanji 887 carries that loss into military collapse, suicide, public humiliation, and a widened purge.

The later memory layer explains why Dou Wu remains politically useful after death. Emperor Ling remembers him and Dou Miao as accession supporters, while [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] remember him as an old enemy. The 172 CE burial dispute over Dou Miao reopens the moral question of Dou Wu and [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]]'s destruction, and Dong Zhuo later uses rehabilitation of Dou, Chen Fan, and Party Prohibition victims as legitimacy packaging for coercive takeover.

## Key Characteristics

- Dou Wu is an accession powerholder whose first profile layer now includes the temptation to choose a controllable young emperor.
- His accession reward peak creates reputation and safety risk because the reward exceeds what Lu Zhi sees as bounded personal merit.
- His anti-eunuch project depends on converting family access and dowager consent into decisive palace action, which he fails to do.
- His pre-leak failure includes selective punishment, slow procedure, exposed paperwork, and leaving the palace during the danger window.
- His exposed intent lets the eunuch side seize the emperor, dowager, gates, seals, tallies, and edict channel before open confrontation.
- His defeat creates immediate collateral punishment for family, clients, followers, recommended officials, and old subordinates.
- His memory remains politically active because later actors can use the injustice around him for repair, factional defense, or coercive legitimacy cover.

## Evidence

- Accession choice and control temptation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-879-si-zai-nvren-shenshang-de-hunyong-nanren-lqz5-mpi0waa-xmuhjhwkaecjjrc|Hanji 879]] says Dou Wu consults on the successor, reports Liu Hong to Dou Miao, and favors the twelve-year-old partly because he is easy to control.
- Accession reward and warning: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-882-fengguang-taisheng-zhuding-bude-haosi-lphagu5ljuxxjwx-uvnbs229wjii|Hanji 882]] says Dou Wu receives Wenxi marquis status, his son and nephews receive honors, and Lu Zhi warns that this overstates Dou's personal merit in Emperor Ling's accession.
- Reform opening and limited first strike: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-884-zhichang-chujian-yao-dui-shui-xian-kaidao-lvtc3uqlso242olkyin3fvz5a7ys|Hanji 884]] says Dou Miao entrusts government to Dou Wu and Chen Fan, Dou agrees with Chen's call to remove Cao Jie and Wang Fu, but the first authorized punishment reaches only Guan Ba and Su Kang.
- Pre-leak delay and absence: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-885-shengsi-guantou-ta-qingjia-xiuxi-luydnh9oqdyyfpbeztooi2e7lvca|Hanji 885]] says Dou Wu repeatedly presses Dou Miao, responds to Liu Yu's warning with partial security appointments, insists on interrogation after Zheng Sa is arrested, and leaves the palace on September 7.
- Exposed plan before decisive action: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-886-shenchu-zhichang-zuoge-chengfu-henshen-de-ren-lo6dgcdbshjgto-qsfcouwjersdi|Hanji 886]] says eunuch-side memorial handlers learn of Dou Wu's anti-eunuch move before he has secured the palace, letting Zhu Yu, Cao Jie, and Wang Fu move first.
- Palace legitimacy lost before battlefield collapse: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-886-shenchu-zhichang-zuoge-chengfu-henshen-de-ren-lo6dgcdbshjgto-qsfcouwjersdi|Hanji 886]] says the eunuch side controls Emperor Ling, Dou Miao, gates, seals, tallies, and written edicts before Dou tries to rally troops outside.
- Defeat and public humiliation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-887-dang-zhuduizhang-pengshang-zhuduiyou-jieguo-taican-lvyh9a-kvsdqaokmylgg139bvhfk|Hanji 887]] says forged authority, Zhang Huan's uninformed force, Wang Fu's shouting, and reward promises break Dou's line before his suicide and head display.
- Accession and old-grievance memory: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-898-renzei-zuofu-kelian-zhiren-neng-you-duo-huangtang-lgfup6tyv85zhvnmzf6xlesmblcj|Hanji 898]] says Emperor Ling remembers Dou and Dou Miao as accession supporters while Cao Jie and Wang Fu resent Dou's earlier attempt to remove them.
- Burial dispute as indirect repair: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-901-jixingzi-de-ren-hui-shi-shenme-xiachang-lh1yfyjzqptxvtnv9taiqms9yjud|Hanji 901]] says Dou Miao's posthumous status threatens to revive the injustice memory around Dou Wu and Chen Fan.
- Continuing eunuch-power memory and later rehabilitation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-921-jinqian-ru-shui-que-ze-ke-si-tan-ze-yan-si-lj1dk4qdgcago9elr7ctuvre3zic|Hanji 921]] pairs Dou Wu with Chen Fan in Cao Jie's negative legacy, while [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-948-dongzhuo-feidi-quanchen-ruhe-gaibian-huangquan-youxi-ltekg1a-5b5u37aeekjm3dm2m6kk|Hanji 948]] uses them as restored Party Prohibition-linked victims in Dong Zhuo's justice-packaging move.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 879's claim that Dou Wu wanted Liu Hong because he was controllable is treated as the source's motive reading; the page does not deny that Liu Hong also had a legitimate Liu-clan genealogy.
- Hanji 886 and Hanji 887 give podcast-summary versions of the coup, so the claim that secrecy and hesitation were decisive remains source-scoped rather than a complete reconstruction of every palace and military channel.
- The page preserves Dou Wu's later moral grievance without treating every later appeal to his memory as sincere repair; Dong Zhuo's rehabilitation branch is explicitly coercive legitimacy packaging.

## What Changed

- The profile now begins with Hanji 879's successor-selection step before Hanji 882's accession reward.
- Dou Wu's early danger now includes the temptation to control a child emperor, not only excessive honors after the accession.
- His later anti-eunuch failure is framed as part of a longer gap between holding succession influence and commanding palace crisis execution.
