## Overview

Wu Han / 吴汉 is synthesized as an Eastern Han founding commander whose current profile now combines early recruitment and campaign work, tactical effectiveness at Chengdu, severe post-surrender overreach, later post-Shu honor, and late-life discipline. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-713-liuxiu-nu-le-mingjiang-wuhan-jing-xuexi-chengdu-lv6555l99it33gw34uydsz4tkbje|Hanji 713]] sharply qualifies the older disciplined-service profile: Wu Han helps win the final Chengdu battle but then kills Gongsun Shu's household and clan, destroys Yan Cen's clan, and permits plunder and burning after surrender, triggering [[LiuXiu|Liu Xiu]]'s rebuke. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-715-shishang-zui-zhihui-de-yici-duoquan-loqkawl247maw9fn-nomaw5gzkgu|Hanji 715]]'s later ceremony and [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-721-ta-sha-jianu-ma-gongzhu-weihe-fanbei-shang-lryzcejdnrm4-92esnddfvyjkqli|Hanji 721]]'s deathbed discipline therefore have to be read beside, not instead of, the Chengdu atrocity.

## Current Profile

Hanji 733-2 pairs Wu Han with [[GengYanEasternHan|Geng Yan]] in [[LiuXiu|刘秀]]'s effort to secure soldiers and cavalry from the Youzhou/Shanggu military environment. He later appears alongside Geng Yan in Qinghe-related action, where the episode emphasizes battlefield pursuit, ambush, and tactical coordination rather than Wu Han's independent career.

Hanji 713 adds the Chengdu endgame. Wu Han steadies the battle against Gongsun Shu by sending Gao Wu and Tang Han with elite troops, leading to Gongsun Shu's mortal wound and Yan Cen's surrender of the city. Wu Han then crosses the surrender boundary: he kills Gongsun Shu's family and clan, destroys Yan Cen's clan, and allows troops to loot and burn Chengdu. Liu Xiu rebukes both Wu Han and [[LiuShangEasternHan|Liu Shang]] because Chengdu had surrendered and officials and civilians had already submitted.

Hanji 715 adds the later reward-and-ceremony side of Wu Han's founding service. After returning from Shu, Wu Han is permitted to go home for ancestor rites, receives a large grain grant, and is honored when Liu Xiu holds a major banquet for the soldiers after Wu Han returns to Luoyang. In the current synthesis, this reward does not erase Hanji 713; it shows that Liu Xiu could both condemn the Chengdu sack and still preserve Wu Han's founding-merit status.

Hanji 721 adds the late-life evaluation. When Wu Han is seriously ill, Liu Xiu visits and asks for final words. Wu Han says only that the ruler should be especially cautious and not lightly pardon criminals. The episode connects this warning to its larger law-and-personnel theme: useful officials and commanders protect public order by resisting easy exemption.

The same source presents Wu Han's military and household discipline as the basis of his durable merit. In battle adversity, he remains composed, prepares arms, and steadies morale; when ordered to campaign, he can depart the same day. After his family buys land and property while army supplies are strained, he rebukes them and distributes the assets to relatives, showing that founding merit should not become unchecked family accumulation.

## Key Characteristics

- Liu Xiu founding general connected to the northern recruitment mission.
- Operational partner to Geng Yan in the episode's Qinghe campaign narrative.
- Tactical Chengdu commander whose reinforcements help defeat Gongsun Shu's final attack.
- Post-surrender overreach actor responsible for killing surrendered-regime households and permitting the Chengdu sack.
- Recipient of post-Shu ceremony and material reward despite Liu Xiu's earlier rebuke.
- Calm battlefield commander who prepares and rallies during adverse conditions.
- Deathbed adviser and family-asset disciplinarian whose later public-order image remains real but morally incomplete.

## Qualifications

This page still does not attempt a complete account of Wu Han's Eastern Han career. Hanji 713 reports Liu Xiu's anger and rebuke but not a formal punishment or removal, and Hanji 715 later records honor and reward after the Shu campaign. The current evidence therefore supports a mixed judgment: Wu Han remains a useful and honored founding commander, but the Chengdu surrender episode is a severe moral and command-discipline qualification.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 713 as the Chengdu layer: tactical victory over Gongsun Shu, followed by clan killing, looting, burning, and Liu Xiu's rebuke after surrender.
- Reframed Hanji 715's post-Shu honor as later reward that coexists with, rather than cancels, the Chengdu rebuke.
- Preserved Hanji 721's deathbed, command-discipline, and family-restraint layer while treating it as morally incomplete.
- Preserved Hanji 733-2's early northern recruitment and Geng Yan campaign context.
