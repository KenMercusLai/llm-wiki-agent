# 刘宠 / Liu Chong (late Han minister)

## Overview

Liu Chong is the ministerial late-Han official whose current wiki profile now runs from Kuaiji burden reduction and a 160 CE sikong appointment to the 169 CE Three Excellencies reshuffle where he moves from situ to taiwei.

## Current Profile

The earliest layer is source-bounded to [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]]. Before entering the central court as sikong in 160 CE, Liu Chong governs [[KuaijiCommandery|会稽郡]] by reducing taxes and levies, restraining subordinate officials, and leaving local people unusually quiet and attached to him. When he departs, elders block his road with farewell money; he refuses the large gift and accepts only a symbolic coin from each person so their affection is acknowledged without turning public trust into private extraction.

The later layer remains the 169 CE office movement from [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]]. Liu Chong is serving as situ and is appointed taiwei in June 169 after [[WenrenXiLateHan|闻人袭]] is removed. The page therefore treats him as the same ministerial actor moving through high court office, while keeping him distinct from [[LiuChongChenKing|陈王刘宠]].

## Key Characteristics

- Local administrator associated with reduced taxes, reduced levies, and restrained subordinate officials in Kuaiji.
- Converts popular affection into a symbolic farewell token rather than private enrichment.
- Summoned from Kuaiji into central service as sikong in 160 CE.
- Later appears as situ and is appointed taiwei during the June 169 high-office reshuffle.
- Part of the same office-turnover field that removes Wenren Xi and Xu Xun and elevates Liu Xiao.
- Disambiguated from the Chen king Liu Chong because the evidence gives a ministerial and local-administrative office context.

## Evidence

- Kuaiji burden reduction: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Liu Chong governs Kuaiji by reducing taxes and levies and by restraining officials, so local people are quiet and grateful.
- Farewell-money boundary: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Kuaiji elders try to give him money on departure, and he accepts only one coin from each as a token.
- Sikong appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-855-ni-you-duo-wuqing-jiu-you-duo-qiangda-lulbpnufi8xfijrviufo6biuy-h1|Hanji 855]] says Emperor Huan summons Liu Chong as sikong in 160 CE.
- Taiwei appointment: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] says situ Liu Chong is appointed taiwei in June 169.
- Office sequence and disambiguation: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] places the taiwei appointment after Wenren Xi and Xu Xun are removed and gives a Three Excellencies officeholder, not the Chen-state ruler represented by [[LiuChongChenKing|Liu Chong / Chen king]].

## Qualifications

The identification remains bounded to the office sequence in the listed source notes. Hanji 855 gives a Kuaiji governor summoned as sikong in 160 CE, and Hanji 891 gives situ Liu Chong becoming taiwei in 169 CE; the page treats these as one ministerial route while preserving the separate Chen-king page. The sources do not yet reconstruct Liu Chong's full biography between those two court posts.

## What Changed

- Expanded the page beyond disambiguation by adding the Kuaiji governance and farewell-token layer.
- Added the 160 CE sikong appointment before the 169 CE taiwei movement.
- Reframed Liu Chong as a local-trust case as well as a high-office turnover actor.
