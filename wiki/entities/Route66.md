# Route 66

## Overview
Route 66 is an American road and cultural itinerary whose tourism value now depends on memory, roadside enterprise, and selectively restored sites rather than speed or transport utility.

## Current Profile
The travelogue contrasts migration and car culture with the interstate bypass and later heritage tourism. Its roadside attractions include Midpoint Cafe, the American Giants Museum, the Threate station, and the U-Drop Inn; the Green Book account complicates the romance of free movement.

## Key Characteristics
- **From transport to heritage:** Interstate bypass and decommissioning shifted the road’s value from efficient travel to deliberate nostalgia tourism.
- **Roadside enterprise:** Midpoint Cafe, fiberglass figures, Seligman and the U-Drop Inn show how local businesses sell the road’s cultural image.
- **Unequal mobility:** The Threate Filling Station and Green Book account show how Jim Crow restrictions qualified the celebrated freedom of the American road.

## Evidence
- **From transport to heritage:** [[JohnFasman]] follows the Chicago-to-Santa-Monica highway at its centenary, relating westward migration and car culture to its decommissioning as a main transport route. Interstate bypass made the road slower and drained passing trade and population in towns such as [[ShamrockTexas]]; the restored [[UDropInn]], *Cars* recognition, and international visitors illustrate a partial shift to deliberate [[Route66NostalgiaTourism|nostalgia tourism]], not renewed transport efficiency. [[the-mourning-show-the-politics-of-khameneis-funeral-6a4b75084955fc4dc12ffcc7]] [[putins-options-an-oligarch-speaks-out-6a50c5ebbafe2fa6a7f38210]]
- **Roadside enterprise and performance:** Midpoint Cafe, Seligman, Oatman, motorcycles, desert travel, burros and staged Old West imagery sell an imagined freedom alongside migration memory. In Illinois the [[AmericanGiantsMuseum]] preserves giant fiberglass figures, restored service-station settings and local advertising meant to catch passing drivers: [[RoadsideAdvertisingSpectacle]] became part of the heritage product after the traffic that sustained it declined. [[continental-rift-natos-tense-summit-6a4cc6b0c4772b27e88e898e]] [[marine-warfare-le-pen-runs-for-president-6a4e1f2781fe1f6460b7117c]]
- **Unequal mobility:** Fasman and [[EdwardThreate]] use the restored [[ThreateFillingStation]] in [[LutherOklahoma]], described as Route 66’s only Black-owned and operated filling station, to complicate the road-freedom myth. During Jim Crow, Black travelers could not count on fuel, food or safety; the [[GreenBook]] listed welcoming businesses and warned of dangerous places including sundown towns from 1936 to 1966. Threate calls the station a “safe haven,” an example of [[BlackTravelInfrastructure]] rather than universal access. [[peace-fire-further-us-iran-strikes-6a4f7acffe878dc8e27e6fbf]]

## Qualifications
The reporting is a series of travel segments rather than a quantified national impact study. The Green Book and Jim Crow account explicitly limits an undifferentiated freedom narrative; restoration did not erase the earlier bypass losses.
