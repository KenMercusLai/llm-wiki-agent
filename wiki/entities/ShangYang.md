# Shang Yang / 商鞅

## Overview
Shang Yang (公孙鞅 / 魏鞅) is treated in the historical-commentary episodes as a Qin reformer whose state-building capacity and military success were inseparable from coercion, credibility, and elite backlash.

## Current Profile
His access through Jing Jian, Qin Xiao Gong’s patronage, local surveillance, military-merit incentives and later administrative and tax changes frame the reform. The Gongzi Ang parley and Zhao Liang rebuke show the strategic and personal cost of methods that succeeded in the short run.

## Key Characteristics
- **Access and coercive reform:** Jing Jian’s court access and Qin Xiao Gong’s backing enabled reforms combining household surveillance, merit ranks and credible reward/punishment.
- **Administration and warfare:** Capital relocation, measurements and a probably poll-tax-like change expanded state capacity; the 340 BCE Gongzi Ang parley won territory at a credibility cost.
- **Backlash and interpretation:** Tutor punishments, elite anger, ruler succession and Zhao Liang’s warning frame an unsafe exit, while the Shiji/Tongjian wording and hypothetical retirement remain contested.

## Evidence
- **Court access and coercive reform:** Before the name 商鞅, 公孙鞅 served [[GongshuWeiChancellor|公叔痤]] in [[WeiState|魏国]], but [[LiangHuiWang|魏惠王]] neither employed him nor acted on Gongshu’s “use him or kill him” warning. Qin’s 361 BCE recruitment opening and [[JingJian|景监]]’s access to [[QinXiaogong|秦孝公]] in [[QinState|秦国]] let him replace unsuccessful slower 帝道/王道 pitches with practical state-strengthening proposals; Jing Jian’s title does not establish that he was a eunuch. In 359 BCE he prevailed over [[GanLong|甘龙]]’s custom-based objection and linked [[CollateralPunishmentAndGuiltByAssociation|什伍连坐]], agricultural rewards, [[MilitaryMeritRankSystem|军功爵制]] and [[XimuLixin|徙木立信]] to credible but severe state rule. When [[YingSi|太子嬴驷]] violated the law, he punished tutors [[GongziQian|公子虔]] and [[GongsunJia|公孙贾]], not the prince directly; the episode reports immediate compliance but accumulated elite enmity and [[WarringStatesReformBacklash|reform backlash]]. [[SimaGuang|司马光]] condemns the harshness while crediting his “信,” not endorsing the whole reform. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-12-gongsunyang-qiuzhi-lu-shang-de-la-guanxi-zou-houmen-lvv4-bz8sm9scezci-o-msapkvil]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-1-lgmjapc2eecefbdshnpenzfcv6ga]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-13-gongsunyang-keke-bianfa-re-shashen-2-lk-d9uibhneerfol6z2rrle-6ypm]]
- **Administration, taxation and war:** Under the name 魏鞅, he attacked [[WeiState|魏国]], putting [[Anyi|安邑]] and [[Guyang|固阳]] under pressure as part of [[QinEastwardPressure|Qin’s eastward advance]]; in 350 BCE a second reform round moved Qin’s capital to [[Xianyang|咸阳]] and reorganized [[HouseholdSeparationLaw|households]], counties, fields and measures. The host interprets the 348 BCE fiscal change as *probably* an early [[PollTax|per-person tax]], not proven first-in-history status. In 340 BCE Shang Yang argued that post-[[MalingBattle|Maling]] Wei was vulnerable, then used old acquaintance and peace-parley language to seize [[GongziAng|公子卬]] during a post-oath drink, a [[PeaceParleyEntrapment|tactical gain]] with a later trust cost. [[LiangHuiWang]] ceded Hexi and moved toward [[Daliang|大梁]]; Shang Yang received the fifteen-city [[Shangyu|商於]] fief and 商君 title, but Wei later refused him refuge, in part because of the deception. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-17-hanzhaohou-he-shenbuhai-lghjmjhq9qs4se96c5ofgz6caqof]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-18-zhongguo-lishishang-di-yi-li-rentoushui-lgqnequk0rxejqgihwdqxqyqnsvq]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-22-gongsunyang-zha-wei-gongzi-ang-lss1l7lojfwgwdtcje7m5ijodpxi]] [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-23-weihuiwang-hui-buyong-gongshu-zhi-yan-lveaqklo7oncmqudgmdk7l03qhsf]]
- **Backlash and later interpretation:** After Qin Xiao Gong’s death in 338 BCE, [[GongziQian]]’s faction accused Shang Yang of rebellion; the chronicle recounts flight, a refused stay under his own inn-registration law, unsuccessful refuge in Wei, armed resistance, death and posthumous dismemberment. [[ZhaoLiang|赵良]]’s earlier comparison with [[BailiXi|百里奚]] challenged Shang Yang’s guarded, enemy-making power; the [[Shiji|《史记》]] offers a hypothetical retreat by surrendering [[Shangyu]], but the host doubts it could have undone resentment, and mentions adviser [[ShiJiao|尸佼]]’s later withdrawal toward Chengdu. The host also contrasts [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]]’s broader resentment wording with Shiji’s royal/noble focus, relating the difference to [[SimaGuang]]’s era and [[WangAnshiReforms|王安石变法]]; the [[CaoCao|曹操]] analogy is interpretation, not another episode in Shang Yang’s life. Later discussions invoke him to criticize unbounded collective punishment under [[PunishmentTheoryTradeoff]], and as a limited comparison with [[NiccoloMachiavelli|Machiavelli]], whose [[ThePrince|The Prince]] considers political forms and contingency beyond Legalist ruler technique. ## Qualifications
The “first poll tax” is the host’s probable interpretation rather than independently established primacy. The Shiji and Zizhi Tongjian resentment formulations differ (royal-noble versus wider resentment), and Zhao Liang’s hypothetical retirement is disputed by the host. Machiavelli is a limited comparison, not a Western synonym for Legalism; the Luo Xiang discussion uses Shang Yang as a critical example.

## What Changed
