## Overview
Ukraine appears in these registered notes primarily as a country sustaining and contesting Russia's war: seeking allied support, conducting deep strikes, defending cities and exports, and debating wartime command and legitimacy. A Mariupol family-history episode extends the page backward into twentieth-century violence; it is not a source for present military policy.

## Current Profile
The 2026 Economist and Marketplace Tech accounts describe simultaneous offensive and defensive pressures: attacks on Russian oil and Crimean infrastructure alongside large Russian missile-and-drone salvos, expensive interception and the vulnerability of Black Sea exports. Alliance reliability and domestic military accountability complicate the response. Individual witnesses and U.S. senators' positions should not be flattened into Ukraine's official policy.

## Key Characteristics
- Relies on allied deterrence and military-industrial support, with limits to European autonomy and U.S. continuity.
- Uses drones and infrastructure attacks to pressure Russian logistics and make the war visible beyond the front.
- Must protect cities, civilians, ports and harvest routes against larger combined air attacks and scarce interceptors.
- Faces wartime command-reform, election and corruption-accountability disputes under legal and public constraints.
- Carries layered civilian and family memory not reducible to the current battlefield.

## Qualifications
The 2025 interviewees and 2026 reports belong to different moments; no later battlefield outcome is inferred from them. Graham's position is a U.S. senator's, the British cleaner is one witness, and the family-history material addresses another century. Russian elite unease described by [[AndreyMelnichenko]] in the July report is not democratic opposition or proof of a policy reversal. The July/ August reshuffle and allegations are source-scoped; spelling variants for Syrskyi/Drapatyi and Fedorov should not be treated as distinct people. The export-loss number is prospective.

The summit discussion links [[NATOAllianceCredibility]] and [[RussianHybridPressure]]—including drone incursions and infrastructure sabotage—without reducing the war to those threats. [[DonaldTrump]] appears as an uncertainty in allied commitment, while [[RepublicanForeignPolicyAdaptation]] describes Graham's stance rather than a change in Ukrainian command. [[continental-rift-natos-tense-summit-6a4cc6b0c4772b27e88e898e]]

The British retrospective treats [[Brexit]] and [[PostBrexitStrategicIdentity]] as [[UnitedKingdom]] policy context for defence help. The Marketplace Tech source's [[DroneDecoyEconomics]] and [[LowCostDroneWarfare]] explain why unarmed decoys can consume costly responses. [[WartimeLeadershipAccountability]] is the reported domestic lens on the July command protest, not a verdict on military success. Wodin's [[FamilyMemoryNonfiction]] belongs to historical civilian witness, not a military situation report. [[tech-20260319-0319-mp-tech-pod-128-tech-20260319-0319-mp-tech-pod-128]]
