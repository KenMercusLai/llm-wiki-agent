## Overview

翟方进 / Zhai Fangjin is synthesized as a Western Han chancellor whose wiki profile now has three connected faces: in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-558-ai-zhuang-caihuiying-wangmang-you-duo-hui-yinren-llvrlct7laohoo54s3lhzgwvsfc9|Hanji 558]] he joins [[HeWuWesternHan|何武]] in advocating the abolition of刺史 and creation of higher-ranked州牧 under [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]], in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-559-mingming-wuzui-weihe-tianxiaren-dou-xiang-ta-si-lu3ckuxwujdbj4zdoqhvo2cg1rs0|Hanji 559]] he becomes the chancellor sacrificed after an 荧惑守心 omen, and in [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-560-zhichang-bu-bei-keng-jiao-ni-zhe-zhao-bu-zhan-er-sheng-fqgut61bpihhdit1d2i7tdo2ridp|Hanji 560]] he appears in an earlier interpersonal register as a classics lecturer who de-escalates [[HuChangWesternHan|胡长]]'s jealousy through visible respect.

## Current Profile

[[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-558-ai-zhuang-caihuiying-wangmang-you-duo-hui-yinren-llvrlct7laohoo54s3lhzgwvsfc9|Hanji 558]] presents Zhai Fangjin as part of a senior-office reform voice. His argument is framed through hierarchy and古制: a low-ranking inspector supervising two-thousand-stone commandery officials looks institutionally improper, so the court should replace刺史 with州牧.

Hanji 559 turns from Zhai's institutional voice to his political vulnerability. The source says his strict, by-the-rules style had produced many enemies: he did not look at personal ties when handling cases, had impeached many officials as丞相司直, and continued attacking factional behavior after becoming chancellor. When 荧惑守心 made Chengdi's cosmic responsibility dangerous, that resentment gave other officials a ready account in which disasters, bandits, hunger, and bureaucratic disorder could be placed on Zhai.

Hanji 560 adds a different kind of self-command. Faced with Hu Chang's jealousy and public disparagement, Zhai neither mirrors the insult nor complains. He sends his own disciples to Hu's lectures, has them ask Hu difficult questions, and records Hu's views, making his respect visible enough that Hu becomes ashamed and later praises him. Zhai therefore becomes a case where hard institutional enforcement coexists with soft interpersonal strategy.

Zhai's overall profile is not simple gentleness. In office, strict enforcement gives him enemies and makes him a usable vessel for blame when the ruler and officials both benefit from downward responsibility transfer. In interpersonal conflict, however, he can read an opponent's status anxiety and answer it with recognition rather than retaliation.

## Key Characteristics

- Zhai Fangjin acts as a chancellor-level advocate for office-rank reform.
- His proposal treats hierarchy and ancient precedent as solutions to provincial oversight tension.
- His reform becomes unstable in later Aidi-era politics, where the same office design is judged differently.
- His classics-teaching episode shows a capacity for indirect conflict management through visible respect.
- His strict enforcement and many impeachments create broad resentment among officials.
- His chancellorship makes him the natural target when an omen is interpreted as needing a ministerial sacrifice.
- His death is coerced through court form: condemnation, gifts, and posthumous honors preserve imperial decorum while forcing suicide.

- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-559-mingming-wuzui-weihe-tianxiaren-dou-xiang-ta-si-lu3ckuxwujdbj4zdoqhvo2cg1rs0|Hanji 559]] says Ben Li's omen advice turns the chancellor into the obvious substitute for imperial danger.
- [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-559-mingming-wuzui-weihe-tianxiaren-dou-xiang-ta-si-lu3ckuxwujdbj4zdoqhvo2cg1rs0|Hanji 559]] says Chengdi sends Zhai a condemning document with wine and beef, after which Zhai dies by suicide.

Accumulated resentment:

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 558, Hanji 559, and Hanji 560. It does not reconstruct Zhai Fangjin's full biography, the independent legal truth of every charge, the complete astrology tradition behind 荧惑守心, or the wider textual history of the Zhai-Hu anecdote.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 560's interpersonal layer: Zhai Fangjin becomes not only an institutional reformer and omen-crisis scapegoat, but also a figure of respect-based conflict de-escalation.
