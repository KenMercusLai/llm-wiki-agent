# Sherlock Holmes / 福尔摩斯

## Overview
Sherlock Holmes is Conan Doyle’s fictional detective and, in these reading episodes, an icon of rational explanation, publishing-era reader attachment, late-Qing detective modernity and cross-cultural reuse.

## Current Profile
The Hound example uses gothic fear without accepting supernatural causation; Eco’s William of Baskerville echoes Holmes but tempers inference with humility. Shimada’s two-manuscript pastiche makes Holmes alternately comic and classically rational in competing narrations.

## Key Characteristics
- **Author-character contrast:** The fictional detective’s rational reputation contrasts with Doyle’s reported advocacy of spiritualism; biography does not prove the character’s beliefs.
- **Rational detective model:** The Hound’s gothic atmosphere leads to non-supernatural explanation; Eco’s William echoes Holmes but stresses interpretive humility.
- **Transnational afterlife:** Late-Qing translation cast Holmes as a modern evidence-and-procedure icon, and Shimada’s dual-manuscript pastiche reworks him through Soseki and Watson.

## Evidence
- **Author-character contrast:** The [[MihuanChishu|蜜獾吃书]] literary-gossip discussion juxtaposes [[ArthurConanDoyle|Arthur Conan Doyle]]’s Sherlock Holmes, a public emblem of rational detection, with Doyle’s reported advocacy of spiritualism, fairy photographs and seances. Its biographical anecdotes are secondhand; the fictional detective’s method should not be treated as evidence of the author’s personal beliefs. [[60-xianliao-weida-zuojiamen-de-bagua-di-yi-dan-648693204]]
- **Rational inference across genres:** [[TheHoundOfTheBaskervilles|The Hound of the Baskervilles]] uses gothic fear without leaving its central mystery to supernatural explanation. [[UmbertoEco]]’s [[WilliamOfBaskerville|William of Baskerville]] echoes Holmes in name and observation but in [[TheNameOfTheRose|The Name of the Rose]] must also guard against [[InterpretationAndOverinterpretation|overreading]] fragmentary signs; it is a literary analogue, not the same detective. Late-Qing translations gave Holmes a further [[ChineseDetectiveModernity|evidence-and-procedure]] role against the magistrate-centered [[GongAnFiction|公案]] tradition. [[69-xianliao-tuili-wenxue-lishixuezhe-ke-bu-jiu-shi-zhentan-ma-666244465]] [[53-meigui-de-mingzi-shang-zhen-yu-jia-zheng-yu-xie-quanshi-yu-guodu-quanshi-636490184]]
- **Cross-cultural pastiche and misreading:** [[ShimadaSoji|Shimada Sōji]]’s [[TheCursedMummy]] sets Holmes and [[JohnWatson|Watson]] beside [[NatsumeSoseki|Natsume Sōseki]] in two discordant manuscript accounts: Sōseki sees an absurd, nearly deranged detective while Watson restores the cool classical investigator. The supposed Eastern curse exploits British confusion about Asian artifacts before mundane imposture and staged-fire mechanics explain the case. The episode’s thought experiment about Holmes meeting [[LaoShe|Lao She]] extends the [[HolmesPasticheTradition|pastiche tradition]]; it is not an event in Shimada’s novel. ## Qualifications
Doyle anecdotes in the literary-gossip episode are explicitly secondhand; Holmes’s rationality should not be inferred as Doyle’s personal beliefs. The Lao She collaboration is an episode thought experiment, not an event in the novel. The Eco analogy is literary, not an identity claim.

## What Changed
- The current profile groups the original source-scoped observations by role and mechanism rather than source arrival; no new source or later event is asserted.
