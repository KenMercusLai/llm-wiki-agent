## Overview

Li Ying is a late-Han scholar-official and party-prohibition victim whose profile now begins inside the first Party Prohibition prison case. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] places him among the imprisoned party figures defended by [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and questioned through [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]]'s prison interrogation. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-884-zhichang-chujian-yao-dui-shui-xian-kaidao-lvtc3uqlso242olkyin3fvz5a7ys|Hanji 884]] then shows him restored into the reform center under [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]], [[ChenFanLateHan|陈蕃]], and Dou Wu, while Hanji 892-893 show how the same public prestige becomes lethal exposure in the second party-prohibition disaster.

## Current Profile

The current evidence presents Li Ying as a public moral figure whose prestige moves through prison liability, reform capital, and fatal exposure. Hanji 876 supplies the earliest bounded layer. Li Ying is one of the named party prisoners after Chen Fan's removal has frightened officials away from open rescue. Dou Wu's memorial argues that Li Ying and the other士人 have been accused without firm evidence, and Wang Fu's softened interrogation of [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]] leads to Li Ying and the others having their heavy restraints removed. That scene makes Li Ying a high-profile victim before he becomes a restored reform figure.

Hanji 884 supplies the reform-capital layer: Li Ying, [[DuMiLateHan|杜密]], [[YinXunLateHan|尹勋]], and [[LiuYuAstrologerLateHan|刘瑜]] enter the political core as part of the court-repair hope around Chen Fan and Dou Wu. Hanji 892 then supplies the pre-crackdown reputation layer: even after dismissal, Li Ying remains respected by士人, and the episode places him in the honor-list environment that turns fame into a warning sign. Hanji 893 supplies the arrest layer: Chen Fan and Dou Wu had briefly restored Li Ying and other士人 to office, but their deaths let the eunuch side remove him again.

When [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] frames party networks as a possible rebellion to the young [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], Li Ying refuses flight, enters prison, and dies under torture. His profile therefore combines moral authority, reputation-network danger, and teacher-network liability. Li Ying's students and former subordinates are prohibited from office after his death, and [[JingYiLateHan|景毅]] treats his son's connection to Li Ying as an honor serious enough to self-report rather than accept omission from the punishment list.

## Key Characteristics

- First Party Prohibition prisoner whose name is important enough for Dou Wu to defend in a high-risk memorial.
- Publicly respected late-Han士人 whose reputation places him inside the party-prohibition target field.
- Reform-cohort figure whose entry into the 168 CE court center helps create expectations of political repair.
- Pre-crackdown honor-list figure whose name remains socially powerful even after dismissal.
- Reinstated under Chen Fan and Dou Wu before being removed again after their destruction.
- Death-facing official who refuses to flee and actively enters prison.
- Teacher and patron figure whose students and former subordinates are punished after his case.

## Qualifications

This page does not yet attempt a full Li Ying biography. The current sources preserve the political meaning of his imprisonment, reputation, death, and student network, while details of earlier offices and the complete first Party Prohibition accusation list remain outside the bounded evidence. Hanji 876 shows a pre-release prison softening, not yet the amnesty and office-exclusion outcome supplied by Hanji 877. Transcript variants such as 李英 are normalized to 李膺.

## What Changed

- Hanji 876 adds Li Ying's first Party Prohibition prison layer before his later reform-core restoration.
- Dou Wu's memorial now makes Li Ying a test case for targeted elite intercession before Hanji 884's reform opening.
- The current profile now separates pre-amnesty prison softening from later release, registry, reputation, and death-facing layers.
