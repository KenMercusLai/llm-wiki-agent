## Overview

Li Ying is a late-Han scholar-official and party-prohibition victim whose profile now begins in Hanji 875's first Party Prohibition arrest field. There he is paired with [[DuMiLateHan|杜密]] as a similarly reputed士人 imprisoned during the case that makes [[HuangfuGuiLateHan|皇甫规]] ashamed not to be arrested. Hanji 876 then shows Li Ying among the imprisoned party figures defended by [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]] and questioned through [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]]'s prison interrogation. Hanji 884 restores him into the reform center, while Hanji 892-893 show how the same public prestige becomes lethal exposure in the second party-prohibition disaster.

## Current Profile

The current evidence presents Li Ying as a public moral figure whose prestige moves through prison liability, reform capital, and fatal exposure. Hanji 875 supplies the earliest bounded layer: Li Ying and Du Mi are arrested together because their reputations are comparable, and the surrounding Huangfu Gui case shows that imprisonment of famous worthy men can become a sign of moral belonging.

Hanji 876 supplies the rescue layer. After Chen Fan's removal frightens officials away from open pleading, Dou Wu's memorial argues that Li Ying and the other士人 have been accused without firm evidence. Wang Fu's softened interrogation of [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]] then leads to Li Ying and the others having their heavy restraints removed. That scene makes Li Ying a high-profile victim before he becomes a restored reform figure.

Hanji 884 supplies the reform-capital layer: Li Ying, Du Mi, [[YinXunLateHan|尹勋]], and [[LiuYuAstrologerLateHan|刘瑜]] enter the political core as part of the court-repair hope around Chen Fan and Dou Wu. Hanji 892 then supplies the pre-crackdown reputation layer: even after dismissal, Li Ying remains respected by士人, and the episode places him in the honor-list environment that turns fame into a warning sign.

Hanji 893 supplies the arrest-death layer. [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] frames party networks as a possible rebellion to the young [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]], Li Ying refuses flight, enters prison, and dies under torture. His students and former subordinates are prohibited from office after his death, and [[JingYiLateHan|景毅]] treats his son's connection to Li Ying as an honor serious enough to self-report rather than accept omission from the punishment list.

## Key Characteristics

- First Party Prohibition figure arrested in the same reputation field as Du Mi.
- Publicly respected late-Han士人 whose imprisonment helps make punishment a moral-belonging signal.
- Prisoner important enough for Dou Wu to defend in a high-risk memorial.
- Reform-cohort figure whose entry into the 168 CE court center helps create expectations of political repair.
- Pre-crackdown honor-list figure whose name remains socially powerful even after dismissal.
- Death-facing official who refuses to flee and actively enters prison.
- Teacher and patron figure whose students and former subordinates are punished after his case.

## Qualifications

This page does not yet attempt a full Li Ying biography. The current sources preserve the political meaning of his imprisonment, reputation, death, and student network, while details of earlier offices and the complete first Party Prohibition accusation list remain outside the bounded evidence. Hanji 876 shows a pre-release prison softening, not yet the amnesty and office-exclusion outcome supplied by Hanji 877. Transcript variants such as 李英 are normalized to 李膺.

## What Changed

- Hanji 875 moves Li Ying's bounded profile one step earlier into the paired arrest with Du Mi.
- The current profile now connects Li Ying's first imprisonment to punishment-as-moral-credential logic before Hanji 876's rescue mechanism.
- The page keeps pre-amnesty prison softening separate from later release, registry, reputation, and death-facing layers.
