The source does not give a full biography; it uses one story to show how humor can puncture flattering supernatural claims near imperial power.

## Current Profile

The episode's Dongfang Shuo is a figure of comic intelligence rather than a developed official biography. When a fangshi flatters [[HanWudi|汉武帝]] by saying a long philtrum predicts long life, Dongfang Shuo laughs and redirects the possible offense away from the emperor toward Peng Zu. He then follows the premise mechanically: if one inch means one hundred years, Peng Zu's eight hundred years would require an absurdly long face.

The current synthesis is that Dongfang Shuo's wit functions as indirect correction. He does not openly accuse the fangshi of deception or the emperor of gullibility; he makes the flattering logic ridiculous from inside. That lets the episode connect him to [[HumorousRemonstrance|滑稽讽谏]] and to later traditions that remember him as a comic or xiangsheng-adjacent ancestor.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han court figure remembered here through wit, eccentricity, and comic proximity to power.
- Uses absurd extrapolation to expose a fangshi's flattering physiognomy claim.
- Protects himself by turning apparent laughter at the emperor into laughter at the logical consequence of the claim.
- Represents humor as a possible remonstrance channel when direct correction would be socially or politically risky.
