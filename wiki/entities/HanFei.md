# Han Fei / 韩非

## Overview
韩非 is presented as a royal-born writer from [[HanState|韩国]] whose Legalist political analysis attracted [[QinShiHuang|嬴政]] but whose attempt to preserve Han brought him into fatal conflict with Qin ministers. Later sources quote his ruler-technique, secrecy, and human-motive arguments, or use him as a contested comparator; these are receptions, not additional actions in his lifetime.

## Current Profile
The Qin-court accounts distinguish literary influence from security: Ying Zheng admired [[Gufen|《孤愤》]] and [[Wudu|《五蠹》]], but [[LiSi|李斯]] and [[YaoJia|姚贾]] could frame Han Fei's Han allegiance as a threat. The history of his death and the judgment of his intentions differ across later interpreters.

## Key Characteristics
- **Writer blocked at home, admired abroad.** Trained in [[Xunzi|荀子]]'s intellectual circle alongside Li Si, Han Fei is portrayed as speech-impaired, incisive in writing, and repeatedly unable to persuade [[HanAn|韩王安]] to reform.
- **Protective Qin-court intervention.** In [[CunHan|《存韩》]] he argues against Li Si's Han-first conquest proposal and redirects Qin toward Zhao; his attack on Yao Jia's origin and diplomatic gifts seeks to weaken the anti-[[HezongAlliance|合纵]] operator.
- **Admiration without protection.** Yao Jia turns biographical attack into a defense of useful talent; Li Si casts Han Fei's Han royal origin as disloyalty, leading to prison, privately delivered poison, and Ying Zheng's too-late pardon in the source account.
- **Political technique and disclosure risk.** Later episodes cite him on using ministers and judging their words, seeing interest beneath moral language, and the risk that a confidential plan fails when spoken prematurely.
- **Uneven textual and comparative afterlife.** A Hanfeizi story of [[MangMao|芒卯]] bargaining over his reward and a harsher [[WuQi|吴起]] death variant sit beside law-and-morality debates and the limited comparison with [[NiccoloMachiavelli|Machiavelli]].

## Evidence
- **Writing and formation:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-110-2-qinshihuang-de-cp-weihe-bei-quzhu-chuguo-lj0222vw8ijbvj25iwfzh-qhvzr9]] contrasts the two Xunzi students' backgrounds and talents. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-2-qinshihuang-yeshi-tade-shumi-llkoy-zvhjjr6af9tflcw1xvogwz]] identifies Han Fei's ignored counsel, speech difficulty, writings including 《内外储说》《说林》《说难》, [[ZhengRenMaiLv|郑人买履]]'s warning against rigid measures, and Ying Zheng's admiration.
- **Intervention:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-1-yaojia-shi-ruhe-ping-yizhangzui-po-siguo-hezong-lthw0fvvinmf6ghxpqut60agsueg]] recounts 《存韩》 and Li Si's concern that Han might threaten Qin's rear if Qin attacks [[ZhaoState|赵国]]; the excerpt ends before completing the accusation. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] details Han Fei's charges against Yao Jia and Yao Jia's precedents including [[GuanZhong|管仲]] and [[BailiXi|百里奚]].
- **Death and reception:** [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-112-3-hanfei-yu-yaojia-lunzhan-qinwang-dang-caipan-ljxxlok4uh-ajjirmolybodlspv9]] reports the imprisonment, poison and pardon sequence, then contrasts [[SimaQian|司马迁]]'s lament, [[YangXiong|扬雄]]'s moral critique and [[SimaGuang|司马光]]'s harsher portrayal with the host's reading of a Han royal trying to save Han.
- **Technique:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-2-lvaqlgyccrugfo0l-wuwf1mt1kcm]] invokes him to assess [[ZhaoJinghou|赵靖侯]]'s ability to employ people and judge ministers despite private indulgence. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-912-mimi-daodi-gai-bugai-shuo-chukou-lhsgipxwc17pahc-r6jl7xdyrs2a]] quotes “事已密成，语已泄败” before [[CaiYong|蔡邕]]'s confidential memorial is leaked under [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-988-shoujin-lingru-ta-shi-hanmo-beican-de-huangdi-lsdkpakvk5u-omegcri-wstup-zl]] invokes interest-based explanation of late-Han actors but also quotes “仁者为其中心欣然爱人也,” resisting an all-evil reading.
- **Later textual uses:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-08-yidai-mingjiang-wuqi-cansi-3-ltca-ikuzo9ej7a0lfrgwv2-wulb]] lists Han Fei among variants saying Wu Qi was torn apart, versus the [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] account of his body being shot. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-67-zhanguo-mieba-baiqi-de-junshi-caineng-jiujing-ruhe-ljw0lltowxd4qz75v-wcnlkwqskv]] cites a Hanfeizi tale of Mang Mao's strategic speech and reward negotiation. [[100-fa-lv-de-bei-lun-xie-tian-xie-di-xing-hao-wo-men-hai-you-luo-xiang-733477302]] uses Han Fei and [[ShangYang|商鞅]] as law-as-command contrasts in a discussion of criminal law as a limit on power; [[72-junzhulun-du-ta-shi-yichang-weixian-de-maoxian-673440837]] cautions that [[ThePrince|《君主论》]] cannot simply be collapsed into Western Legalism.

## Qualifications
The Qinji 112-1 text cuts off before Li Si's complete charge, and 112-2 is a podcast summary rather than a full transcript; the later 112-3 account supplies the prison outcome. The host's claim that Sima Guang distorted Han Fei because of Song anti-Legalist politics is interpretation, not demonstrated authorial intent; [[Shiji|《史记》]] and [[ZizhiTongjian|《资治通鉴》]] frame his loyalty differently. The alleged envy of Li Si and revenge of Yao Jia are attributed motives. A later account of Wu Qi's dismemberment is a variant, not an event Han Fei witnessed. Legalist law-as-command and the Machiavelli analogy are modern discussion frames, not comprehensive definitions of Han Fei's thought. Interest-centered explanation does not erase the source's benevolence qualification.

## What Changed
- Han Fei's writings, Qin-court action, and later reception now have distinct evidentiary roles instead of accumulating as disconnected mentions.
- Qin's admiration of the author is explicitly distinguished from the politically unsafe presence of a Han royal envoy.
- Conflicting moral and historiographical judgments remain attributed rather than resolved by assertion.
