# 汉中 / Hanzhong

## Overview
汉中 is a strategic region linking the Qin/Guanzhong sphere with the Shu-Ba interior. Across these podcast readings it is lost in a Warring States war, assigned as a constrained Han kingship, developed into a base for an eastward campaign and famine refuge, and later used to cut a route to the Han court. Those episodes are separated by eras rather than one continuous political history.

## Current Profile
Control of Hanzhong matters through both land and access: possession after battle, a difficult but usable base at [[Nanzheng|南郑]], routes toward [[GuanzhongRegion|关中]], the capacity to receive refugees, and the [[XieguRoadLateHan|斜谷道]] passage to the imperial center. Geography permits these roles but does not predetermine success.

## Key Characteristics
- **Concrete Warring States territorial loss.** After the 312 BCE [[DanyangBattle|丹阳之战]], Qin takes Hanzhong from Chu; the podcast contrasts recovery of real lost territory with [[ChuHuaiwang|楚怀王]]'s fixation on [[Shangyu|商於]]'s promised six hundred li.
- **Constrained Chu-Han kingship.** [[XiangYu|项羽]] grants [[LiuBang|刘邦]] Hanzhong and [[BaShu|巴蜀]] with Nanzheng as capital, while the Three Qin hold the Guanzhong approach.
- **Morale and political-base test.** Homesick deserters and [[HanXin|韩信]]'s flight show that a remote grant cannot retain people without credible command, recognition, and an offensive future.
- **Strategic launch position.** Han Xin's [[HanzhongDui|汉中对]] identifies the Three Qin's political weakness; army reform and layered feints then allow a central strike through [[ChenCang|陈仓]].
- **Refuge and corridor control.** The region receives people fleeing Guanzhong famine in the Chu-Han account; centuries later Liu Yan's agents take Hanzhong and block Xiegu to justify severed communication with the court.

## Evidence
- **Loss and recovery priority:** [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-51-chuhuaiwang-qin-xiao-er-kan-jiujiu-ruhe-mie-le-ni-ltfkdlivomnvux5xodh2rlfqf6ac]] says Qin defeated Chu, captured [[QuGai|屈匄]], claimed 80,000 Chu dead and took Hanzhong; the host judges Chu's renewed march toward Shangyu an anger-driven misprioritization. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-53-qinhuiwang-yong-zhangyi-huan-dipi-lvmo2cwbafcg2gnxliuo18fewimk]] later cites the roughly six hundred li Hanzhong gain while assessing [[ZhangYiStrategist|张仪]] as a valued yet exchangeable Qin minister.
- **Kingship:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-143-xiangyu-shi-ruhe-feijin-xinji-nongsi-chuhuaiwang-mixin-lstfzfvbd2h9i39f7y5dxrbxxwv2]] explains the nominal Han kingship against the [[HuaiwangAgreement|怀王之约]] and [[ThreeQinContainment|三秦压制]]; calling it part of a broader Guanzhong frame did not give Liu Bang direct Guanzhong rule.
- **Retaining talent:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-146-qiannian-jingdian-xiaohe-yuexia-zhui-hanxin-lq0oc3vqr-tsr0r-iwztifxeezl]] places the desertions, [[XiaoHe|萧何]]'s pursuit, and the formal public appointment at Nanzheng.
- **Opening east:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-147-pimei-zhugeliang-longzhongdui-de-hanzhongdui-lqucctzhipgtcvtjlixvj5qbdeai]] has Han Xin contrast Liu Bang's earlier restraint with the unpopular Three Qin commanders; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-148-1-hanxin-yupan-le-zhanghan-de-yupan-lodhpuxpossjno87gorjkfrtitbe]] adds army reform, Ziwu/Qishan diversions and Chen Cang breakthrough against [[ZhangHanQin|章邯]]. The familiar “明修栈道，暗渡陈仓” phrase is later literary reception, not a verbatim standard-history report in that source.
- **Refuge and route:** [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-155-1-lishi-jizai-shenme-qingkuang-hui-fasheng-ren-chi-ren-canju-lrwz-prnxd8hzchagyrbywkux7lh]] says Liu Bang permits famine-stricken Guanzhong residents to migrate to Shu and Hanzhong; [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-959-ruguo-ni-chuanyue-dao-sanguo-gai-ruhe-cunhuo-lroaihmpnrelmfcugym9zx2h2dhq]] has [[LiuYanLateHan|刘焉]] send [[ZhangLuFivePecks|张鲁]] and [[ZhangXiuFivePecks|张修]] to seize Hanzhong, kill Su Gu and block Xiegu.

## Qualifications
The Warring States six-hundred-li Hanzhong gain and eight-myriad casualty figure are source-reported, not independently surveyed here; the earlier six-hundred-li Shangyu promise is a different, unfulfilled claim. The hostage-like quality of Liu Bang's grant is an interpretation of Xiang Yu's settlement, not proof Hanzhong could not support a campaign. Han Xin's motives for leaving are reconstructed by the host. The comparison with [[ZhugeLiang|诸葛亮]] is explicitly conditional: a later march through similar geography faces different armies, opponents, timing, and institutions. The famine and later Liu Yan road closure occur in distinct historical contexts.

## What Changed
- Hanzhong is presented as both an imposed constraint and a base that organization and legitimacy could turn toward Guanzhong.
- Civilian refuge and late-Han route denial qualify an exclusively battlefield reading of the region.
