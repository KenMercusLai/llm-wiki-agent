# Planet Money Book

## Overview
The [[PlanetMoneyBook|Planet Money book]] is documented from proposal and publisher auction through printing, bookstore placement, bestseller ranking and reported chapters.

## Current Profile
The book itself becomes a case of publishing economics: an established audience helps acquisition, while design, supply-chain risks, retail gatekeeping and launch concentration determine what readers encounter.

## Key Characteristics
- The existing audience helped win a seven-figure acquisition.
- A physical book is a costed, regulated supply-chain object.
- Retail discovery and bestseller labels are mediated, not raw popularity.
- The project extends from chapters to podcast adaptations and live interviews.

## Evidence
- **Acquisition and audience:** [[PlanetMoney]]'s listeners and previous participatory products supplied [[AuthorPlatformRiskReduction|audience evidence]] for a proposal developed over roughly a year. [[AlexGoldmark]], agents [[LauraNolan]] and [[JaneVonMehren|Jane von Mehren]], and writer [[AlexMaiassi]] moved it through 23 publisher meetings and a two-round auction with 16 first-round bids; agents described a 15% commission. [[TomMayer]] at [[WWNorton|Norton]] weighed comparable sales and portfolio risk. Norton won on color and educational distribution rather than the highest upfront bid; the disclosed advance was between $1 million and $2 million. [[StephenPace]] planned a major 2026 print/distribution push across stores, institutions and translation channels. [[inside-a-book-auction]] [[bookstore-economics]]
- **Production tradeoffs:** A $30 retail-price target, 16-page [[PrintingSignatureConstraint|signatures]], color illustrations and rejected scratch-and-sniff idea constrained [[PhysicalBookDesignTradeoff|design]]. [[BookPrinterSelectionRisk|Printer choice]] weighed China, Malaysia, Turkey and U.S. production against tariffs, shipping, [[EuropeanDeforestationRegulationSupplyChain|EU compliance]] and fast reprints; final production went to [[LakesideBookCompany]]. Price targets are not a profit measure. [[our-book-vs-the-global-supply-chain]]
- **Retail and launch:** At [[CarmichaelsBookstore|Carmichael's]], shelf placement and [[BookReturnability|return risk]] mattered; its order rose from four copies per store to twenty (forty across two locations), while Norton modeled [[BookPrintRunStrategy|print runs]]. Launch-day discovery still varied at [[McNallyJackson]]. [[PreOrderLaunchConcentration|Preorders]], live bundles and publicity helped, while a poster incentive produced early negative reviews ([[ProductLaunchUnderConstraint]]). The book reached number three on the [[NewYorkTimesBestsellerList|New York Times bestseller lists]] for hardcover nonfiction and combined print/e-book nonfiction; any [[BestsellerStatusFeedbackLoop|further exposure]] is possible, not guaranteed. The show's own book makes [[BookPublishingEconomics]] observable from proposal to shelf. [[bookstore-economics]] [[how-to-make-a-book-into-a-bestseller]]
- **Chapter and live events:** A chapter on [[SquamishNation|Squamish Nation]]'s [[SinakVillageSite|housing development]] became an episode about [[SovereignHousingDevelopment|sovereign development]], [[LocalVetoHousingPolitics|local vetoes]] and [[PermittingDelayCost|delay costs]]. Book-tour events staged interviews with [[JackClark]], [[DarylFairweather]], [[RajChetty]] and Emily Oster, extending the book's economics explanations into live audiences. [[the-skyscrapers-that-nimbys-and-zoning-couldnt-stop]] [[live-anthropic-co-founder-on-ai-and-jobs]]

## Qualifications
The $30 figure is a retail-price target, not profit; number-three bestseller placement is source-reported launch-week ranking, not durable profitability. The Squamish case belongs to a book chapter; the live guest’s AI forecasts are not claims by the book.

## What Changed
- The book is treated as one commercial experiment across acquisition, physical manufacture, retail launch and reporting.
