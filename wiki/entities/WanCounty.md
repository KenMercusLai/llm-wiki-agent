## Overview
宛县 / Wan County, also called Wancheng in the later episodes, recurs as a military and political crossroads in the Nanyang area. The Qin-collapse, Chu–Han and late-Han episodes describe different campaigns centuries apart, not one continuous garrison or administrative policy.

## Current Profile
For Liu Bang, leaving Wan behind threatened his westward line, while accepting surrender made the city a model for less costly pacification. Later it appears in a diversion drawing Xiang Yu south. In the late Han it is Zhang Xiu's station and the site of Cao Cao's failed management of a new surrender.

## Key Characteristics
- A strong rear position whose control mattered to the campaign toward Guanzhong.
- A negotiated surrender case in which rewarding the defeated governor could save time, lives and secure the army's rear.
- A Chu–Han diversionary destination, not the same event as the earlier Qin surrender.
- A late-Han base where inherited troops, alliance with Liu Biao and Cao Cao's treatment of Zhang Xiu led to renewed fighting.

## Evidence
- **Qin-collapse rear security and surrender:** After the defeated [[NanyangCommandery]] governor withdrew to Wan, [[LiuBang]] tried to continue toward [[GuanzhongRegion]] under the [[HuaiwangAgreement]]. [[ZhangLiang]] warned that bypassing the city left an enemy at the army's rear; Liu Bang returned at night and surrounded it by morning. [[ChenHuiNanyang]] then proposed accepting the governor's surrender and rewarding him rather than storming or bypassing Wan. The episode says subsequent towns cooperated more readily, a [[PoliticalSurrenderCascade]]/[[OccupationPacificationStrategy]] interpretation qualified by Liu Bang's earlier [[YingchuanCommandery]] massacre, not proof he never used terror. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-136-meinv-xijiao-liubang-ye-hao-zhe-kouer-lplgzwzahepwx9p7krfc4x0cnz4o]]
- **Separate Chu–Han diversion:** After his [[XingyangStandoff]] escape, [[YuanShengChuHan]] urged Liu Bang to move through [[Wuguan]] toward Wan, Ye and Chou, recruit and hold without battle so [[XiangYu]] would turn from [[Chenggao]] and Xingyang; [[PengYue]] meanwhile pressured Chu logistics near the [[SuiRiver]]. Wan here is a diversionary route, not a repeat of the Qin governor's surrender. The old [[YingyangWarringStates]] key labels that Chu–Han setting awkwardly rather than placing it in the Warring States period. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-162-1-ta-ti-liubang-qu-si-rujin-chengle-zhengzhou-de-shouhushen-li3su2zlmbm1ift9nibujgtcrxwm]]
- **Late-Han garrison and failed submission:** After [[ZhangJiLateHan]] died at [[Rangcheng]], [[ZhangXiu]] inherited his force, stationed it at Wan and attached to [[LiuBiao]] on [[JiaXu]]'s advice; “渊城” in the Hanji 1010 transcript is treated as the same location. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1010-sanguo-liubiao-jiujing-shibushi-piaoliang-de-caobao-lo58hzfo-qv0soauogtgfrkourrd]] In 197 CE, Zhang Xiu surrendered to [[CaoCao]]; Cao Cao's taking of Zhang Ji's widow [[LadyZouWancheng]] offended her nephew's household standing, but Hanji 1012 stops before the attack. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-1012-haose-caocao-luanshi-zhong-de-dute-qingshi-llvrucihuv2buvp1-lisz4usbprh]] Hanji 1013 adds Cao Cao's gifts to [[HuCheEr]], Zhang Xiu's resentment, a leaked plan to kill him, Jia Xu's armored camp-relocation ruse, Hu Che'er disarming [[DianWei]], and the night attack in which Dian Wei, [[CaoAng]] and [[CaoAnmin]] died as Cao Cao escaped. That sequence is more specific than a single [[DesireDrivenCommandExposure|sexual-motive]] explanation; “周氏” in the later transcript is a name variant for Lady Zou, not another person. ## Qualifications
“Wan County” and “Wancheng” are contextual labels for this place in the registered episodes; the late-Han transcript's “渊城” is an apparent transcription variant. Different centuries and regimes must not be fused. Hanji 1013 calls Lady Zou “周氏,” while Hanji 1012 names “邹氏”; this is a source variant, not another woman. The surrender-cascade and desire-driven command-exposure mechanisms are podcast interpretations, not independently verified causal estimates. The old page's prose identified [[YingyangWarringStates]] as the escape setting; this is an inherited canonical key whose label is chronologically awkward in a Chu–Han context, not a claim that the Warring States period persisted.

## What Changed
- Organized the city's appearances by separate strategic functions and eras rather than ingest order.
- Kept the Qin pacification claim qualified by the earlier Yingchuan violence and the Wancheng crisis qualified by multiple causes.

## Relationships
- [[NanyangCommandery]] - regional administrative setting.
- [[LiuBang]] - commander whose westward army surrounded and negotiated Wan.
- [[ZhangLiang]] - adviser who identified the danger of bypassing Wan.
- [[ChenHuiNanyang]] - proposer of the negotiated surrender.
- [[HuaiwangAgreement]] - political incentive behind Liu Bang's westward pace.
- [[YuanShengChuHan]] - adviser whose later diversion routed Liu Bang toward Wan.
- [[ZhangXiu]] - later troop commander based at Wan.
- [[CaoCao]] - commander whose acceptance of surrender unraveled at Wancheng.
- [[DesireDrivenCommandExposure]] - attributed interpretation of Cao Cao's command failure, not its sole cause.
