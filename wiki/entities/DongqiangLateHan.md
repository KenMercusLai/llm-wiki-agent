# 东羌 / Eastern Qiang (late Han)

## Overview

Dongqiang / Eastern Qiang are the frontier group whose late-Han conflict now spans Hanji 881's early Fengyishan campaign and reward horizon, Hanji 883's policy dispute, Hanji 891's tactical campaign narrative, and Hanji 892's critique of border governance.

## Current Profile

The current evidence is source-scoped to Hanji 881, Hanji 883, Hanji 891, and Hanji 892. Hanji 881 adds the immediate campaign prelude: [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] defeats [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] forces at [[FengyishanBattleLateHan|冯翊山之战]], and [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] defers final reward until the Eastern Qiang are fully pacified while still giving Duan military funding and the `破羌将军` title. The page treats that as a campaign-context connection, not as a reason to merge all Qiang labels.

Hanji 883 then shows remaining Qiang forces as a policy problem after military defeat: [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] argues they are hard to eliminate and should be induced to surrender, while Duan treats them as a recurrent frontier threat that must be crushed before they recover. Hanji 891 shows a later surrender mission that Duan rejects on food, timing, and reliability grounds; the episode then follows a Han surprise attack, Qiang retreat, valley defense, and reported mass killing. Hanji 892 gives the post-campaign settlement and ethical interpretation: some surrendered people are resettled, while [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host frame Qiang rebellion as socially caused by harsh commandery exploitation rather than inherent hostility to Han rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier population treated in the sources as participants in the Eastern Qiang rebellion.
- Campaign frame that connects the earlier Fengyishan victory to a promised final reward after full pacification.
- Policy-dispute object between surrender-first conciliation and hard-line elimination.
- Military target of Duan Jiong's pursuit, surprise attack, valley encirclement, and later court reward.
- Surrender-policy test case where hunger and provisioning needs make submission appear potentially unstable.
- Subject of Sima Guang's anti-extermination critique: non-Han peoples still value life and safety.
- Example of rebellion rooted in local official extraction and harsh governance.

## Evidence

- Early campaign and reward horizon: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-881-shi-shui-zai-fa-zhanzheng-hengcai-lipglslijnbp0cuqfpps88q5wit6|Hanji 881]] places Duan Jiong's Fengyishan victory over Xianling Qiang forces inside an Eastern Qiang pacification horizon by having Dou Miao delay final reward until all Eastern Qiang are pacified.
- Policy split before the later campaign: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] says Zhang Huan urges surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while Duan Jiong argues they are already weakened enough to suppress.
- Surrender-risk setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] says Feng Chan is sent to persuade remaining Qiang forces to surrender, while Duan Jiong argues that hunger and local provisioning costs make the surrender path unreliable.
- Tactical defeat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] describes a surprise cavalry attack, Qiang retreat to Shehu Valley, east-west Han pressure, and reported killing of more than 19,000 Qiang.
- Military pacification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says the Eastern Qiang rebellion is considered pacified after Duan Jiong's campaign and Feng Chan's receipt of surrendered Qiang.
- Resettlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says four thousand surrendered Qiang are settled across Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi.
- Ethical and governance critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's comment to reject treating Qiang and other frontier peoples as killable animals and attributes rebellion to commandery-level exploitation.

## Qualifications

This page is not an ethnic history of Qiang peoples. It records only the Eastern Qiang role in four short Hanji episodes, all mediated through the host's summary of the Zizhi Tongjian tradition. Hanji 881 explicitly connects the Fengyishan episode to the Eastern Qiang pacification horizon, but the named [[XianlingQiang|先零羌]] opponent still keeps its own page.

## What Changed

- Added Hanji 881's Fengyishan battle and delayed-final-reward horizon before the Hanji 883 policy dispute.
- Clarified that Hanji 881 connects Xianling Qiang to the Eastern Qiang campaign field without merging the labels.
- Preserved Hanji 892's ethical and root-cause qualification around later military success.
