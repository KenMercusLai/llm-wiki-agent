# 东羌 / Eastern Qiang (late Han)

## Overview

Dongqiang / Eastern Qiang are the frontier group whose late-Han conflict anchors Hanji 883's policy dispute, Hanji 891's tactical campaign narrative, and Hanji 892's critique of border governance.

## Current Profile

The current evidence is source-scoped to Hanji 883, Hanji 891, and Hanji 892. Hanji 883 shows remaining Qiang forces as a policy problem after military defeat: [[ZhangHuanLateHan|张奂]] argues they are hard to eliminate and should be induced to surrender, while [[DuanJiongLateHan|段熲]] treats them as a recurrent frontier threat that must be crushed before they recover. Hanji 891 shows a later surrender mission that Duan rejects on food, timing, and reliability grounds; the episode then follows a Han surprise attack, Qiang retreat, valley defense, and reported mass killing. Hanji 892 gives the post-campaign settlement and ethical interpretation: some surrendered people are resettled, while [[SimaGuang|司马光]] and the host frame Qiang rebellion as socially caused by harsh commandery exploitation rather than inherent hostility to Han rule.

## Key Characteristics

- Late-Han frontier population treated in the sources as participants in the Eastern Qiang rebellion.
- Policy-dispute object between surrender-first conciliation and hard-line elimination.
- Military target of Duan Jiong's pursuit, surprise attack, valley encirclement, and later court reward.
- Surrender-policy test case where hunger and provisioning needs make submission appear potentially unstable.
- Subject of Sima Guang's anti-extermination critique: non-Han peoples still value life and safety.
- Example of rebellion rooted in local official extraction and harsh governance.

## Evidence

- Policy split before the later campaign: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-883-laoban-yao-fansi-ruhe-bimian-gaoguan-neihong-lph-fycnqas6hztjq1jbsisvylid|Hanji 883]] says Zhang Huan urges surrender and peace because residual Qiang groups are hard to eliminate, while Duan Jiong argues they are already weakened enough to suppress.
- Surrender-risk setup: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] says Feng Chan is sent to persuade remaining Qiang forces to surrender, while Duan Jiong argues that hunger and local provisioning costs make the surrender path unreliable.
- Tactical defeat: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-891-gao-duanwei-de-muqin-shi-jia-de-dinghai-shenzhen-lmqkzsnhtiwc9wzcee869kuu57m5|Hanji 891]] describes a surprise cavalry attack, Qiang retreat to Shehu Valley, east-west Han pressure, and reported killing of more than 19,000 Qiang.
- Military pacification: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says the Eastern Qiang rebellion is considered pacified after Duan Jiong's campaign and Feng Chan's receipt of surrendered Qiang.
- Resettlement: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] says four thousand surrendered Qiang are settled across Anding, Hanyang, and Longxi.
- Ethical and governance critique: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-892-xiu-zhiyu-guren-youshenme-jiejing-lnobbkp6mj4xyb9jeuqsiuqogct|Hanji 892]] uses Sima Guang's comment to reject treating Qiang and other frontier peoples as killable animals and attributes rebellion to commandery-level exploitation.

## Qualifications

This page is not an ethnic history of Qiang peoples. It records only the Eastern Qiang role in three short Hanji episodes, all mediated through the host's summary of the Zizhi Tongjian tradition. Later Western Qiang or Xianling Qiang pages should stay separate unless a source explicitly connects those groups.

## What Changed
