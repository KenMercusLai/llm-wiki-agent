## Overview

陈蕃 / Chen Fan (late Han) is an anti-eunuch official whose profile now begins with an Emperor Huan-era remonstrance and removal before moving into interregnum duty enforcement, title-discipline refusal, 168 CE reform opening, pre-coup warning, fatal palace intervention, and later memory. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-876-zhichang-zong-pengbi-shuoming-ni-meiyou-zhaodui-guanxi-lrjob-a07t29hdr0iogrmcsjr7qp|Hanji 876]] shows Chen repeatedly and sharply remonstrating with [[EmperorHuanOfHan|汉桓帝]] during the first Party Prohibition prisoner crisis; Huan then removes him from the Taiwei office on a personnel pretext, frightening officials who might otherwise plead for [[LiYingLateHan|李膺]], [[FanPangLateHan|范滂]], and the other prisoners. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-880-weihe-zhichang-laoyoutiao-rezhong-yu-chengbing-lnzfgwghrxrpltsmb8godap-nyzw|Hanji 880]] later shows him using "事亡如存" to pressure shangshu officials who claimed illness after Huan's death and before [[EmperorLingOfHan|汉灵帝]]'s formal accession.

## Current Profile

Chen Fan's current profile is built around role discipline under danger. Hanji 876 gives the first layer: he confronts Emperor Huan while the party-prisoner case is still live, angers the ruler, and is removed as Taiwei. The removal does not only harm Chen personally; it changes the rescue field by teaching other civil and military officials that open pleading for the prisoners is dangerous. That makes Chen an early pressure point in [[JiaBiaoLateHan|贾彪]]'s later decision to find a better-positioned intercessor in [[DouWuLateHan|窦武]].

Hanji 880 shows the next pattern in a different crisis. While the court is between a dead ruler and a not-yet-enthroned successor, many shangshu officials hide behind illness claims. Chen rebukes them by invoking "事亡如存," treating service to the deceased ruler's state as still binding. In this branch, Chen's force is moral and administrative rather than military: he restores attendance by making avoidance look dishonorable.

Hanji 882 adds the title-discipline branch. [[EmpressDowagerDouMiao|窦妙 / 窦皇太后]] wants to reward Chen because he helped make her empress under Emperor Huan, but Chen repeatedly refuses the marquisate because title, land, and food revenue should follow public merit or legitimate royal favor rather than private gratitude.

Hanji 884 and Hanji 885 then move Chen into the live anti-eunuch crisis. He is one of the senior figures entrusted with government under Dou Miao, reads the palace field as deteriorating because [[CaoJieLateHan|曹节]] and [[WangFuLateHan|王甫]] remain close to the dowager, and urges Dou Wu to remove them before the window closes. His later memorial names eunuch danger openly, and after [[ZhengSaLateHan|郑飒 / 郑萨]] is arrested he argues for immediate killing rather than slow interrogation.

Hanji 887 shows the cost of that profile. Chen enters the palace with officials and students to defend Dou Wu, but Wang Fu controls the accusation language and armed response. Chen's reading is sharper than Dou Wu's, yet it does not become command capacity once palace access and violence shift to the eunuch side. After his death, Hanji 901, Hanji 921, Hanji 941, and Hanji 948 reuse his name in disputes over Dou Miao's burial status, Cao Jie's remembered injuries, [[ChenYiLateHan|陈逸]]'s later plot connection, and Dong Zhuo's rehabilitation of Party Prohibition victims.

## Key Characteristics

- Chen Fan is a remonstrating high official whose removal during the first Party Prohibition crisis chills open rescue.
- He treats public office as binding through crisis, not suspended by the death of the ruler or by succession uncertainty.
- His refusal of Dou Miao's marquisate makes title legitimacy part of his profile before the anti-eunuch crisis.
- He is more decisive than Dou Wu from the first 168 CE decision window, but without enough command control to overcome the eunuch side.
- His initial strategy identifies Cao Jie and Wang Fu as urgent removal targets before Dou Miao's selective purge of Guan Ba and Su Kang.
- His palace intervention converts moral clarity into direct bodily exposure once Wang Fu controls the accusation and seizure.
- His death becomes a later anti-eunuch grievance used by repair efforts, risky plots, and coercive legitimacy packaging.

## Qualifications

- Hanji 876 shows Chen Fan's removal as the event that chills open rescue, but it does not make him the only rescuer or the only reason later intercession succeeds.
- Hanji 880's shangshu illness episode is treated as a specific risk-avoidance case, not as a claim that all illness leave by officials is false.
- Hanji 887's evaluation of Chen Fan as seeing the crisis more clearly than Dou Wu is the host's interpretation, not a complete proof that his preferred violent timing would have succeeded.
- The page treats Chen Fan as a morally charged memory node, but later uses of that memory vary: Li Xian and Chen Qiu use it for ritual repair, Wang Fen's circle for risky coup politics, and Dong Zhuo for legitimacy packaging.
- Minor transcript variants such as 陈藩 are normalized to 陈蕃 because existing late-Han routes already carry this actor.

## What Changed

- Hanji 876 adds Chen Fan's Emperor Huan-era removal before the interregnum duty and anti-eunuch crisis branches.
- The profile now treats Chen's removal as a field-level event that changes other officials' willingness to plead for party prisoners.
- Jia Biao's targeted rescue strategy now appears as a response to the fear created by Chen Fan's removal.
- The older "事亡如存" branch remains part of Chen's duty profile, but it is no longer the earliest bounded layer.
