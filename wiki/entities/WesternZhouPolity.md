# 西周国 / Western Zhou Polity

## Overview
西周国 / Western Zhou Polity: The late Western Zhou polity was distinct from both Eastern Zhou polity and the earlier Western Zhou dynasty.

## Current Profile
The late Western Zhou polity was distinct from both Eastern Zhou polity and the earlier Western Zhou dynasty. Ritual objects and the nominal royal title retained symbolic leverage despite narrow territory. The failed 256 BCE anti-Qin campaign ended with surrender and extinction of the Western Zhou polity.

## Key Characteristics
- The late Western Zhou polity was distinct from both Eastern Zhou polity and the earlier Western Zhou dynasty.
- Ritual objects and the nominal royal title retained symbolic leverage despite narrow territory.
- The failed 256 BCE anti-Qin campaign ended with surrender and extinction of the Western Zhou polity.

## Evidence
- **The late Western Zhou polity was distinct from both Eastern Zhou polity and the earlier Western Zhou dynasty:** A 353 BCE notice that [[HanState|韩]] attacked [[EasternZhouPolity|东周国]] refers to one small polity in a divided [[ZhouRoyalHouse|royal domain]], not to the whole Eastern Zhou era; the episode's [[QiState|齐]], [[WeiState|魏]] and [[ZhaoState|赵]] war chronology under [[ZhouXianwang|周显王]] also illustrates how far that house's territorial authority had shrunk in the [[WarringStatesPeriod|Warring States]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-16-sunbin-pangjuan-enyuan-jiuge-guiling-zhi-zhan-lryhzkc1-xsvfjhvicu8yxa9d2jr]] The later division is described as an [[EasternWesternZhouSplit|eastern–western split]], not a revival of the earlier Western Zhou dynasty. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]]
- **Ritual objects and the nominal royal title retained symbolic leverage despite narrow territory:** The source distinguishes the western polity from the former dynasty and recounts how [[ZhouNanwang|周赧王]] sent [[DongzhouWugong|东周武公]] to warn [[ChuState|楚]] that seizing the vessels would carry legitimacy costs despite the tiny territory. This is [[ZhouRoyalSymbolicDeterrence|symbolic deterrence]], not restored military strength. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-76-shui-geile-chuqingxiangwang-de-zixin-lsut4cfic7xbbrmbmimrruf7n6y0]]
- **The failed 256 BCE anti-Qin campaign ended with surrender and extinction of the Western Zhou polity:** In the episode's 256 BCE sequence, [[MiWan|楚考烈王]] and [[Chunshenjun|春申君]] urge Zhou Nan Wang to mobilize against [[QinState|秦]], but promised allies fail to arrive; loans for his small force create the [[ZhaitaiGaozhu|债台高筑]] story and expose a [[NominalAuthorityMobilizationTrap|nominal-authority trap]]. [[QinZhaoxiangwang|秦昭襄王]] then compels surrender of thirty-six cities and thirty thousand people and extinguishes the polity. [[zizhi-tongjian-zhouji-98-lishishang-shouge-laolai-dengchang-le-luxsxepdedsxd-zprezfg3u3bsnu]] A later chronicle refers back to the Zhou collapse as background for Qin succession, without documenting any subsequent movement of the lord or treasures. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-99-4-shui-shi-kongzi-hou-zui-weidade-sixiangjia-lr4phoq9chc4tz0yb8y95gy21qdd]]

## Qualifications
The episode’s 867-year arithmetic is inconsistent with the dates it gives; do not treat it as a verified dynastic duration. The 353 BCE East Zhou notice is a separate small polity. The declared later source note does not substantiate the older page’s eastward-flight, treasure-transfer, or lord-relocation details.

## What Changed
- The polity’s symbolic leverage is distinguished from its inability to sustain the 256 BCE military coalition.
