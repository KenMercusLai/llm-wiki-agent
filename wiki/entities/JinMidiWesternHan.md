## Overview

## Current Profile

The sources present Jin Midi as a rare case of foreign-origin service being fully trusted and honored inside the Western Han court. Hanji 390-2 says Han Wudi trusts him partly because a Xiongnu-origin official without deep Central Plains roots has fewer local interest ties and less reason to rebel. It also turns the "third eye" story into a political-perception metaphor: later people could imagine Jin as Ma Wangye-like because his caution and close service let him detect hidden palace danger. Hanji 391-2 then makes his delicacy visible at the succession origin point: he says that if an outside-origin minister led the regency, Xiongnu would look down on Han, so he yields the central assisting role to Huo Guang. Han Wudi had also ordered that he be enfeoffed as Du Hou, but Jin initially refused because [[EmperorZhaoOfHan|汉昭帝刘弗陵]] was still young and because standing out too much was dangerous for a Xiongnu-born official. When he became seriously ill, Huo Guang requested the title again and Jin accepted, which Hanji 394-1 reads as a choice for his sons' inheritance rather than a sudden appetite for rank.

His profile is therefore defined by restraint, family continuity, and institutionally bounded favor. Han Zhao personally wants both Jin sons to receive special marquis-level treatment, but Huo Guang answers that only one son can inherit and that marquisate should follow prior rules and state merit. Jin's posthumous honors remain extraordinary: the episode says he receives military-style funeral treatment and burial honor near the imperial mausoleum, placing him with a very small group of highly honored Han Wudi-era ministers. The closing link to Jin's mother and her strict discipline makes family education part of the source's explanation for the Jin household's later caution.

## Key Characteristics

- Xiongnu-born Western Han minister whose Han-court service is presented as unusually successful and honored.
- Trusted outsider whose lack of deep Central Plains court entanglement is treated as a political reliability asset.
- Vigilant palace servant whose later "third eye" image is read as mythic language for detecting hidden danger.
- Entrusted sickbed minister who deflects regency leadership to Huo Guang on external-prestige grounds.
- Cautious official who refuses an early marquisate because accepting high rank under a young emperor could make him too conspicuous.
- Father who accepts sickbed enfeoffment so the title can pass to his sons.
- Posthumously honored figure whose funeral treatment marks exceptional court recognition.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 390-2, Hanji 391-2, and Hanji 394-1. It does not reconstruct Jin Midi's full early life, the complete story of his Xiongnu origin, the full Ma Heluo palace incident, the full legal form of the entrusted-minister arrangement, the full legal form of Du Hou inheritance, the exact funeral protocol, the complete record of his sons, or the later Jin lineage under Wang Mang. The sources' claims about motive are interpretive: trust, refusal, and later acceptance are read through political isolation, caution, foreign-origin delicacy, and family continuity rather than proven from private statements. The Ma Wangye link is treated as folklore, not as literal evidence that Jin had a third eye.
