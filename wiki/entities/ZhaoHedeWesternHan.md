## Overview

赵合德 / Zhao Hede enters this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] as [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s favored consort and the central named target in accusations that Chengdi and Hede repeatedly killed imperial sons. [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-571-hanchengdi-qinshou-shazi-shi-zhen-shi-jia-lhjrwpg-t6izp__tshwzem1t0dyzh|Hanji 571]] supplies the earlier case narrative: after Chengdi's sudden death, popular rumor blames Zhao Hede, [[WangZhengjun|王政君]] orders investigation, and Zhao Hede kills herself. The received suspicion is historically important but remains evidentially unstable in the wiki.

## Current Profile

Hanji 571 makes the accusation field concrete. In [[XieGuangWesternHan|谢光]]'s report, Zhao Zhaoyi's driver asks whether [[CaoGongWesternHan|曹宫]]'s newborn boy has died, conveys anger that the child is still alive, later delivers pills that kill Cao Gong, and the six attendants say Zhao Zhaoyi knows they are innocent but will not let them live. The episode identifies the immediate post-death rumor around Zhao Hede and her suicide, but it stops before fully adjudicating the title question.

Hanji 573 then treats that received narrative critically rather than erasing it. It focuses on how Zhao Hede becomes the figure onto whom Chengdi's sudden death, repeated child loss, and late Western Han succession anxiety can be concentrated. The source acknowledges that the received texts make her suspicious, then presses against the case by asking whether the evidence is too testimony-dependent and whether [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s political interests make unchecked heir-killing implausible.

The profile therefore remains qualified. Zhao Hede is neither exonerated nor treated as the sole cause of Chengdi's no-heir outcome. She is a case where palace intimacy, accusation, missing evidence, and later "red-beauty disaster" narration overlap.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han favored consort tied to the most serious allegations around Chengdi's dead or missing heirs.
- Source-scoped suspect in the `Han Shu` and `Zizhi Tongjian` tradition, not a fully adjudicated actor in this wiki.
- Immediate target of post-Chengdi-death rumor in Hanji 571, followed by suicide after Wang Zhengjun orders investigation.
- Accused through a reported palace execution chain involving Cao Gong, six attendants, and a missing infant.
- Convenient blame target after Chengdi's sudden death and Han Aidi's anti-Zhao-family incentives.
- Paired with Zhao Feiyan in later gendered and literary memory.
- Helps expose how palace accusations can combine testimony, political need, and moralized storytelling.

- Death rumor and suicide: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-571-hanchengdi-qinshou-shazi-shi-zhen-shi-jia-lhjrwpg-t6izp__tshwzem1t0dyzh|Hanji 571]] says popular rumor blamed Zhao Hede for Chengdi's sudden death and that she killed herself after Wang Zhengjun ordered investigation.
- Cao Gong case: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-571-hanchengdi-qinshou-shazi-shi-zhen-shi-jia-lhjrwpg-t6izp__tshwzem1t0dyzh|Hanji 571]] reports a Zhao Zhaoyi-linked messenger asking whether Cao Gong's infant had died, delivering pills to Cao Gong, and pressuring the attendants toward death.

- Political implausibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] argues Wang Zhengjun and the Wang family had strong reasons to monitor Chengdi's heir production.
- Scapegoat possibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] says many palace actors disliked the Zhao sisters and would have reason to place blame on them.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 571 and Hanji 573. Hanji 571 supplies a grave accusation narrative, while Hanji 573 warns against treating that narrative as fully settled. The page does not settle Zhao Hede's guilt, the full legal record, Chengdi's exact death cause, or the medical explanation for the deaths of Chengdi's children. Hanji 573's family-health argument remains a hypothesis from historical pattern rather than proof.
