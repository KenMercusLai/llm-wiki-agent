## Overview

赵合德 / Zhao Hede enters this wiki through [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] as [[HanChengdi|汉成帝]]'s favored consort and the central named target in accusations that Chengdi and Hede repeatedly killed imperial sons. The episode treats that received suspicion as historically important but evidentially unstable.

## Current Profile

Hanji 573 does not reconstruct Zhao Hede's full biography. It focuses on how she becomes the figure onto whom Chengdi's sudden death, repeated child loss, and late Western Han succession anxiety can be concentrated. The source acknowledges that the received texts make her suspicious, then presses against the case by asking whether the evidence is too testimony-dependent and whether [[WangZhengjun|王政君]]'s political interests make unchecked heir-killing implausible.

The profile therefore remains qualified. Zhao Hede is neither exonerated nor treated as the sole cause of Chengdi's no-heir outcome. She is a case where palace intimacy, accusation, missing evidence, and later "red-beauty disaster" narration overlap.

## Key Characteristics

- Western Han favored consort tied to the most serious allegations around Chengdi's dead or missing heirs.
- Source-scoped suspect in the `Han Shu` and `Zizhi Tongjian` tradition, not a fully adjudicated actor in this wiki.
- Convenient blame target after Chengdi's sudden death and Han Aidi's anti-Zhao-family incentives.
- Paired with Zhao Feiyan in later gendered and literary memory.
- Helps expose how palace accusations can combine testimony, political need, and moralized storytelling.

## Evidence

- Received suspicion: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] says `Han Shu` and `Zizhi Tongjian` both imply Chengdi and Zhao Hede killed imperial sons.
- Evidentiary weakness: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] says the later investigation relies heavily on testimony and lacks expected physical evidence.
- Political implausibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] argues Wang Zhengjun and the Wang family had strong reasons to monitor Chengdi's heir production.
- Scapegoat possibility: [[zizhi-tongjian-hanji-573-zhaofeiyan-jiemei-shi-hongyanhuoshui-haishi-tizuigaoyang-lkacfndvi8t1opep67xmquyzr_ww|Hanji 573]] says many palace actors disliked the Zhao sisters and would have reason to place blame on them.

## Qualifications

This page is bounded to Hanji 573. It does not settle Zhao Hede's guilt, the full legal record, Chengdi's exact death cause, or the medical explanation for the deaths of Chengdi's children. The source's family-health argument remains a hypothesis from historical pattern rather than proof.
