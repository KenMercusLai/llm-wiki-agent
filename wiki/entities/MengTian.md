# 蒙恬 / Meng Tian

## Overview
蒙恬 / Meng Tian served [[QinState]] and [[QinShiHuang]] as a Chu-campaign commander, northern frontier general and road-project official. His death in the succession crisis is narrated through competing textual traditions and later moral criticism.

## Current Profile
The cited chronology runs from an unsuccessful paired attack on Chu, through northern campaigning and construction, to a refusal to obey a suspect suicide order followed by execution under Hu Hai. Loyalty in the last episode does not erase the cost of the projects he served.

## Key Characteristics
- Shared command with Li Xin in the failed first Chu expedition.
- Northern offensive and defensive works under Qin Shi Huang.
- Straight-road building and Fu Su's frontier assignment amid [[CourtFeedbackCollapse]] linked him to the imperial center.
- The succession purge and posthumous moral judgments remain disputed in sequence and evaluation.

## Evidence
- **Chu expedition:** [[LiXinQin]] estimated 200,000 troops to attack [[ChuState]], against [[WangJianQin]]'s 600,000; the host uses [[AnchoringEffect]] to interpret Ying Zheng's choice of Li Xin and Meng Tian, following Li Xin's success against [[YanTaiziDan]]. The next source says their troops initially took more than twenty cities, but [[XiangYan]] drew Li Xin toward [[NanCommandery]] and defeated him; Meng Tian's relief force was also beaten, with seven Qin generals reportedly killed. The first episode sets up the risk, not the outcome. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-116-shishang-zuican-taizi-bei-fuwang-qinshou-zhansha-lthruqxtd4iqofdxzvim58ph8rlk]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-117-2-xuezhedian-qinchao-wujiang-jingran-ruci-gaoqingshang-lsyw3eitcnzfexl1l6vfh2gbtaoy]]
- **Northern command and engineering:** After [[LuSheng]]'s [[ProphecyTriggeredPolicy|“亡秦者胡” warning]], the court reads “胡” as [[Xiongnu]] and assigns Meng Tian a reported 300,000 troops. The continuation reports Hetao recovered, forty-four counties established, older [[WarringStatesNorthernGreatWalls]] linked and a long posting in [[Shangjun]]; the host reads this partly as [[PostUnificationWarInertia]]. In 212 BCE he is ordered to build [[QinZhidao]] from Yunyang to Jiuyuan, an unfinished and labor-intensive work in [[ImperialConstructionOverload]]. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-1-shen-zhi-yuyan-wangqin-zhe-hu-jing-shi-zhi-lg7m3vfdbcweb4joxe4jvhxjx6y8]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-123-2-gudai-shiqi-de-daochamen-daodi-you-duo-weixian-lgcknwubwm3uzqmzgbgdlxlvcwxy]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-1-qinshihuang-xiujian-shishang-zuida-lanweilou-afanggong-lhqc6jw4jzoyrg7jzc2mbxdebj4]]
- **Fu Su and succession:** After [[FuSu]] remonstrates over the [[QinKengruIncident]], Qin Shi Huang sends him to supervise Meng Tian at Shangjun. [[ZhaoGao]] exploits [[LiSi]]'s [[StatusAnxietyPersuasion|status anxiety]] that a Fu Su succession would elevate the decorated, trusted Meng Tian; the traditional [[ForgedEdictSuccessionCoup|forged order]] demands both men's suicides. Fu Su dies, whereas Meng Tian urges verification, refuses suicide, is imprisoned at Yangzhou and loses his troops to Li Si's confidant. The [[ZhaoZhengShu]] account potentially changes the [[EmergencySuccessionLegitimation|accession sequence]] as a [[TextualVariantPoliticalStakes|textual variant]] without sparing Meng Tian. [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-125-2-lishishang-qinshihuang-weihe-yao-fenshu-kengru-lulaivzp1xqs71nbaltr-ygpjihw]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-2-lisi-weisha-bu-bang-yingming-de-fusu-lq-vny5duwwzfubpeijr21oazxbg]] [[zizhi-tongjian-qinji-127-3-haiwai-huigui-yipi-zhujian-jiekai-huhai-lidi-zhi-mi-lja0pankcx4ugtylbxxqkwuavdnw]]
- **Death and assessment:** [[ZiyingQin]] invokes the fate of [[LiMu]] and the later comparison with [[BaiQi]] and Qi ministers to warn [[QinErshi]] against killing Meng Tian and [[MengYiQin]]. Meng Tian says his family served Qin three generations and refuses rebellion even from prison before taking poison. [[YangXiong]] condemns the road and wall labor, [[SimaGuang]] condemns service to Qin's harm while praising refusal to rebel, and [[SimaQian]] rejects his “截断地脉” self-explanation in favor of responsibility for failing to urge rest. ## Qualifications
The 200,000/600,000, 300,000, seven generals and forty-four counties are reported narrative figures, not audited military statistics. The “亡秦者胡” omen is an episode's immediate narrative trigger, not a sufficient strategic cause. The source renders 蒙恬 as 蒙田 in one battle passage; this is not a second person. The order and authorship of the succession purge vary between the received account and 《赵正书》. ## What Changed
- Joined military, engineering and succession episodes while preserving distinct chronology and source limitations.
- Kept both loyalty and labor-burden criticisms rather than a one-sided vindication.

## Relationships
- [[LiXinQin]] - paired commander in the unsuccessful first Chu expedition.
- [[FuSu]] - prince stationed at Meng Tian's Shangjun command and targeted by the same order.
- [[QinZhidao]] - road project whose labor burden figures in later critique.
- [[LoyalServiceMoralLiability]] - moral tension between obedience and public harm.
- [[ZhaoZhengShu]] - alternate succession narrative that changes the sequence of his elimination.
