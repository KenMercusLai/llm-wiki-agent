# Robert Morris

## Overview
Robert Morris appears as a technically discerning collaborator in the Viaweb/early YC circle and as a candid customer signal for a founder; these are distinct interview accounts, not evidence of a formal role in every later startup.

## Current Profile
His relationships with Graham and Blackwell connected programming judgment to Viaweb and early YC selection. His blunt response to Reinhardt illustrates the difference between polite interest and actual demand.

## Key Characteristics
- **Programming ties:** Blackwell recalls Morris as a Harvard peer who referred him to Graham; Graham recalls Morris as a technically capable Viaweb collaborator.
- **Technical and customer judgment:** Morris helped test early YC applicants' technology and gave Reinhardt a frank negative customer signal about ClassMetric.
- **Founder-network advice:** Graham brought Morris into a conversation about Patrick Collison's MIT application; this is advisory contact, not evidence of a role at Stripe.

## Evidence
- **Programming ties:** Blackwell remembers sharing a Harvard adviser and nearby offices with Morris, who recommended him as the program’s best programmer to [[PaulGraham]]. Graham, in turn, describes Morris as an exceptional programmer: they tested the browser-controlled software that became [[Viaweb]], though Morris later declined Graham’s proposed browser-based development platform, which Graham narrowed into [[ArcLanguage|Arc]]. Graham also recounts the Morris worm as an unintended-consequences episode. [[tsr-s2-trevorblackwell-v2]] [[socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll-socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll]]
- **Technical and customer judgment:** In Graham’s account Morris helped check whether technology proposed by the first [[YCombinator]] applicants could work, a concrete role in [[StartupAcceleratorBatchSelection]]. [[PeterReinhardt]] separately recalls that Morris had sounded encouraging during ClassMetric customer discovery but told Graham at their YC interview that he would not use [[ClassMetric]]; the contrast illustrates why polite interest is weak [[CustomerEvidenceStrategy|customer evidence]], not a measured verdict on the whole market. [[socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll-socialradarsseason1-pg-v4-updated-finallll]] [[peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted-peter-tsr-v3-audio-converted]]
- **Founder-network advice:** Graham invited Morris to dinner to advise teenage [[PatrickCollison]] about applying to MIT. The episode situates that advice in the future [[Stripe]] founders’ network, without making Morris a Stripe founder or operator. [[socialradarss2-stripe-v2]]

## Qualifications
The ClassMetric refusal is Reinhardt’s recollection, not an independently measured market result. The Stripe-linked dinner was advice about MIT applications, not a claim that Morris founded Stripe.
